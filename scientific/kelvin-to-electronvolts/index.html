About Converting Kelvin to Electronvolts

Kelvin and electronvolt are both units of energy. As an energy equivalent, one kelvin corresponds to k_B × 1 K = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ joules. The electronvolt is exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules, the energy gained by an electron crossing one volt.

Formula

eV = K × 0.00008617333262

This factor comes from the defined size of each unit: 1 kelvin equals 0.00008617333262 electronvolts, and the reverse factor is 11604.51812.

Understanding the Kelvin

Kelvin (K) measures energy. As an energy equivalent, one kelvin corresponds to k_B × 1 K = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ joules.

Convention: Energy–temperature equivalence E = k_B·T with the exact Boltzmann constant 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K. It is not a conversion of temperature scales.

Standard and definition

The kelvin is covered by NIST — CODATA Recommended Values of the Fundamental Physical Constants.

Uses of the kelvin today

  • Expressing energy scales as temperatures in physics and astrophysics
  • Comparing thermal and electronic energies (1 eV ≙ 11,604.5 K)

Understanding the Electronvolt

Electronvolt (eV) measures energy. The electronvolt is exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules, the energy gained by an electron crossing one volt.

Standard and definition

The electronvolt is covered by BIPM — The International System of Units (SI Brochure).

Uses of the electronvolt today

  • Atomic, nuclear and particle physics
  • Semiconductor band gaps and photon energies
