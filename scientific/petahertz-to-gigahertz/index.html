Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the petahertz (PHz).

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the gigahertz (GHz).

How do I convert petahertz to gigahertz?

Multiply the petahertz value by 1000000. For example, 5 PHz × 1000000 = 5000000 GHz.

Why do petahertz and gigahertz need converting at all?

Peta (P) represents 10^15, familiar from petabyte-scale data storage. Giga (G) represents 10^9, familiar from gigabyte and gigahertz measurements.

Is the petahertz to gigahertz conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between petahertz and gigahertz is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1000000), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert gigahertz back to petahertz?

Yes. Multiply the gigahertz value by 1.000000e-06 (or divide by 1000000) to get petahertz.

About this conversion

This page converts petahertz (PHz) into gigahertz (GHz) within the scientific converter, using the fixed relationship 1 PHz = 1000000 GHz.

Worked example: to convert 10 PHz to GHz, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1000000 = 10000000 GHz.

Reverse conversion: to go from gigahertz back to petahertz, divide by 1000000 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e-06).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.
