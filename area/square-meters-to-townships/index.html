Formula

1 square meter = 1.072505996×10^-8 townships

twp = m² × 1.072505996×10^-8

m² = twp ÷ 1.072505996×10^-8

Worked example

One square meter (1 m² in absolute terms) is about the size of a standard doormat, while one township (9.324e+07 m²) is about the size of a small town -- so converting square meter to township means moving 1.072506×10^-8x across that scale.

Frequently asked questions

How many townships are in one square meter?

One Square meter equals 1.072506×10^-8 townships.

Why is the township used for this kind of measurement?

Grouping 36 sections into a township gave 18th-century surveyors a manageable large-area unit for mapping vast tracts of frontier land before finer subdivision.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. This conversion uses the exact defined relationship between square meters and townships, so the result shown is mathematically precise (rounded only for display).

How does a square meter compare in size to a township?

By definition, one square meter is 9.323957×10^7 times smaller than one township (Square meter = 1 m², Township = 9.32396e+07 m²).

When is square meters to townships conversion commonly used?

It commonly comes up in the SI base unit for area, used in construction, real estate, and everyday science, as well as US land surveying, large-scale rural land division, and local government/administrative boundaries in many US states.

About this conversion

Square meters and Townships are both units used to measure area. A square meter is the SI derived unit of area, the area of a square with 1-meter sides. A township is a US Public Land Survey System unit equal to 36 sections, or 36 square miles. This converter uses the precise relationship 1 square meter = 1.072505996×10^-8 townships (equivalently 1 township = 93239571.97 square meters), so results stay accurate for everyday construction, real estate, flooring and general area measurement, as well as US land surveying, cadastral mapping and rural land administration.
