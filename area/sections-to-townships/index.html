Frequently asked questions

How many townships are in one section?

One Section equals 0.027778 townships.

Why is the section used for this kind of measurement?

The section gives US land surveyors and farmers a standardized, easy-to-subdivide grid unit (a section splits neatly into quarters, halves, and smaller lots) that has structured American land ownership since the 18th century.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. This conversion uses the exact defined relationship between sections and townships, so the result shown is mathematically precise (rounded only for display).

How does a section compare in size to a township?

By definition, one section is 36 times smaller than one township (Section = 2.58999e+06 m², Township = 9.32396e+07 m²).

When is sections to townships conversion commonly used?

It commonly comes up in US land surveying, agricultural land division, and rural property descriptions in the central and western United States, as well as US land surveying, large-scale rural land division, and local government/administrative boundaries in many US states.

About this conversion

Sections and Townships are both units used to measure area. A section is a US Public Land Survey System unit equal to one square mile, or 640 acres. A township is a US Public Land Survey System unit equal to 36 sections, or 36 square miles. This converter uses the precise relationship 1 section = 0.0277777778 townships (equivalently 1 township = 36 sections), so results stay accurate for US land surveying, rural property descriptions and cadastral mapping, as well as US land surveying, cadastral mapping and rural land administration.
