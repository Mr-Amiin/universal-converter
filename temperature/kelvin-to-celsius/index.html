Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the kelvin (K). Kelvin is the SI absolute temperature scale, where 0 K is absolute zero.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the celsius (°C). Celsius is a temperature scale based on the freezing and boiling points of water.

How do I convert kelvin to celsius?

Use the formula °C = K × 1 − 273.15. For example, 25K converts to -248.15°C.

Why isn't this just a simple multiplication like other unit conversions?

Unlike most physical quantities, temperature scales don't all share the same zero point. The kelvin is the SI base unit of temperature, defined so that absolute zero is exactly 0 K. The Celsius scale sets 0 degrees at water's freezing point and 100 degrees at its boiling point at sea level. Converting between them requires both a scaling factor and an offset, not a single ratio.

Is the kelvin to celsius conversion exact?

Yes. The formula °C = K × 1 − 273.15 is a fixed, defined relationship between the two scales, so results are exact rather than estimated.

Can I convert celsius back to kelvin?

Yes. Rearranging the formula gives K = (°C + 273.15) ÷ 1.

About this conversion

This page converts kelvin (K) into celsius (°C) using the fixed relationship °C = K × 1 − 273.15.

Worked example: to convert 25K to °C, apply the formula: 25 × 1 − 273.15 = -248.15°C.

Reverse conversion: K = (°C + 273.15) ÷ 1.

Accuracy: temperature scale relationships are fixed by definition, so this conversion is exact; any imprecision comes only from the number of decimal places shown.

Kelvin: Kelvin is the SI absolute temperature scale, where 0 K is absolute zero. Celsius: Celsius is a temperature scale based on the freezing and boiling points of water.
