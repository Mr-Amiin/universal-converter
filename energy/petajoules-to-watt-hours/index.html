How the formula works

Both petajoule and watt-hour measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petajoules by 277777777778 to get watt hours. To reverse the conversion, multiply a watt-hour value by 3.600000000e-12 to get back to petajoules.

Frequently asked questions

What is Petajoule (PJ)?

What is Watt-hour (Wh)?

How do I convert petajoules to watt hours?

Multiply the petajoule value by 277777777778 to get the equivalent in watt hours. For example, 5 PJ × 277777777778 = 1.388889e+12 Wh.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 277777777778 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert watt hours back to petajoules?

Yes. Divide the watt-hour value by 277777777778, or multiply it by 3.600000000e-12, to get petajoules.

What is Petajoule used for in energy?

Petajoule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Petajoule and Watt-hour sit about 15 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Petajoule to Watt-hour?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 2.777778e+11, or assuming Petajoule and Watt-hour sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
