How the formula works

Both petajoule and kilojoule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petajoules by 1000000000000 to get kilojoules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a kilojoule value by 1.000000000e-12 to get back to petajoules.

Frequently asked questions

What is Petajoule (PJ)?

What is Kilojoule (kJ)?

A kilojoule (kJ) equals 1,000 joules and is commonly used on nutrition labels outside the United States.

How do I convert petajoules to kilojoules?

Multiply the petajoule value by 1000000000000 to get the equivalent in kilojoules. For example, 5 PJ × 1000000000000 = 5.000000e+12 kJ.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 1000000000000 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert kilojoules back to petajoules?

Yes. Divide the kilojoule value by 1000000000000, or multiply it by 1.000000000e-12, to get petajoules.

What is Petajoule used for in energy?

Petajoule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Petajoule and Kilojoule sit about 12 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Petajoule to Kilojoule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 1e+12, or assuming Petajoule and Kilojoule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
