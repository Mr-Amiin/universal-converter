How the formula works

Both microjoule and petajoule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microjoules by 1.000000000e-21 to get petajoules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petajoule value by 1.000000000e+21 to get back to microjoules.

What is Petajoule (PJ)?

How do I convert microjoules to petajoules?

Multiply the microjoule value by 1.000000000e-21 to get the equivalent in petajoules. For example, 5 uJ × 1.000000000e-21 = 5.000000e-21 PJ.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 1.000000000e-21 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert petajoules back to microjoules?

Yes. Divide the petajoule value by 1.000000000e-21, or multiply it by 1.000000000e+21, to get microjoules.

What is Microjoule used for in energy?

Microjoule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Microjoule and Petajoule sit about 21 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Microjoule to Petajoule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 1e-21, or assuming Microjoule and Petajoule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
