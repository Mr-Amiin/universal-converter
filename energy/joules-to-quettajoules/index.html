How the formula works

Both joule and quettajoule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in joules by 1.000000000e-30 to get quettajoules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettajoule value by 1.000000000e+30 to get back to joules.

Frequently asked questions

What is Joule (J)?

The joule (J) is the SI derived unit of energy, equal to the work done when a force of one newton acts over one meter.

What is Quettajoule (QJ)?

A quettajoule (QJ) is a metric-prefixed unit of energy equal to 1030 joules, using the SI quetta prefix.

How do I convert joules to quettajoules?

Multiply the joule value by 1.000000000e-30 to get the equivalent in quettajoules. For example, 5 J × 1.000000000e-30 = 5.000000e-30 QJ.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 1.000000000e-30 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert quettajoules back to joules?

Yes. Divide the quettajoule value by 1.000000000e-30, or multiply it by 1.000000000e+30, to get joules.

What is Joule used for in energy?

Joule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Joule and Quettajoule sit about 30 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Joule to Quettajoule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 1e-30, or assuming Joule and Quettajoule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
