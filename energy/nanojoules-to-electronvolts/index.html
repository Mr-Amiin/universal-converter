From definition

A nanojoule (nJ) is a metric-prefixed unit of energy equal to 10-9 joules, a fraction of a single joule, using the SI nano prefix.

To definition

An electronvolt (eV) is the energy gained by a single electron accelerating through a potential difference of one volt, widely used in atomic and particle physics.

How the formula works

Both nanojoule and electronvolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in nanojoules by 6241509074 to get electronvolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a electronvolt value by 1.602176634e-10 to get back to nanojoules.

What is Electronvolt (eV)?

An electronvolt (eV) is the energy gained by a single electron accelerating through a potential difference of one volt, widely used in atomic and particle physics.

How do I convert nanojoules to electronvolts?

Multiply the nanojoule value by 6241509074 to get the equivalent in electronvolts. For example, 5 nJ × 6241509074 = 3.120755e+10 eV.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 6241509074 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert electronvolts back to nanojoules?

Yes. Divide the electronvolt value by 6241509074, or multiply it by 1.602176634e-10, to get nanojoules.

What is Nanojoule used for in energy?

Nanojoule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Nanojoule and Electronvolt sit about 9 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Nanojoule to Electronvolt?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 6.241509e+09, or assuming Nanojoule and Electronvolt sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
