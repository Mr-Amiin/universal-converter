About Converting Hartrees to Electronvolts

Hartree and electronvolt are both units of energy. The hartree is the atomic unit of energy, about 4.3597447 × 10⁻¹⁸ joules (27.211386 electronvolts). The electronvolt is exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules, the energy gained by an electron crossing one volt.

Formula

eV = Eh × 27.21138625

This factor follows the definitions and conventions stated below: 1 hartree equals 27.21138625 electronvolts, and the reverse factor is 0.03674932218.

Understanding the Hartree

Hartree (Eh) measures energy. The hartree is the atomic unit of energy, about 4.3597447 × 10⁻¹⁸ joules (27.211386 electronvolts).

Convention: Value: CODATA 2022 Hartree energy, 4.359 744 722 206 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. This is a measured constant, not an exact definition.

Standard and definition

The hartree is covered by NIST — CODATA Recommended Values of the Fundamental Physical Constants.

Uses of the hartree today

  • Quantum chemistry and electronic-structure calculations
  • Reporting molecular energies in atomic units

Understanding the Electronvolt

Electronvolt (eV) measures energy. The electronvolt is exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules, the energy gained by an electron crossing one volt.

Standard and definition

The electronvolt is covered by BIPM — The International System of Units (SI Brochure).

Uses of the electronvolt today

  • Atomic, nuclear and particle physics
  • Semiconductor band gaps and photon energies
