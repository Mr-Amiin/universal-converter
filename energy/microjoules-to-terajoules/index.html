How the formula works

Both microjoule and terajoule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microjoules by 1.000000000e-18 to get terajoules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a terajoule value by 1.000000000e+18 to get back to microjoules.

Frequently asked questions

What is Microjoule (uJ)?

A microjoule (uJ) is a metric-prefixed unit of energy equal to 10-6 joules, a fraction of a single joule, using the SI micro prefix.

What is Terajoule (TJ)?

A terajoule (TJ) is a metric-prefixed unit of energy equal to 1012 joules, using the SI tera prefix.

How do I convert microjoules to terajoules?

Multiply the microjoule value by 1.000000000e-18 to get the equivalent in terajoules. For example, 5 uJ × 1.000000000e-18 = 5.000000e-18 TJ.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 1.000000000e-18 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert terajoules back to microjoules?

Yes. Divide the terajoule value by 1.000000000e-18, or multiply it by 1.000000000e+18, to get microjoules.

What is Microjoule used for in energy?

Microjoule belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

Why is the converted number so extreme?

Microjoule and Terajoule sit about 18 orders of magnitude apart on the metric scale, so converting between them naturally produces a very large or very small number, even for an everyday input value. This is expected and does not indicate an error.

What is a common mistake when converting Microjoule to Terajoule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 1e-18, or assuming Microjoule and Terajoule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
