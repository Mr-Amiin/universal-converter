How the formula works

Both kilowatt-hour and joule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kilowatt-hours by 3600000 to get joules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a joule value by 2.777777778e-07 to get back to kilowatt-hours.

Frequently asked questions

What is Kilowatt-hour (kWh)?

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the standard billing unit for electricity, equal to 1,000 watts sustained for one hour.

What is Joule (J)?

The joule (J) is the SI derived unit of energy, equal to the work done when a force of one newton acts over one meter.

How do I convert kilowatt-hours to joules?

Multiply the kilowatt-hour value by 3600000 to get the equivalent in joules. For example, 5 kWh × 3600000 = 1.800000e+07 J.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 3600000 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert joules back to kilowatt-hours?

Yes. Divide the joule value by 3600000, or multiply it by 2.777777778e-07, to get kilowatt-hours.

What is Kilowatt-hour used for in energy?

Kilowatt-hour belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

How precise is the 3.6e+06 conversion factor?

The figure shown is accurate to the precision displayed. For energy calculations that need more decimal places, use the exact factor 3.6e+06 directly rather than a rounded intermediate value.

What is a common mistake when converting Kilowatt-hour to Joule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 3.6e+06, or assuming Kilowatt-hour and Joule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
