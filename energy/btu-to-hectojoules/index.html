How the formula works

Both british thermal unit and hectojoule measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in btu by 10.55055853 to get hectojoules. To reverse the conversion, multiply a hectojoule value by 0.09478171203 to get back to btu.

Frequently asked questions

What is British thermal unit (BTU)?

The British thermal unit (BTU) is the energy needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit, widely used for heating and air-conditioning ratings.

What is Hectojoule (hJ)?

A hectojoule (hJ) is a metric-prefixed unit of energy equal to 102 joules, using the SI hecto prefix.

How do I convert btu to hectojoules?

Multiply the british thermal unit value by 10.55055853 to get the equivalent in hectojoules. For example, 5 BTU × 10.55055853 = 52.752793 hJ.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are defined against the SI base unit for this dimension, so the factor 10.55055853 is an exact, non-rounded value.

Can I convert hectojoules back to btu?

Yes. Divide the hectojoule value by 10.55055853, or multiply it by 0.09478171203, to get btu.

What is British thermal unit used for in energy?

British thermal unit belongs to the energy family of units. For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water.

How precise is the 10.5506 conversion factor?

The figure shown is accurate to the precision displayed. For energy calculations that need more decimal places, use the exact factor 10.5506 directly rather than a rounded intermediate value.

What is a common mistake when converting British thermal unit to Hectojoule?

The most common mistake is multiplying by the reverse factor instead of 10.5506, or assuming British thermal unit and Hectojoule sit on the same point of the energy scale. Always confirm the direction of the conversion before applying the formula.
