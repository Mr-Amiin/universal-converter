Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Slug (slug): The slug is the coherent unit of mass in the imperial (foot-pound-second) engineering system, equal to 1 lbf·s²/ft.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (4.04699872e-05) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Dram = 0.001771845195 kg, Tablespoon (US) = 0.01478676478 L, Slug = 14.59390294 kg, Teaspoon (US) = 0.004928921594 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Dram per Tablespoon (US) vs Slug per Teaspoon (US)

  • Where used: Dram per Tablespoon (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and cooking and recipe formulation, while slug per teaspoon (US) is more common in aerospace, mechanical, and structural engineering calculations and cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Tablespoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Tablespoon (US) history — Standardized alongside the US cup by home-economics and cookbook authorities in the early 1900s.
