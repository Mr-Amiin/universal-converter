Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Tonne (t): The metric tonne equals 1,000 kilograms.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Unit comparison

Dram per Pint (US) vs Tonne per Liter

  • Where used: Dram per Pint (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while tonne per liter is more common in international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing and virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Pint (US) history — Inherited from English liquid-measure tradition, the US pint diverged slightly from the UK pint after independence.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per pint (US) to tonne per liter?

Multiply the dram per pint (US) value by 3.744575854e-06, since 1 dr/pt = 3.744575854e-06 t/L. This works because dram per pint (US) and tonne per liter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Tonne in kg / Liter in liters).

How do I convert tonne per liter back to dram per pint (US)?

Multiply by 267052.9425, or equivalently divide by 3.744575854e-06, since 1 t/L = 267052.9425 dr/pt.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such very large mass units used for density?

Tonne is very large on its own, but density conversions often need it because international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per pint (US) to tonne per liter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in liter terms.
