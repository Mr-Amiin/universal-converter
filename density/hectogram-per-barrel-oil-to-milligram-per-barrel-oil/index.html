Frequently asked questions

How do you convert hectogram per barrel (oil) to milligram per barrel (oil)?

Multiply the hectogram per barrel (oil) value by 100000, since 1 hg/bbl = 100000 mg/bbl. This works because hectogram per barrel (oil) and milligram per barrel (oil) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Hectogram in kg / Barrel (oil) in liters) to (Milligram in kg / Barrel (oil) in liters).

How do I convert milligram per barrel (oil) back to hectogram per barrel (oil)?

Multiply by 1e-05, or equivalently divide by 100000, since 1 mg/bbl = 1e-05 hg/bbl.

Why is hectogram used to measure mass here?

A hectogram equals one hundred grams. It is common in European grocery retail and deli portioning, which is why densities are often reported using hectogram.

Why is barrel (oil) used as the volume unit?

The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products. Barrel (oil) shows up most often in petroleum production, refining, and commodity trading.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Milligram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the hectogram per barrel (oil) to milligram per barrel (oil) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between European grocery retail and deli portioning on one side and medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry on the other, or whenever a barrel (oil)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in barrel (oil) terms.
