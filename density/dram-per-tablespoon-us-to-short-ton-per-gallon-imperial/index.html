Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Short ton (tn): The US short ton equals 2,000 pounds, the standard 'ton' used in the United States.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Unit comparison

Dram per Tablespoon (US) vs Short ton per Gallon (imperial)

  • Where used: Dram per Tablespoon (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and cooking and recipe formulation, while short ton per gallon (imperial) is more common in US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight and UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Tablespoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Tablespoon (US) history — Standardized alongside the US cup by home-economics and cookbook authorities in the early 1900s.
