Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Troy ounce (oz t): The troy ounce is a heavier ounce (about 10% more than the avoirdupois ounce) used exclusively for precious metals and gemstones.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per gallon (US) to troy ounce per cubic inch?

Multiply the grain per gallon (US) value by 9.018759019e-06, since 1 gr/gal = 9.018759019e-06 oz t/in³. This works because grain per gallon (US) and troy ounce per cubic inch both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Gallon (US) in liters) to (Troy ounce in kg / Cubic inch in liters).

How do I convert troy ounce per cubic inch back to grain per gallon (US)?

Multiply by 110880, or equivalently divide by 9.018759019e-06, since 1 oz t/in³ = 110880 gr/gal.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is gallon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids. Gallon (US) shows up most often in US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per gallon (US) to troy ounce per cubic inch conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and precious-metal bullion trading (gold, silver, platinum) on the other, or whenever a gallon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic inch terms.
