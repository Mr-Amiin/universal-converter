Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Kilogram (kg): The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, currently defined via the fixed value of the Planck constant.

Cubic meter (m³): A cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume, equal to 1,000 liters.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (64.79891) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Grain = 6.479891e-05 kg, Milliliter = 0.001 L, Kilogram = 1 kg, Cubic meter = 1000 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per milliliter to kilogram per cubic meter?

Multiply the grain per milliliter value by 64.79891, since 1 gr/mL = 64.79891 kg/m³. This works because grain per milliliter and kilogram per cubic meter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Milliliter in liters) to (Kilogram in kg / Cubic meter in liters).

How do I convert kilogram per cubic meter back to grain per milliliter?

Multiply by 0.01543235835, or equivalently divide by 64.79891, since 1 kg/m³ = 0.01543235835 gr/mL.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is milliliter used as the volume unit?

A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. Milliliter shows up most often in medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per milliliter to kilogram per cubic meter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and virtually all scientific, industrial, and international trade contexts on the other, or whenever a milliliter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic meter terms.
