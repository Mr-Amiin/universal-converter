Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Short ton (tn): The US short ton equals 2,000 pounds, the standard 'ton' used in the United States.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (3.444722847e-08) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Gram = 0.001 kg, Pint (US) = 0.473176473 L, Short ton = 907.18474 kg, Tablespoon (US) = 0.01478676478 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Gram per Pint (US) vs Short ton per Tablespoon (US)

  • Where used: Gram per Pint (US) appears mainly in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while short ton per tablespoon (US) is more common in US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight and cooking and recipe formulation.
  • Scale: Gram is a everyday-scale mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Gram history — Defined in 1795 as the mass of one cubic centimeter of water at its freezing point, the gram remains the everyday metric mass unit worldwide. Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per pint (US) to short ton per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply the gram per pint (US) value by 3.444722847e-08, since 1 g/pt = 3.444722847e-08 tn/tbsp. This works because gram per pint (US) and short ton per tablespoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Short ton in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert short ton per tablespoon (US) back to gram per pint (US)?

Multiply by 29029911.68, or equivalently divide by 3.444722847e-08, since 1 tn/tbsp = 29029911.68 g/pt.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such very large mass units used for density?

Short ton is very large on its own, but density conversions often need it because US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the gram per pint (US) to short ton per tablespoon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in tablespoon (US) terms.
