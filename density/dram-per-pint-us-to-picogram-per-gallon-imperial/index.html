Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Picogram (pg): A picogram is one trillionth of a gram.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per pint (US) to picogram per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply the dram per pint (US) value by 1.702317884e+13, since 1 dr/pt = 1.702317884e+13 pg/gal (imp). This works because dram per pint (US) and picogram per gallon (imperial) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Picogram in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters).

How do I convert picogram per gallon (imperial) back to dram per pint (US)?

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Picogram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per pint (US) to picogram per gallon (imperial) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in gallon (imperial) terms.
