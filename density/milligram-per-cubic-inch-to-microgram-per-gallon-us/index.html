Unit history

Milligram: A decimal-prefixed SI unit built on the gram, part of the metric system formalized in 18th-century France.

Cubic inch: Derived from the British imperial inch, the cubic inch has been used in English-speaking engineering and manufacturing since before 19th-century standardization.

Microgram: An SI-prefixed fraction of the gram used since the metric system's 18th-century origins for very small quantities.

Gallon: The US liquid gallon derives from the old English wine gallon of 231 cubic inches, still used in the United States today.

Why is milligram per cubic inch used to express density?

Milligram measures mass (Milligram is 0.001 gram units.) and cubic inch measures volume (A cubic inch is the volume of a one-inch cube.), so milligram per cubic inch expresses how much mass is packed into that volume — the definition of density.
