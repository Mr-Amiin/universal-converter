Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Microgram (µg): A microgram is one millionth of a gram.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per pint (US) to microgram per gallon (US)?

Multiply the gram per pint (US) value by 8000000, since 1 g/pt = 8000000 µg/gal. This works because gram per pint (US) and microgram per gallon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Microgram in kg / Gallon (US) in liters).

How do I convert microgram per gallon (US) back to gram per pint (US)?

Multiply by 1.25e-07, or equivalently divide by 8000000, since 1 µg/gal = 1.25e-07 g/pt.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Microgram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because pharmacology, toxicology, and trace-contaminant testing routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the gram per pint (US) to microgram per gallon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and pharmacology, toxicology, and trace-contaminant testing on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in gallon (US) terms.
