Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Tonne (t): The metric tonne equals 1,000 kilograms.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Unit comparison

Dram per Pint (US) vs Tonne per Fluid ounce (US)

  • Where used: Dram per Pint (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while tonne per fluid ounce (US) is more common in international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing and beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Pint (US) history — Inherited from English liquid-measure tradition, the US pint diverged slightly from the UK pint after independence.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per pint (US) to tonne per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply the dram per pint (US) value by 1.107403247e-07, since 1 dr/pt = 1.107403247e-07 t/fl oz. This works because dram per pint (US) and tonne per fluid ounce (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Tonne in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters).

How do I convert tonne per fluid ounce (US) back to dram per pint (US)?

Multiply by 9030134.259, or equivalently divide by 1.107403247e-07, since 1 t/fl oz = 9030134.259 dr/pt.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such very large mass units used for density?

Tonne is very large on its own, but density conversions often need it because international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per pint (US) to tonne per fluid ounce (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in fluid ounce (US) terms.
