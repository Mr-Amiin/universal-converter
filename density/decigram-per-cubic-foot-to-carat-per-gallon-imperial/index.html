Frequently asked questions

How do you convert decigram per cubic foot to carat per imperial gallon?

Why is decigram used to measure mass here?

Deci- (d), from the Latin 'decimus' (tenth), is one of the original 1795 metric prefixes, familiar from the deciliter. In this pairing it measures mass per cubic foot of volume.

Why is cubic foot used to measure volume here?

A cubic foot is exactly 0.028316846592 cubic meters.

How do I convert carat per imperial gallon back to decigram per cubic foot?

About the Decigram per Cubic foot to Carat per Imperial gallon conversion

Decigram per Cubic foot (dg/ft3) is a density unit: Decigram is 0.1 gram units. A cubic foot is exactly 0.028316846592 cubic meters. Carat per Imperial gallon (ct/imp gal) is a density unit: A metric carat is exactly 200 milligrams. An imperial gallon is exactly 4.54609 liters.

Deci- (d), from the Latin 'decimus' (tenth), is one of the original 1795 metric prefixes, familiar from the deciliter. The carat is named after the carob seed, historically used by gem traders as a natural weight reference; it was fixed at 200 mg internationally in 1907. The imperial gallon was defined in 1824 as the volume of 10 pounds of water and remains standard in the UK.
