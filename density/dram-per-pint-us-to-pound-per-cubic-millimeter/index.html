Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Pound (lb): The avoirdupois pound is the standard mass unit in the US customary and British imperial systems.

Cubic millimeter (mm³): A cubic millimeter is an extremely small metric volume unit, a thousandth of a cubic centimeter.

Unit comparison

Dram per Pint (US) vs Pound per Cubic millimeter

  • Where used: Dram per Pint (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while pound per cubic millimeter is more common in US retail, shipping, construction, and everyday commerce and microfluidics, precision manufacturing, and materials science.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a large mass unit and extremely small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Pint (US) history — Inherited from English liquid-measure tradition, the US pint diverged slightly from the UK pint after independence.

How do I convert pound per cubic millimeter back to dram per pint (US)?
