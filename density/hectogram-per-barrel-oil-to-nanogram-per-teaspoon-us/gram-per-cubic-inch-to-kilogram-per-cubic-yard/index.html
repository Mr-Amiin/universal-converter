Formula

kg/yd³ = g/in³ × 46.656

g/in³ = kg/yd³ ÷ 46.656

Step-by-step worked example: Say a material sample measures 12.5 g/in³. Multiply by the conversion factor: 12.5 × 46.656 = 583.2. So 12.5 g/in³ is equal to 583.2 kg/yd³.

In context: For scale, aluminum has a density of about 44.2450728 g/in³ (roughly 2064.298117 kg/yd³). Materials denser than water push both numbers up; gases like air push them far below 1.

Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Kilogram (kg): The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, currently defined via the fixed value of the Planck constant.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (46.656) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Gram = 0.001 kg, Cubic inch = 0.016387064 L, Kilogram = 1 kg, Cubic yard = 764.554858 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Gram per Cubic inch vs Kilogram per Cubic yard

  • Where used: Gram per Cubic inch appears mainly in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging and engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances, while kilogram per cubic yard is more common in virtually all scientific, industrial, and international trade contexts and concrete, landscaping, and bulk earthwork estimating.
  • Scale: Gram is a everyday-scale mass unit and Cubic inch is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and large volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Gram history — Defined in 1795 as the mass of one cubic centimeter of water at its freezing point, the gram remains the everyday metric mass unit worldwide. Cubic inch history — Widely used historically to rate engine displacement in US and UK automotive industries.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per cubic inch to kilogram per cubic yard?

Multiply the gram per cubic inch value by 46.656, since 1 g/in³ = 46.656 kg/yd³. This works because gram per cubic inch and kilogram per cubic yard both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Cubic inch in liters) to (Kilogram in kg / Cubic yard in liters).

How do I convert kilogram per cubic yard back to gram per cubic inch?

Multiply by 0.02143347051, or equivalently divide by 46.656, since 1 kg/yd³ = 0.02143347051 g/in³.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is cubic inch used as the volume unit?

A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides. Cubic inch shows up most often in engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances.

Where is the gram per cubic inch to kilogram per cubic yard conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and virtually all scientific, industrial, and international trade contexts on the other, or whenever a cubic inch-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic yard terms.
