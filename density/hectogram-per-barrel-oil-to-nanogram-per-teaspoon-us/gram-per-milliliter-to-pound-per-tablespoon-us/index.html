Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Pound (lb): The avoirdupois pound is the standard mass unit in the US customary and British imperial systems.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Gram per Milliliter vs Pound per Tablespoon (US)

  • Where used: Gram per Milliliter appears mainly in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging and medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry, while pound per tablespoon (US) is more common in US retail, shipping, construction, and everyday commerce and cooking and recipe formulation.
  • Scale: Gram is a everyday-scale mass unit and Milliliter is a small volume unit, versus a large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Gram history — Defined in 1795 as the mass of one cubic centimeter of water at its freezing point, the gram remains the everyday metric mass unit worldwide.
