Formula

ct/qt = g/gal × 1.25

g/gal = ct/qt ÷ 1.25

Step-by-step worked example: Say a material sample measures 12.5 g/gal. Multiply by the conversion factor: 12.5 × 1.25 = 15.625. So 12.5 g/gal is equal to 15.625 ct/qt.

In context: For scale, air (sea level) has a density of about 4.542494141 g/gal (roughly 5.678117676 ct/qt). Materials denser than water push both numbers up; gases like air push them far below 1.

Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Carat (ct): A carat is the standard mass unit for gemstones and pearls, fixed at exactly 200 milligrams.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per gallon (US) to carat per quart (US)?

Multiply the gram per gallon (US) value by 1.25, since 1 g/gal = 1.25 ct/qt. This works because gram per gallon (US) and carat per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Gallon (US) in liters) to (Carat in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert carat per quart (US) back to gram per gallon (US)?

Multiply by 0.8, or equivalently divide by 1.25, since 1 ct/qt = 0.8 g/gal.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is gallon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids. Gallon (US) shows up most often in US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries.

Where is the gram per gallon (US) to carat per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and gemology, jewelry appraisal, and diamond grading on the other, or whenever a gallon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
