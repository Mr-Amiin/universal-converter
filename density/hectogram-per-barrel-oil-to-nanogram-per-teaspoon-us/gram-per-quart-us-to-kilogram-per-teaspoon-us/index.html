Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Kilogram (kg): The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, currently defined via the fixed value of the Planck constant.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per quart (US) to kilogram per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply the gram per quart (US) value by 5.208333333e-06, since 1 g/qt = 5.208333333e-06 kg/tsp. This works because gram per quart (US) and kilogram per teaspoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Quart (US) in liters) to (Kilogram in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert kilogram per teaspoon (US) back to gram per quart (US)?

Multiply by 192000, or equivalently divide by 5.208333333e-06, since 1 kg/tsp = 192000 g/qt.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is quart (US) used as the volume unit?

Where is the gram per quart (US) to kilogram per teaspoon (US) conversion used?
