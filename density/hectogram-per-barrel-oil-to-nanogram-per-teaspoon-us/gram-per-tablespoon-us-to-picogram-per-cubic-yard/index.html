Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Picogram (pg): A picogram is one trillionth of a gram.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per tablespoon (US) to picogram per cubic yard?

Multiply the gram per tablespoon (US) value by 5.170535065e+16, since 1 g/tbsp = 5.170535065e+16 pg/yd³. This works because gram per tablespoon (US) and picogram per cubic yard both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters) to (Picogram in kg / Cubic yard in liters).

How do I convert picogram per cubic yard back to gram per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply by 0, or equivalently divide by 5.170535065e+16, since 1 pg/yd³ = 0 g/tbsp.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is tablespoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons. Tablespoon (US) shows up most often in cooking and recipe formulation.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Picogram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the gram per tablespoon (US) to picogram per cubic yard conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry on the other, or whenever a tablespoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic yard terms.
