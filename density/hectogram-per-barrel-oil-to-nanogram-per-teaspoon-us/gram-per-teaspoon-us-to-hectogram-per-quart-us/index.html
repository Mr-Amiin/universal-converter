Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Hectogram (hg): A hectogram equals one hundred grams.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per teaspoon (US) to hectogram per quart (US)?

Multiply the gram per teaspoon (US) value by 1.92, since 1 g/tsp = 1.92 hg/qt. This works because gram per teaspoon (US) and hectogram per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters) to (Hectogram in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert hectogram per quart (US) back to gram per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply by 0.5208333333, or equivalently divide by 1.92, since 1 hg/qt = 0.5208333333 g/tsp.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is teaspoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon. Teaspoon (US) shows up most often in cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.

Where is the gram per teaspoon (US) to hectogram per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and European grocery retail and deli portioning on the other, or whenever a teaspoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
