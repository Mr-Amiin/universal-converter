Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Carat (ct): A carat is the standard mass unit for gemstones and pearls, fixed at exactly 200 milligrams.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (0.03252633534) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Gram = 0.001 kg, Gallon (imperial) = 4.54609 L, Carat = 0.0002 kg, Fluid ounce (US) = 0.02957352956 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Gram per Gallon (imperial) vs Carat per Fluid ounce (US)

  • Where used: Gram per Gallon (imperial) appears mainly in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging and UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade, while carat per fluid ounce (US) is more common in gemology, jewelry appraisal, and diamond grading and beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.
  • Scale: Gram is a everyday-scale mass unit and Gallon (imperial) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a small mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Gram history — Defined in 1795 as the mass of one cubic centimeter of water at its freezing point, the gram remains the everyday metric mass unit worldwide. Gallon (imperial) history — Defined in 1824 as the volume of 10 pounds of water, the imperial gallon remains in use across the UK and parts of the Commonwealth.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per gallon (imperial) to carat per fluid ounce (US)?

How do I convert carat per fluid ounce (US) back to gram per gallon (imperial)?

Why is gram used to measure mass here?
