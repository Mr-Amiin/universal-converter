Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Centigram (cg): A centigram is one hundredth of a gram in the metric system.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (0.02199692483) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Gram = 0.001 kg, Gallon (imperial) = 4.54609 L, Centigram = 1e-05 kg, Milliliter = 0.001 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per gallon (imperial) to centigram per milliliter?

Multiply the gram per gallon (imperial) value by 0.02199692483, since 1 g/gal (imp) = 0.02199692483 cg/mL. This works because gram per gallon (imperial) and centigram per milliliter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters) to (Centigram in kg / Milliliter in liters).

How do I convert centigram per milliliter back to gram per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply by 45.4609, or equivalently divide by 0.02199692483, since 1 cg/mL = 45.4609 g/gal (imp).

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is gallon (imperial) used as the volume unit?

The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon. Gallon (imperial) shows up most often in UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.

Where is the gram per gallon (imperial) to centigram per milliliter conversion used?
