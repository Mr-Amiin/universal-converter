Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Stone (st): The stone equals 14 pounds and remains a common body-weight unit in the UK and Ireland.

Cubic foot (ft³): A cubic foot is the volume of a cube with 1-foot sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per liter to stone per cubic foot?

Multiply the grain per liter value by 0.0002889474142, since 1 gr/L = 0.0002889474142 st/ft³. This works because grain per liter and stone per cubic foot both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Liter in liters) to (Stone in kg / Cubic foot in liters).

How do I convert stone per cubic foot back to grain per liter?

Multiply by 3460.837339, or equivalently divide by 0.0002889474142, since 1 st/ft³ = 3460.837339 gr/L.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is liter used as the volume unit?

The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter. Liter shows up most often in virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per liter to stone per cubic foot conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and UK/Ireland personal body-weight reporting on the other, or whenever a liter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic foot terms.
