Formula & worked example

lb/L = oz/mm3 × 62500

oz/mm3 = lb/L ÷ 62500 (or × 1.6e-05)

For example, granite has a density of about 2700 kg/m³, which is roughly 9.5e-05 oz/mm3. Multiplying by the conversion factor: 9.5e-05 × 62500 = 5.9375. So 9.5e-05 oz/mm3 of granite is equal to 5.9375 lb/L.

What is 1 Ounce per Cubic Millimeter in Pound per Liter?

How do I convert pound per liter back to ounce per cubic millimeter?

Divide by 62500, or multiply by 1.6e-05, since 1 lb/L = 1.6e-05 oz/mm3.

Why is the ounce per cubic millimeter to pound per liter conversion factor so large?

The Pound per Liter is a much larger unit of density than the Ounce per Cubic Millimeter, because Ounce and Pound (and/or the underlying volume units) differ by several orders of magnitude. That is why the conversion factor (62500) is far from 1.

Ounce per Cubic Millimeter vs Pound per Liter

Ounce per Cubic Millimeter is the larger density unit here: it takes 62500 lb/L to equal one ounce per cubic millimeter. Ounce per Cubic Millimeter is built from ounce (An avoirdupois ounce is 1/16 pound.) divided by cubic millimeter (Cubic metric volume unit.), while Pound per Liter is built from pound (An avoirdupois pound is exactly 0.45359237 kilograms.) divided by liter (A liter is one cubic decimeter.). Both are imperial/US customary combinations, typically used in imperial and US customary engineering, construction, and shipping calculations.

Ounce: An avoirdupois ounce is 1/16 pound. Cubic Millimeter: Cubic metric volume unit.
