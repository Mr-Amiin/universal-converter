Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Megagram (Mg): A megagram equals one million grams, numerically identical to a metric tonne.

Cubic foot (ft³): A cubic foot is the volume of a cube with 1-foot sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per quart (US) to megagram per cubic foot?

Multiply the dram per quart (US) value by 5.301729e-05, since 1 dr/qt = 5.301729e-05 Mg/ft³. This works because dram per quart (US) and megagram per cubic foot both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Quart (US) in liters) to (Megagram in kg / Cubic foot in liters).

How do I convert megagram per cubic foot back to dram per quart (US)?

Multiply by 18861.77132, or equivalently divide by 5.301729e-05, since 1 Mg/ft³ = 18861.77132 dr/qt.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is quart (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints. Quart (US) shows up most often in US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.

Why are such very large mass units used for density?

Megagram is very large on its own, but density conversions often need it because scientific literature and SI-strict engineering documentation routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per quart (US) to megagram per cubic foot conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and scientific literature and SI-strict engineering documentation on the other, or whenever a quart (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic foot terms.
