Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Stone (st): The stone equals 14 pounds and remains a common body-weight unit in the UK and Ireland.

Cubic meter (m³): A cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume, equal to 1,000 liters.

Unit comparison

Dram per Teaspoon (US) vs Stone per Cubic meter

  • Where used: Dram per Teaspoon (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing, while stone per cubic meter is more common in UK/Ireland personal body-weight reporting and civil engineering, shipping, and international trade.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Teaspoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and large volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Teaspoon (US) history — Standardized for home cooking measurement in the US alongside the tablespoon and cup.

How do I convert stone per cubic meter back to dram per teaspoon (US)?
