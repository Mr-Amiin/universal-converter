Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Microgram (µg): A microgram is one millionth of a gram.

Cubic millimeter (mm³): A cubic millimeter is an extremely small metric volume unit, a thousandth of a cubic centimeter.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per fluid ounce (US) to microgram per cubic millimeter?

Multiply the grain per fluid ounce (US) value by 2.191111814, since 1 gr/fl oz = 2.191111814 µg/mm³. This works because grain per fluid ounce (US) and microgram per cubic millimeter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters) to (Microgram in kg / Cubic millimeter in liters).

How do I convert microgram per cubic millimeter back to grain per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply by 0.456389306, or equivalently divide by 2.191111814, since 1 µg/mm³ = 0.456389306 gr/fl oz.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is fluid ounce (US) used as the volume unit?

The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages. Fluid ounce (US) shows up most often in beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per fluid ounce (US) to microgram per cubic millimeter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmacology, toxicology, and trace-contaminant testing on the other, or whenever a fluid ounce (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic millimeter terms.
