Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Pound (lb): The avoirdupois pound is the standard mass unit in the US customary and British imperial systems.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per milliliter to pound per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply the grain per milliliter value by 0.004224789937, since 1 gr/mL = 0.004224789937 lb/fl oz. This works because grain per milliliter and pound per fluid ounce (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Milliliter in liters) to (Pound in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters).

How do I convert pound per fluid ounce (US) back to grain per milliliter?

Multiply by 236.6981589, or equivalently divide by 0.004224789937, since 1 lb/fl oz = 236.6981589 gr/mL.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is milliliter used as the volume unit?

A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. Milliliter shows up most often in medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per milliliter to pound per fluid ounce (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and US retail, shipping, construction, and everyday commerce on the other, or whenever a milliliter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in fluid ounce (US) terms.
