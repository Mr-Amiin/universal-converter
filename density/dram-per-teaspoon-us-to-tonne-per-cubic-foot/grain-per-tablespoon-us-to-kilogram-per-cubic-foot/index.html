Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Kilogram (kg): The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, currently defined via the fixed value of the Planck constant.

Cubic foot (ft³): A cubic foot is the volume of a cube with 1-foot sides.

  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Tablespoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per tablespoon (US) to kilogram per cubic foot?

Multiply the grain per tablespoon (US) value by 0.1240907542, since 1 gr/tbsp = 0.1240907542 kg/ft³. This works because grain per tablespoon (US) and kilogram per cubic foot both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters) to (Kilogram in kg / Cubic foot in liters).

How do I convert kilogram per cubic foot back to grain per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply by 8.058618118, or equivalently divide by 0.1240907542, since 1 kg/ft³ = 8.058618118 gr/tbsp.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is tablespoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons. Tablespoon (US) shows up most often in cooking and recipe formulation.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per tablespoon (US) to kilogram per cubic foot conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and virtually all scientific, industrial, and international trade contexts on the other, or whenever a tablespoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic foot terms.
