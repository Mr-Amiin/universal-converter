Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Ounce (oz): The avoirdupois ounce is 1/16 of a pound, the everyday ounce used for food and retail goods.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (0.07314285714) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Grain = 6.479891e-05 kg, Fluid ounce (US) = 0.02957352956 L, Ounce = 0.02834952313 kg, Quart (US) = 0.946352946 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Grain per Fluid ounce (US) vs Ounce per Quart (US)

  • Where used: Grain per Fluid ounce (US) appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement, while ounce per quart (US) is more common in US food packaging, retail, and postal weight limits and US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Fluid ounce (US) is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per fluid ounce (US) to ounce per quart (US)?

Multiply the grain per fluid ounce (US) value by 0.07314285714, since 1 gr/fl oz = 0.07314285714 oz/qt. This works because grain per fluid ounce (US) and ounce per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters) to (Ounce in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert ounce per quart (US) back to grain per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply by 13.671875, or equivalently divide by 0.07314285714, since 1 oz/qt = 13.671875 gr/fl oz.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is fluid ounce (US) used as the volume unit?

The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages. Fluid ounce (US) shows up most often in beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per fluid ounce (US) to ounce per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and US food packaging, retail, and postal weight limits on the other, or whenever a fluid ounce (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
