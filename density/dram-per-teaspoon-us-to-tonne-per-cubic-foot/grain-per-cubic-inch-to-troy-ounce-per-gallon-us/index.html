Formula

oz t/gal = gr/in³ × 0.48125

gr/in³ = oz t/gal ÷ 0.48125

Step-by-step worked example: Say a material sample measures 12.5 gr/in³. Multiply by the conversion factor: 12.5 × 0.48125 = 6.015625. So 12.5 gr/in³ is equal to 6.015625 oz t/gal.

In context: For scale, gasoline has a density of about 187.1393726 gr/in³ (roughly 90.06082304 oz t/gal). Materials denser than water push both numbers up; gases like air push them far below 1.

Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Troy ounce (oz t): The troy ounce is a heavier ounce (about 10% more than the avoirdupois ounce) used exclusively for precious metals and gemstones.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Conversion accuracy

Unit comparison

Grain per Cubic inch vs Troy ounce per Gallon (US)

  • Where used: Grain per Cubic inch appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances, while troy ounce per gallon (US) is more common in precious-metal bullion trading (gold, silver, platinum) and US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Cubic inch is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Cubic inch history — Widely used historically to rate engine displacement in US and UK automotive industries.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic inch to troy ounce per gallon (US)?

Multiply the grain per cubic inch value by 0.48125, since 1 gr/in³ = 0.48125 oz t/gal. This works because grain per cubic inch and troy ounce per gallon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic inch in liters) to (Troy ounce in kg / Gallon (US) in liters).

How do I convert troy ounce per gallon (US) back to grain per cubic inch?

Multiply by 2.077922078, or equivalently divide by 0.48125, since 1 oz t/gal = 2.077922078 gr/in³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic inch used as the volume unit?

A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides. Cubic inch shows up most often in engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic inch to troy ounce per gallon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and precious-metal bullion trading (gold, silver, platinum) on the other, or whenever a cubic inch-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in gallon (US) terms.
