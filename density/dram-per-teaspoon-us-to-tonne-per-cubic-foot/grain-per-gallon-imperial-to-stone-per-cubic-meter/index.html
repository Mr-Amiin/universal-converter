Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Stone (st): The stone equals 14 pounds and remains a common body-weight unit in the UK and Ireland.

Cubic meter (m³): A cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume, equal to 1,000 liters.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (0.002244584166) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Grain = 6.479891e-05 kg, Gallon (imperial) = 4.54609 L, Stone = 6.35029318 kg, Cubic meter = 1000 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Gallon (imperial) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and large volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per gallon (imperial) to stone per cubic meter?

Multiply the grain per gallon (imperial) value by 0.002244584166, since 1 gr/gal (imp) = 0.002244584166 st/m³. This works because grain per gallon (imperial) and stone per cubic meter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters) to (Stone in kg / Cubic meter in liters).

How do I convert stone per cubic meter back to grain per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply by 445.51682, or equivalently divide by 0.002244584166, since 1 st/m³ = 445.51682 gr/gal (imp).

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is gallon (imperial) used as the volume unit?

The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon. Gallon (imperial) shows up most often in UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per gallon (imperial) to stone per cubic meter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and UK/Ireland personal body-weight reporting on the other, or whenever a gallon (imperial)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic meter terms.
