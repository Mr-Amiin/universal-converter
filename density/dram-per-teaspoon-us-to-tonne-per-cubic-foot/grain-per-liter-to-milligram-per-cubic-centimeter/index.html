Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Milligram (mg): A milligram is one thousandth of a gram.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Conversion accuracy

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per liter to milligram per cubic centimeter?

Multiply the grain per liter value by 0.06479891, since 1 gr/L = 0.06479891 mg/cm³. This works because grain per liter and milligram per cubic centimeter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Liter in liters) to (Milligram in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters).

How do I convert milligram per cubic centimeter back to grain per liter?

Multiply by 15.43235835, or equivalently divide by 0.06479891, since 1 mg/cm³ = 15.43235835 gr/L.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is liter used as the volume unit?

The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter. Liter shows up most often in virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per liter to milligram per cubic centimeter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry on the other, or whenever a liter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic centimeter terms.
