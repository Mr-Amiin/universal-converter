Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Barrel (oil) (bbl): The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products.

Slug (slug): The slug is the coherent unit of mass in the imperial (foot-pound-second) engineering system, equal to 1 lbf·s²/ft.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Unit comparison

Grain per Barrel (oil) vs Slug per Teaspoon (US)

  • Where used: Grain per Barrel (oil) appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and petroleum production, refining, and commodity trading, while slug per teaspoon (US) is more common in aerospace, mechanical, and structural engineering calculations and cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Barrel (oil) is a large volume unit, versus a large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Why is grain used to measure mass here?
