Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Centigram (cg): A centigram is one hundredth of a gram in the metric system.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic centimeter to centigram per cubic inch?

Multiply the grain per cubic centimeter value by 106.1863885, since 1 gr/cm³ = 106.1863885 cg/in³. This works because grain per cubic centimeter and centigram per cubic inch both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters) to (Centigram in kg / Cubic inch in liters).

How do I convert centigram per cubic inch back to grain per cubic centimeter?

Multiply by 0.009417402869, or equivalently divide by 106.1863885, since 1 cg/in³ = 0.009417402869 gr/cm³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic centimeter used as the volume unit?

A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter. Cubic centimeter shows up most often in medical dosing, engine displacement, and laboratory science.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic centimeter to centigram per cubic inch conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmacy compounding and laboratory dosing on the other, or whenever a cubic centimeter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic inch terms.
