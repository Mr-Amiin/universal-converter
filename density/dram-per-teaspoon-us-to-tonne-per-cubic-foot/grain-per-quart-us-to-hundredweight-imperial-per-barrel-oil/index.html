Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Hundredweight (long) (cwt (long)): The imperial (long) hundredweight equals 112 pounds, used historically in the UK and Commonwealth.

Barrel (oil) (bbl): The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per quart (US) to hundredweight (long) per barrel (oil)?

Multiply the grain per quart (US) value by 0.0002142857143, since 1 gr/qt = 0.0002142857143 cwt (long)/bbl. This works because grain per quart (US) and hundredweight (long) per barrel (oil) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Quart (US) in liters) to (Hundredweight (long) in kg / Barrel (oil) in liters).

How do I convert hundredweight (long) per barrel (oil) back to grain per quart (US)?

Multiply by 4666.666667, or equivalently divide by 0.0002142857143, since 1 cwt (long)/bbl = 4666.666667 gr/qt.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is quart (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints. Quart (US) shows up most often in US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per quart (US) to hundredweight (long) per barrel (oil) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and UK agricultural and bulk commodity trading on the other, or whenever a quart (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in barrel (oil) terms.
