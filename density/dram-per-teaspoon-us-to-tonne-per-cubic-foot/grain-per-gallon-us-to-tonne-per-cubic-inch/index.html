Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Tonne (t): The metric tonne equals 1,000 kilograms.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Unit comparison

Grain per Gallon (US) vs Tonne per Cubic inch

  • Where used: Grain per Gallon (US) appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries, while tonne per cubic inch is more common in international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing and engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Gallon (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Gallon (US) history — Based on the old English wine gallon rather than the imperial gallon, giving the US gallon its smaller, distinct value.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per gallon (US) to tonne per cubic inch?

Multiply the grain per gallon (US) value by 0, since 1 gr/gal = 0 t/in³. This works because grain per gallon (US) and tonne per cubic inch both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Gallon (US) in liters) to (Tonne in kg / Cubic inch in liters).

How do I convert tonne per cubic inch back to grain per gallon (US)?

Multiply by 3564874780, or equivalently divide by 0, since 1 t/in³ = 3564874780 gr/gal.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is gallon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids. Gallon (US) shows up most often in US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per gallon (US) to tonne per cubic inch conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing on the other, or whenever a gallon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic inch terms.
