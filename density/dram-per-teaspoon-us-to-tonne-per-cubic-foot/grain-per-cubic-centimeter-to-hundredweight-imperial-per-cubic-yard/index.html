Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Hundredweight (long) (cwt (long)): The imperial (long) hundredweight equals 112 pounds, used historically in the UK and Commonwealth.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Cubic centimeter is a small volume unit, versus a large mass unit and large volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic centimeter to hundredweight (long) per cubic yard?

Multiply the grain per cubic centimeter value by 0.9751975229, since 1 gr/cm³ = 0.9751975229 cwt (long)/yd³. This works because grain per cubic centimeter and hundredweight (long) per cubic yard both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters) to (Hundredweight (long) in kg / Cubic yard in liters).

How do I convert hundredweight (long) per cubic yard back to grain per cubic centimeter?

Multiply by 1.025433286, or equivalently divide by 0.9751975229, since 1 cwt (long)/yd³ = 1.025433286 gr/cm³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic centimeter used as the volume unit?

A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter. Cubic centimeter shows up most often in medical dosing, engine displacement, and laboratory science.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic centimeter to hundredweight (long) per cubic yard conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and UK agricultural and bulk commodity trading on the other, or whenever a cubic centimeter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic yard terms.
