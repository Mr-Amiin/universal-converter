Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Hundredweight (long) (cwt (long)): The imperial (long) hundredweight equals 112 pounds, used historically in the UK and Commonwealth.

Cup (US) (cup): The US cup is a customary volume unit used mainly in cooking and recipes.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per milliliter to hundredweight (long) per cup (US)?

Multiply the grain per milliliter value by 0.0003017707098, since 1 gr/mL = 0.0003017707098 cwt (long)/cup. This works because grain per milliliter and hundredweight (long) per cup (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Milliliter in liters) to (Hundredweight (long) in kg / Cup (US) in liters).

How do I convert hundredweight (long) per cup (US) back to grain per milliliter?

Multiply by 3313.774225, or equivalently divide by 0.0003017707098, since 1 cwt (long)/cup = 3313.774225 gr/mL.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is milliliter used as the volume unit?

A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. Milliliter shows up most often in medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per milliliter to hundredweight (long) per cup (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and UK agricultural and bulk commodity trading on the other, or whenever a milliliter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cup (US) terms.
