Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

  • Where used: Grain per Pint (US) appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while dram per cubic yard is more common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and concrete, landscaping, and bulk earthwork estimating.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a small mass unit and large volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per pint (US) to dram per cubic yard?

Multiply the grain per pint (US) value by 59.09182931, since 1 gr/pt = 59.09182931 dr/yd³. This works because grain per pint (US) and dram per cubic yard both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Dram in kg / Cubic yard in liters).

How do I convert dram per cubic yard back to grain per pint (US)?

Multiply by 0.01692281338, or equivalently divide by 59.09182931, since 1 dr/yd³ = 0.01692281338 gr/pt.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per pint (US) to dram per cubic yard conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic yard terms.
