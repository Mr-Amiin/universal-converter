Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Barrel (oil) (bbl): The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products.

Hectogram (hg): A hectogram equals one hundred grams.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Unit comparison

Grain per Barrel (oil) vs Hectogram per Quart (US)

  • Where used: Grain per Barrel (oil) appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and petroleum production, refining, and commodity trading, while hectogram per quart (US) is more common in European grocery retail and deli portioning and US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Barrel (oil) is a large volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Barrel (oil) history — Standardized by the early US oil industry from the 42-gallon wooden barrels used to ship crude oil in the 1860s.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per barrel (oil) to hectogram per quart (US)?

Multiply the grain per barrel (oil) value by 3.857077976e-06, since 1 gr/bbl = 3.857077976e-06 hg/qt. This works because grain per barrel (oil) and hectogram per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Barrel (oil) in liters) to (Hectogram in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert hectogram per quart (US) back to grain per barrel (oil)?

Multiply by 259263.6203, or equivalently divide by 3.857077976e-06, since 1 hg/qt = 259263.6203 gr/bbl.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is barrel (oil) used as the volume unit?

The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products. Barrel (oil) shows up most often in petroleum production, refining, and commodity trading.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per barrel (oil) to hectogram per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and European grocery retail and deli portioning on the other, or whenever a barrel (oil)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
