Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Pound (lb): The avoirdupois pound is the standard mass unit in the US customary and British imperial systems.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per fluid ounce (US) to pound per cubic centimeter?

Multiply the grain per fluid ounce (US) value by 4.830574672e-06, since 1 gr/fl oz = 4.830574672e-06 lb/cm³. This works because grain per fluid ounce (US) and pound per cubic centimeter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters) to (Pound in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters).

How do I convert pound per cubic centimeter back to grain per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply by 207014.7069, or equivalently divide by 4.830574672e-06, since 1 lb/cm³ = 207014.7069 gr/fl oz.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is fluid ounce (US) used as the volume unit?

The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages. Fluid ounce (US) shows up most often in beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per fluid ounce (US) to pound per cubic centimeter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and US retail, shipping, construction, and everyday commerce on the other, or whenever a fluid ounce (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic centimeter terms.
