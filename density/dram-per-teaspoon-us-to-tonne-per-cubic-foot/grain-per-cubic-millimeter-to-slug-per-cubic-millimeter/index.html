Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic millimeter (mm³): A cubic millimeter is an extremely small metric volume unit, a thousandth of a cubic centimeter.

Slug (slug): The slug is the coherent unit of mass in the imperial (foot-pound-second) engineering system, equal to 1 lbf·s²/ft.

Cubic millimeter (mm³): A cubic millimeter is an extremely small metric volume unit, a thousandth of a cubic centimeter.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic millimeter to slug per cubic millimeter?

Multiply the grain per cubic millimeter value by 4.440135739e-06, since 1 gr/mm³ = 4.440135739e-06 slug/mm³. This works because grain per cubic millimeter and slug per cubic millimeter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic millimeter in liters) to (Slug in kg / Cubic millimeter in liters).

How do I convert slug per cubic millimeter back to grain per cubic millimeter?

Multiply by 225218.3399, or equivalently divide by 4.440135739e-06, since 1 slug/mm³ = 225218.3399 gr/mm³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic millimeter used as the volume unit?

A cubic millimeter is an extremely small metric volume unit, a thousandth of a cubic centimeter. Cubic millimeter shows up most often in microfluidics, precision manufacturing, and materials science.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic millimeter to slug per cubic millimeter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and aerospace, mechanical, and structural engineering calculations on the other, or whenever a cubic millimeter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic millimeter terms.
