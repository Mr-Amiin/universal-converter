Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Troy ounce (oz t): The troy ounce is a heavier ounce (about 10% more than the avoirdupois ounce) used exclusively for precious metals and gemstones.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per liter to troy ounce per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply the grain per liter value by 6.161151992e-05, since 1 gr/L = 6.161151992e-05 oz t/fl oz. This works because grain per liter and troy ounce per fluid ounce (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Liter in liters) to (Troy ounce in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters).

How do I convert troy ounce per fluid ounce (US) back to grain per liter?

Multiply by 16230.7309, or equivalently divide by 6.161151992e-05, since 1 oz t/fl oz = 16230.7309 gr/L.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is liter used as the volume unit?

The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter. Liter shows up most often in virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per liter to troy ounce per fluid ounce (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and precious-metal bullion trading (gold, silver, platinum) on the other, or whenever a liter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in fluid ounce (US) terms.
