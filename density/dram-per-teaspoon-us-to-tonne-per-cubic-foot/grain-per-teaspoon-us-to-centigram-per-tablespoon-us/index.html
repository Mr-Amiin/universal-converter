Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Centigram (cg): A centigram is one hundredth of a gram in the metric system.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per teaspoon (US) to centigram per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply the grain per teaspoon (US) value by 19.439673, since 1 gr/tsp = 19.439673 cg/tbsp. This works because grain per teaspoon (US) and centigram per tablespoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters) to (Centigram in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert centigram per tablespoon (US) back to grain per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply by 0.05144119451, or equivalently divide by 19.439673, since 1 cg/tbsp = 0.05144119451 gr/tsp.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is teaspoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon. Teaspoon (US) shows up most often in cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per teaspoon (US) to centigram per tablespoon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmacy compounding and laboratory dosing on the other, or whenever a teaspoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in tablespoon (US) terms.
