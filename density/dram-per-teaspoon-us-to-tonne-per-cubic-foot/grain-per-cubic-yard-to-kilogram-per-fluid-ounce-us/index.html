Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

Kilogram (kg): The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass, currently defined via the fixed value of the Planck constant.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic yard to kilogram per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply the grain per cubic yard value by 2.506468255e-09, since 1 gr/yd³ = 2.506468255e-09 kg/fl oz. This works because grain per cubic yard and kilogram per fluid ounce (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic yard in liters) to (Kilogram in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters).

How do I convert kilogram per fluid ounce (US) back to grain per cubic yard?

Multiply by 398967750, or equivalently divide by 2.506468255e-09, since 1 kg/fl oz = 398967750 gr/yd³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic yard used as the volume unit?

A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil. Cubic yard shows up most often in concrete, landscaping, and bulk earthwork estimating.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic yard to kilogram per fluid ounce (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and virtually all scientific, industrial, and international trade contexts on the other, or whenever a cubic yard-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in fluid ounce (US) terms.
