oz/cm³ = gr/gal (imp) × 5.027868533e-07

gr/gal (imp) = oz/cm³ ÷ 5.027868533e-07

Step-by-step worked example: Say a material sample measures 12.5 gr/gal (imp). Multiply by the conversion factor: 12.5 × 5.027868533e-07 = 6.284835666e-06. So 12.5 gr/gal (imp) is equal to 6.284835666e-06 oz/cm³.

Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Ounce (oz): The avoirdupois ounce is 1/16 of a pound, the everyday ounce used for food and retail goods.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (5.027868533e-07) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Grain = 6.479891e-05 kg, Gallon (imperial) = 4.54609 L, Ounce = 0.02834952313 kg, Cubic centimeter = 0.001 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Gallon (imperial) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per gallon (imperial) to ounce per cubic centimeter?

Multiply the grain per gallon (imperial) value by 5.027868533e-07, since 1 gr/gal (imp) = 5.027868533e-07 oz/cm³. This works because grain per gallon (imperial) and ounce per cubic centimeter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters) to (Ounce in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters).

How do I convert ounce per cubic centimeter back to grain per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply by 1988914.375, or equivalently divide by 5.027868533e-07, since 1 oz/cm³ = 1988914.375 gr/gal (imp).

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is gallon (imperial) used as the volume unit?

The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon. Gallon (imperial) shows up most often in UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per gallon (imperial) to ounce per cubic centimeter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and US food packaging, retail, and postal weight limits on the other, or whenever a gallon (imperial)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic centimeter terms.
