Unit history

Milligram: A decimal-prefixed SI unit built on the gram, part of the metric system formalized in 18th-century France.

Cubic Millimeter: A coherent SI volume unit derived directly from the meter, used where fine spatial precision is required.

US hundredweight: The US short hundredweight of 100 pounds was adopted so that 20 hundredweight equal one short ton.

Gallon: The US liquid gallon derives from the old English wine gallon of 231 cubic inches, still used in the United States today.

Why is milligram per cubic millimeter used to express density?

Milligram measures mass (Milligram is 0.001 gram units.) and cubic millimeter measures volume (Cubic metric volume unit.), so milligram per cubic millimeter expresses how much mass is packed into that volume — the definition of density.
