Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Long ton (long tn): The long (imperial) ton equals 2,240 pounds, still used in UK shipping and some commodity markets.

Cubic foot (ft³): A cubic foot is the volume of a cube with 1-foot sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per quart (US) to long ton per cubic foot?

Multiply the grain per quart (US) value by 1.908295786e-06, since 1 gr/qt = 1.908295786e-06 long tn/ft³. This works because grain per quart (US) and long ton per cubic foot both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Quart (US) in liters) to (Long ton in kg / Cubic foot in liters).

How do I convert long ton per cubic foot back to grain per quart (US)?

Multiply by 524027.7778, or equivalently divide by 1.908295786e-06, since 1 long tn/ft³ = 524027.7778 gr/qt.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is quart (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints. Quart (US) shows up most often in US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per quart (US) to long ton per cubic foot conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and maritime shipping and UK heavy industry on the other, or whenever a quart (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic foot terms.
