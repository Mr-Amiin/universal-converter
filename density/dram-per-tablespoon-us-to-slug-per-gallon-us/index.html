Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Slug (slug): The slug is the coherent unit of mass in the imperial (foot-pound-second) engineering system, equal to 1 lbf·s²/ft.

Gallon (US) (gal): The US liquid gallon is the standard US volume unit for fuel, beverages, and household liquids.

Unit comparison

Dram per Tablespoon (US) vs Slug per Gallon (US)

  • Where used: Dram per Tablespoon (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and cooking and recipe formulation, while slug per gallon (US) is more common in aerospace, mechanical, and structural engineering calculations and US fuel economy, dairy, and beverage industries.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Tablespoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a large mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
