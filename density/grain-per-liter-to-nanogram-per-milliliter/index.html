Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Nanogram (ng): A nanogram is one billionth of a gram.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (64798.91) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Grain = 6.479891e-05 kg, Liter = 1 L, Nanogram = 0 kg, Milliliter = 0.001 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Grain per Liter vs Nanogram per Milliliter

  • Where used: Grain per Liter appears mainly in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology and virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail, while nanogram per milliliter is more common in genomics, forensic trace analysis, and environmental testing and medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.
  • Scale: Grain is a extremely small mass unit and Liter is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a extremely small mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Grain history — Grain-based mass standards appear in nearly every historical weight system; it survives today mainly for bullet and arrow-point mass and pharmaceutical dosing. Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per liter to nanogram per milliliter?

Multiply the grain per liter value by 64798.91, since 1 gr/L = 64798.91 ng/mL. This works because grain per liter and nanogram per milliliter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Liter in liters) to (Nanogram in kg / Milliliter in liters).

How do I convert nanogram per milliliter back to grain per liter?

Multiply by 1.543235835e-05, or equivalently divide by 64798.91, since 1 ng/mL = 1.543235835e-05 gr/L.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is liter used as the volume unit?

The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter. Liter shows up most often in virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per liter to nanogram per milliliter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and genomics, forensic trace analysis, and environmental testing on the other, or whenever a liter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in milliliter terms.
