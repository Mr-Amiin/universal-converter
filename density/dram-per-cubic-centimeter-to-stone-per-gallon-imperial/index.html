Why is dram used to measure mass here?

The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was long used by apothecaries for small weights. In this pairing it measures mass per cubic centimeter of volume.

About the Dram per Cubic Centimeter to Stone per Imperial gallon conversion

Dram per Cubic Centimeter (dr/cm3) is a density unit: An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 ounce. Cubic metric volume unit. Stone per Imperial gallon (st/imp gal) is a density unit: A stone is 14 pounds. An imperial gallon is exactly 4.54609 liters.

The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was long used by apothecaries for small weights. Centi- (c), from the Latin 'centum' (hundred), has been used since 1795 and is best known from the centimeter. The stone was standardized to 14 pounds in England in 1835 and remains in everyday use for body weight in the UK and Ireland.
