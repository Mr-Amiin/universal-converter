Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Fluid ounce (US) (fl oz): The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages.

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per fluid ounce (US) to dram per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply the grain per fluid ounce (US) value by 5.621818166, since 1 gr/fl oz = 5.621818166 dr/gal (imp). This works because grain per fluid ounce (US) and dram per gallon (imperial) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Fluid ounce (US) in liters) to (Dram in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters).

How do I convert dram per gallon (imperial) back to grain per fluid ounce (US)?

Multiply by 0.1778783964, or equivalently divide by 5.621818166, since 1 dr/gal (imp) = 0.1778783964 gr/fl oz.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is fluid ounce (US) used as the volume unit?

The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon, used for liquid measurement in cooking and retail beverages. Fluid ounce (US) shows up most often in beverage packaging, pharmacy, and recipe measurement.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per fluid ounce (US) to dram per gallon (imperial) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on the other, or whenever a fluid ounce (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in gallon (imperial) terms.
