Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Short ton (tn): The US short ton equals 2,000 pounds, the standard 'ton' used in the United States.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per gallon (imperial) to short ton per quart (US)?

Multiply the gram per gallon (imperial) value by 2.29466543e-07, since 1 g/gal (imp) = 2.29466543e-07 tn/qt. This works because gram per gallon (imperial) and short ton per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Gallon (imperial) in liters) to (Short ton in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert short ton per quart (US) back to gram per gallon (imperial)?

Multiply by 4357933.784, or equivalently divide by 2.29466543e-07, since 1 tn/qt = 4357933.784 g/gal (imp).

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is gallon (imperial) used as the volume unit?

The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon. Gallon (imperial) shows up most often in UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.

Why are such very large mass units used for density?

Short ton is very large on its own, but density conversions often need it because US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the gram per gallon (imperial) to short ton per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and US bulk material trade: gravel, grain, scrap metal, and freight on the other, or whenever a gallon (imperial)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
