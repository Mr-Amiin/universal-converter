Unit definitions

Hectogram (hg): A hectogram equals one hundred grams.

Barrel (oil) (bbl): The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products.

Tonne (t): The metric tonne equals 1,000 kilograms.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Hectogram per Barrel (oil) vs Tonne per Milliliter

  • Where used: Hectogram per Barrel (oil) appears mainly in European grocery retail and deli portioning and petroleum production, refining, and commodity trading, while tonne per milliliter is more common in international shipping, bulk commodities, and manufacturing and medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.
  • Scale: Hectogram is a everyday-scale mass unit and Barrel (oil) is a large volume unit, versus a very large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Hectogram history — The hectogram uses the metric 'hecto-' (x100) prefix and is a common everyday unit for food portions in several European countries.
