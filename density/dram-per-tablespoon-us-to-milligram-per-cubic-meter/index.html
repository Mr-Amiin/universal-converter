Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Milligram (mg): A milligram is one thousandth of a gram.

Cubic meter (m³): A cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume, equal to 1,000 liters.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per tablespoon (US) to milligram per cubic meter?

Multiply the dram per tablespoon (US) value by 119826427.3, since 1 dr/tbsp = 119826427.3 mg/m³. This works because dram per tablespoon (US) and milligram per cubic meter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters) to (Milligram in kg / Cubic meter in liters).

How do I convert milligram per cubic meter back to dram per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply by 8.345404452e-09, or equivalently divide by 119826427.3, since 1 mg/m³ = 8.345404452e-09 dr/tbsp.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is tablespoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons. Tablespoon (US) shows up most often in cooking and recipe formulation.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Milligram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per tablespoon (US) to milligram per cubic meter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry on the other, or whenever a tablespoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic meter terms.
