Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Milligram (mg): A milligram is one thousandth of a gram.

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Conversion accuracy

Unit comparison

Dram per Pint (US) vs Milligram per Milliliter

  • Where used: Dram per Pint (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while milligram per milliliter is more common in medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry and medical dosing, cosmetics, and laboratory chemistry.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a extremely small mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per pint (US) to milligram per milliliter?

Multiply the dram per pint (US) value by 3.744575854, since 1 dr/pt = 3.744575854 mg/mL. This works because dram per pint (US) and milligram per milliliter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Milligram in kg / Milliliter in liters).

How do I convert milligram per milliliter back to dram per pint (US)?

Multiply by 0.2670529425, or equivalently divide by 3.744575854, since 1 mg/mL = 0.2670529425 dr/pt.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon. Pint (US) shows up most often in US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Milligram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per pint (US) to milligram per milliliter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry on the other, or whenever a pint (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in milliliter terms.
