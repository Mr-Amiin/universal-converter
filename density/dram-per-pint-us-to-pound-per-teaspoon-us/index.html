Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Pint (US) (pt): The US liquid pint is 1/8 of a US gallon.

Pound (lb): The avoirdupois pound is the standard mass unit in the US customary and British imperial systems.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Conversion accuracy

This factor (4.069010417e-05) is computed directly from each unit's exact SI definition (Dram = 0.001771845195 kg, Pint (US) = 0.473176473 L, Pound = 0.45359237 kg, Teaspoon (US) = 0.004928921594 L), so results stay consistent to the precision shown regardless of which direction you convert.

Unit comparison

Dram per Pint (US) vs Pound per Teaspoon (US)

  • Where used: Dram per Pint (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and US beverage retail (beer, milk) and cooking, while pound per teaspoon (US) is more common in US retail, shipping, construction, and everyday commerce and cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Pint (US) is a everyday-scale volume unit, versus a large mass unit and small volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Pint (US) history — Inherited from English liquid-measure tradition, the US pint diverged slightly from the UK pint after independence.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per pint (US) to pound per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply the dram per pint (US) value by 4.069010417e-05, since 1 dr/pt = 4.069010417e-05 lb/tsp. This works because dram per pint (US) and pound per teaspoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Pint (US) in liters) to (Pound in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert pound per teaspoon (US) back to dram per pint (US)?

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

Why is pint (US) used as the volume unit?

Where is the dram per pint (US) to pound per teaspoon (US) conversion used?
