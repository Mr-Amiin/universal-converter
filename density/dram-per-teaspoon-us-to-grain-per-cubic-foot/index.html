Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic foot (ft³): A cubic foot is the volume of a cube with 1-foot sides.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per teaspoon (US) to grain per cubic foot?

Multiply the dram per teaspoon (US) value by 157090.9091, since 1 dr/tsp = 157090.9091 gr/ft³. This works because dram per teaspoon (US) and grain per cubic foot both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters) to (Grain in kg / Cubic foot in liters).

How do I convert grain per cubic foot back to dram per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply by 6.365740741e-06, or equivalently divide by 157090.9091, since 1 gr/ft³ = 6.365740741e-06 dr/tsp.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is teaspoon (US) used as the volume unit?

The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon. Teaspoon (US) shows up most often in cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per teaspoon (US) to grain per cubic foot conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on the other, or whenever a teaspoon (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic foot terms.
