Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Ounce (oz): The avoirdupois ounce is 1/16 of a pound, the everyday ounce used for food and retail goods.

Gallon (imperial) (gal (imp)): The imperial gallon is the UK standard liquid volume unit, larger than the US gallon.

Unit comparison

Dram per Teaspoon (US) vs Ounce per Gallon (imperial)

  • Where used: Dram per Teaspoon (US) appears mainly in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing and cooking, baking, and over-the-counter medicine dosing, while ounce per gallon (imperial) is more common in US food packaging, retail, and postal weight limits and UK fuel economy figures and Commonwealth liquid trade.
  • Scale: Dram is a small mass unit and Teaspoon (US) is a small volume unit, versus a everyday-scale mass unit and everyday-scale volume unit on the other side, which is why the numeric values can look very different for the same physical density.
  • Precision: Both units convert exactly through their defined SI equivalents, so no rounding is introduced by the unit choice itself — only by how many digits you choose to keep in the result.
  • History: Dram history — The dram descends from the ancient Greek drachma and was folded into the English avoirdupois system used for everyday trade goods. Teaspoon (US) history — Standardized for home cooking measurement in the US alongside the tablespoon and cup.

How do you convert dram per teaspoon (US) to ounce per gallon (imperial)?

How do I convert ounce per gallon (imperial) back to dram per teaspoon (US)?

Why is dram used to measure mass here?
