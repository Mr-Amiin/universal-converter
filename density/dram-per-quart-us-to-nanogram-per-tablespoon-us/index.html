Unit definitions

Dram (dr): An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Nanogram (ng): A nanogram is one billionth of a gram.

Tablespoon (US) (tbsp): The US tablespoon is a standard cooking-measurement volume unit equal to 3 US teaspoons.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert dram per quart (US) to nanogram per tablespoon (US)?

Multiply the dram per quart (US) value by 27685081.18, since 1 dr/qt = 27685081.18 ng/tbsp. This works because dram per quart (US) and nanogram per tablespoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Dram in kg / Quart (US) in liters) to (Nanogram in kg / Tablespoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert nanogram per tablespoon (US) back to dram per quart (US)?

Multiply by 3.612053704e-08, or equivalently divide by 27685081.18, since 1 ng/tbsp = 3.612053704e-08 dr/qt.

Why is dram used to measure mass here?

An avoirdupois dram is 1/16 of an ounce, or 1/256 of a pound. It is common in pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing, which is why densities are often reported using dram.

Why is quart (US) used as the volume unit?

The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints. Quart (US) shows up most often in US dairy, cooking, and automotive fluid packaging.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Nanogram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because genomics, forensic trace analysis, and environmental testing routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the dram per quart (US) to nanogram per tablespoon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between pharmacy compounding and small-parts manufacturing on one side and genomics, forensic trace analysis, and environmental testing on the other, or whenever a quart (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in tablespoon (US) terms.
