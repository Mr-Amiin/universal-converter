Unit definitions

Gram (g): The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram.

Liter (L): The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter.

Picogram (pg): A picogram is one trillionth of a gram.

Teaspoon (US) (tsp): The US teaspoon is a small standard cooking-measurement volume unit, 1/3 of a US tablespoon.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert gram per liter to picogram per teaspoon (US)?

Multiply the gram per liter value by 4928921594, since 1 g/L = 4928921594 pg/tsp. This works because gram per liter and picogram per teaspoon (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Gram in kg / Liter in liters) to (Picogram in kg / Teaspoon (US) in liters).

How do I convert picogram per teaspoon (US) back to gram per liter?

Multiply by 0, or equivalently divide by 4928921594, since 1 pg/tsp = 0 g/L.

Why is gram used to measure mass here?

The gram is the base metric unit of mass, defined as one thousandth of a kilogram. It is common in cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging, which is why densities are often reported using gram.

Why is liter used as the volume unit?

The liter is the standard metric unit of volume, equal to one cubic decimeter. Liter shows up most often in virtually all metric-system science, industry, and retail.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Picogram is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the gram per liter to picogram per teaspoon (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between cooking, chemistry, and retail food packaging on one side and DNA/RNA quantification and ultra-trace analytical chemistry on the other, or whenever a liter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in teaspoon (US) terms.
