Formula & worked example

mg/cup = ng/tbsp × 1.6e-05

ng/tbsp = mg/cup ÷ 1.6e-05 (or × 62500)

For example, lead has a density of about 11340 kg/m³, which is roughly 1.67682e+11 ng/tbsp. Multiplying by the conversion factor: 1.67682e+11 × 1.6e-05 = 2682911. So 1.67682e+11 ng/tbsp of lead is equal to 2682911 mg/cup.

How do I convert milligram per cup back to nanogram per tablespoon?

Why is the nanogram per tablespoon to milligram per cup conversion factor so small?

Milligram per Cup (mg/cup) is a density unit: Milligram is defined as milligram is 0.001 gram units. Cup is defined as a us customary cup is 236.5882365 milliliters. Use this calculator for imperial and US customary engineering, construction, and shipping calculations, or any workflow that needs an accurate nanogram per tablespoon-to-milligram per cup density figure.

Nanogram per Tablespoon vs Milligram per Cup

Milligram per Cup is the larger density unit here: it takes 62500 units of nanogram per tablespoon to equal one milligram per cup. Nanogram per Tablespoon is built from nanogram (Nanogram is 1e-9 gram units.) divided by tablespoon (A US tablespoon is half a US fluid ounce.), while Milligram per Cup is built from milligram (Milligram is 0.001 gram units.) divided by cup (A US customary cup is 236.5882365 milliliters.). Both are imperial/US customary combinations, typically used in imperial and US customary engineering, construction, and shipping calculations.

Unit background

Nanogram: Nanogram is 1e-9 gram units. Tablespoon: A US tablespoon is half a US fluid ounce.

Milligram: Milligram is 0.001 gram units. Cup: A US customary cup is 236.5882365 milliliters.

Density as a quantity (mass divided by volume) dates to Archimedes' work on buoyancy in the 3rd century BCE. Modern unit systems pair a mass unit with a volume unit to express it, which is why density has so many valid unit combinations — this page covers one specific pairing.
