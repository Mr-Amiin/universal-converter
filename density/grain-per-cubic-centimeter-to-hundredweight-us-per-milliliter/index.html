Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Hundredweight (short) (cwt (short)): The US (short) hundredweight equals 100 pounds and underlies the short ton (2,000 lb).

Milliliter (mL): A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic centimeter to hundredweight (short) per milliliter?

Multiply the grain per cubic centimeter value by 1.428571429e-06, since 1 gr/cm³ = 1.428571429e-06 cwt (short)/mL. This works because grain per cubic centimeter and hundredweight (short) per milliliter both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters) to (Hundredweight (short) in kg / Milliliter in liters).

How do I convert hundredweight (short) per milliliter back to grain per cubic centimeter?

Multiply by 700000, or equivalently divide by 1.428571429e-06, since 1 cwt (short)/mL = 700000 gr/cm³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic centimeter used as the volume unit?

A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter. Cubic centimeter shows up most often in medical dosing, engine displacement, and laboratory science.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic centimeter to hundredweight (short) per milliliter conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and US grain, livestock, and steel pricing on the other, or whenever a cubic centimeter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in milliliter terms.
