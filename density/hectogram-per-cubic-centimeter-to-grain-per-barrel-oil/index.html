Unit definitions

Hectogram (hg): A hectogram equals one hundred grams.

Cubic centimeter (cm³): A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter.

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Barrel (oil) (bbl): The oil barrel is fixed at 42 US gallons and is the standard unit for pricing crude oil and petroleum products.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert hectogram per cubic centimeter to grain per barrel (oil)?

Multiply the hectogram per cubic centimeter value by 245354890.9, since 1 hg/cm³ = 245354890.9 gr/bbl. This works because hectogram per cubic centimeter and grain per barrel (oil) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Hectogram in kg / Cubic centimeter in liters) to (Grain in kg / Barrel (oil) in liters).

How do I convert grain per barrel (oil) back to hectogram per cubic centimeter?

Multiply by 4.07572882e-09, or equivalently divide by 245354890.9, since 1 gr/bbl = 4.07572882e-09 hg/cm³.

Why is hectogram used to measure mass here?

A hectogram equals one hundred grams. It is common in European grocery retail and deli portioning, which is why densities are often reported using hectogram.

Why is cubic centimeter used as the volume unit?

A cubic centimeter is the volume of a 1 cm cube and is numerically identical to a milliliter. Cubic centimeter shows up most often in medical dosing, engine displacement, and laboratory science.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the hectogram per cubic centimeter to grain per barrel (oil) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between European grocery retail and deli portioning on one side and ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on the other, or whenever a cubic centimeter-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in barrel (oil) terms.
