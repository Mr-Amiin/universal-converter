Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cubic inch (in³): A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides.

Milligram (mg): A milligram is one thousandth of a gram.

Quart (US) (qt): The US liquid quart is 1/4 of a US gallon, or two US pints.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cubic inch to milligram per quart (US)?

Multiply the grain per cubic inch value by 3742.137053, since 1 gr/in³ = 3742.137053 mg/qt. This works because grain per cubic inch and milligram per quart (US) both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cubic inch in liters) to (Milligram in kg / Quart (US) in liters).

How do I convert milligram per quart (US) back to grain per cubic inch?

Multiply by 0.0002672269845, or equivalently divide by 3742.137053, since 1 mg/qt = 0.0002672269845 gr/in³.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cubic inch used as the volume unit?

A cubic inch is the volume of a cube with 1-inch sides. Cubic inch shows up most often in engine displacement and small mechanical part tolerances.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cubic inch to milligram per quart (US) conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and medicine dosing, nutrition labeling, and chemistry on the other, or whenever a cubic inch-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in quart (US) terms.
