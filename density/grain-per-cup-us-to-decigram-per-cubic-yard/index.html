Unit definitions

Grain (gr): A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat.

Cup (US) (cup): The US cup is a customary volume unit used mainly in cooking and recipes.

Decigram (dg): A decigram is one tenth of a gram.

Cubic yard (yd³): A cubic yard is the volume of a cube with 1-yard sides, commonly used for bulk materials like concrete and soil.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert grain per cup (US) to decigram per cubic yard?

Multiply the grain per cup (US) value by 2094.031477, since 1 gr/cup = 2094.031477 dg/yd³. This works because grain per cup (US) and decigram per cubic yard both describe mass per volume, so the conversion factor is simply the ratio of (Grain in kg / Cup (US) in liters) to (Decigram in kg / Cubic yard in liters).

How do I convert decigram per cubic yard back to grain per cup (US)?

Multiply by 0.0004775477403, or equivalently divide by 2094.031477, since 1 dg/yd³ = 0.0004775477403 gr/cup.

Why is grain used to measure mass here?

A grain is the smallest common avoirdupois mass unit, originally based on the mass of a single grain of barley or wheat. It is common in ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology, which is why densities are often reported using grain.

Why is cup (US) used as the volume unit?

The US cup is a customary volume unit used mainly in cooking and recipes. Cup (US) shows up most often in cooking, baking, and nutrition labeling.

Why are such extremely small mass units used for density?

Grain is extremely small on its own, but density conversions often need it because ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology routinely deal with concentrations or masses at that scale, and using a matched unit avoids writing out long strings of zeros.

Where is the grain per cup (US) to decigram per cubic yard conversion used?

It comes up most often when moving between ammunition reloading, archery, and pharmacology on one side and pharmaceutical and analytical chemistry weighing on the other, or whenever a cup (US)-based recipe or spec needs to be re-expressed in cubic yard terms.
