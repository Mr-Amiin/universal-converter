Unit definitions

Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Microliter per Decades (uL/decade)
The microliter is equal to 10^-6 liters (a millionth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'micro-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Microliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Fortnight unit represents 2.609 × 10¹⁴ times more volume-per-time than one Microliter per Decades unit.

Megaliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Microliter per Decades is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

A flow of one megaliter every fortnight is the same physical flow as 2.60887 × 10¹⁴ microliter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/fn works out to 2.609 × 10¹⁵ uL/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Microliter: The 'micro-' prefix (u, 10^-6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
