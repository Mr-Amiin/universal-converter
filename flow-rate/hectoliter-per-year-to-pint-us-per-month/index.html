Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Years (hL/yr)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Pint per Months (pt/mo)
The pint us is 473.18 mL, 1/8 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Pint per Months (US customary)

One Hectoliter per Years unit represents 17.61147 times more volume-per-time than one Pint per Months unit.

Hectoliter per Years is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Pint per Months is commonly used in US beverage service and small-batch process rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one hectoliter every year is the same physical flow as 17.6114701572 pint(s) every month, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Pint us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage as the US cup and quart.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
