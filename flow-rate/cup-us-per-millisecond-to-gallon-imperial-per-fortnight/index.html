Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is cups per millisecond (cup/ms). The Cup (cup) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is imperial gallons per fortnight (imp gal/fn). The Imperial Gallon (imp gal) is a unit of volume. The Fortnight (fn) is a unit of time.

How do I convert cups per millisecond to imperial gallons per fortnight?

Multiply the cups per millisecond value by 6.29502e+7 to get imperial gallons per fortnight. For example, 10 cup/ms × 6.29502e+7 = 6.29502e+8 imp gal/fn.

Is the cups per millisecond to imperial gallons per fortnight conversion exact?

It's exact under the SI-based volume and time definitions used here, since both units convert to cubic meters per second through fixed, non-rounded factors.

Where is this conversion commonly used?

Flow rate conversions like this are used in plumbing, HVAC, irrigation, chemical processing, fuel and water metering, and engineering flow calculations.

Can I convert imperial gallons per fortnight back to cups per millisecond?

Yes. Divide the imperial gallons per fortnight value by 6.29502e+7 (or multiply by 1.58856e-8) to get cups per millisecond.

About this conversion

This page converts cups per millisecond (cup/ms) into imperial gallons per fortnight (imp gal/fn), two units of volumetric flow rate.

The Cup (cup) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

The Imperial Gallon (imp gal) is a unit of volume. The Fortnight (fn) is a unit of time.

The formula is imp gal/fn = cup/ms × 6.29502e+7, based on the fixed relationship between these two flow rate units.

Flow rate is calculated as a volume unit divided by a time unit, so every combination of a volume unit and a time unit forms a valid, exact flow rate unit.
