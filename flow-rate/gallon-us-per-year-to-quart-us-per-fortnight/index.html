Unit definitions

Gallon per Years (gal/yr)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Quart per Fortnight (qt/fn)
The quart us is 946.35 mL, 1/4 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Years (US customary) vs Quart per Fortnight (US customary)

One Quart per Fortnight unit represents 6.522187 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Years unit.

Gallon per Years is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Quart per Fortnight is commonly used in US food and beverage packaging rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Quart us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage inherited from English wine-measure standards.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
