Unit definitions

Gallon per Decades (gal/decade)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Pint per Decades (pt/decade)
The pint us is 473.18 mL, 1/8 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Decades (US customary) vs Pint per Decades (US customary)

One Gallon per Decades unit represents 8 times more volume-per-time than one Pint per Decades unit.

Gallon per Decades is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Pint per Decades is commonly used in US beverage service and small-batch process rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Decades (gal/decade) to Pint per Decades (pt/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every decade is the same physical flow as 8 pint(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/decade works out to 80 pt/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Pint us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage as the US cup and quart.
