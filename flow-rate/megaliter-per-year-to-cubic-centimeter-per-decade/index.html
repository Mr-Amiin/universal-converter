Unit history

Megaliter per Years: The liter dates to the 1795 French metric system, originally defined as the volume of one kilogram of water. The year is based on Earth's orbital period; the modern Gregorian calendar year was adopted in 1582.

Cubic Centimeter per Decades: The 'Cubic' prefix was from the Latin 'centum' (hundred); an original 1795 metric prefix. Decade comes from the Greek 'deka' (ten), used generally for any ten-year span.

About this conversion

This page converts Megaliter per Years (ML/yr) to Cubic Centimeter per Decades (cm3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every year is the same physical flow as 1 cubic centimeter(s) every decade. Flows at this scale are typical of municipal water systems, industrial pumps and pipelines, irrigation networks, and process-engineering calculations, where year- and decade-based reporting periods are both common depending on the equipment and region.

For example, an industrial line or irrigation system running at 150 ML/yr moves the same volume as 1.5 × 10^12 cm3/decade — comparable to municipal water-supply or agricultural pumping rates.
