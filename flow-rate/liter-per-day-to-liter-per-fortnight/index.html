Unit definitions

Liter per Days (L/d)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Liter per Fortnight (L/fn)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Liter per Days (L/d) to Liter per Fortnight (L/fn) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one liter every day is the same physical flow as 14 liter(s) every fortnight, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering: a flow reported as 10 L/d works out to 140 L/fn, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
