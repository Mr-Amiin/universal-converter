Unit definitions

Gallon per Months (gal/mo)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Cubic inch per Weeks (in3/wk)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Months (US customary) vs Cubic inch per Weeks (Imperial/US engineering)

One Gallon per Months unit represents 53.126348 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic inch per Weeks unit.

Gallon per Months is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic inch per Weeks is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
