Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Minutes (kL/min)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cubic Kilometer per Minutes (km3/min)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Kilometer per Minutes (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Minutes unit represents 1000000000 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Minutes unit.

Kiloliter per Minutes is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic Kilometer per Minutes is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
