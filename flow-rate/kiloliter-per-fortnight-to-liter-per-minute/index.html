Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Fortnight (kL/fn)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Liter per Minutes (L/min)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Liter per Minutes (Metric)

One Liter per Minutes unit represents 20.16 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Fortnight unit.

Kiloliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Liter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
