Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Seconds (hL/s)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Tablespoon per Seconds (tbsp/s)
The tablespoon us is 14.79 mL, equal to 3 US teaspoons or 1/2 US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Seconds (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Tablespoon per Seconds (Cooking (US))

One Hectoliter per Seconds unit represents 6762.80454 times more volume-per-time than one Tablespoon per Seconds unit.

Hectoliter per Seconds is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Tablespoon per Seconds is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one hectoliter every second is the same physical flow as 6762.80454037 tablespoon(s) every second, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Tablespoon us: Standardized alongside the rest of the US customary cooking-measure family in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
