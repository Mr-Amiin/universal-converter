Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (imp gal/ms)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Bushel per Millisecond (bu/ms)
The bushel us is a customary dry-volume unit equal to 2,150.42 cubic inches (about 35.24 liters). This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (Imperial) vs Bushel per Millisecond (Agriculture)

One Bushel per Millisecond unit represents 7.751512 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Millisecond unit.

Imperial gallon per Millisecond is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Bushel per Millisecond is commonly used in grain elevator throughput, harvest yield rates, and crop-drying airflow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Bushel us: The US bushel descends from the medieval English Winchester bushel, a grain-measuring standard formalized in the 15th century. The United States kept the Winchester bushel even after England adopted the larger Imperial bushel in 1824, so US and Imperial bushels differ slightly to this day.
