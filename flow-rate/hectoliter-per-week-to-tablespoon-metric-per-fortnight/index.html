Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Weeks (hL/wk)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Metric tablespoon per Fortnight (metric tbsp/fn)
The tablespoon metric is 15 mL, a rounded, decimal-friendly cooking measure. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Weeks (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Metric tablespoon per Fortnight (Cooking (metric))

One Hectoliter per Weeks unit represents 13333.333333 times more volume-per-time than one Metric tablespoon per Fortnight unit.

Hectoliter per Weeks is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Metric tablespoon per Fortnight is commonly used in recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Tablespoon metric: Adopted during 20th-century metrication as a simple 15 mL figure, close enough to the older household tablespoon to substitute directly in most recipes.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
