Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Months (hL/mo)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Quart per Years (qt/yr)
The quart us is 946.35 mL, 1/4 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Quart per Years (US customary)

One Hectoliter per Months unit represents 1268.025851 times more volume-per-time than one Quart per Years unit.

Hectoliter per Months is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Quart per Years is commonly used in US food and beverage packaging rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one hectoliter every month is the same physical flow as 1268.02585132 quart(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Quart us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage inherited from English wine-measure standards.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
