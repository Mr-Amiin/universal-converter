Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Millisecond (hL/ms)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Milliliter per Days (mL/d)
The milliliter is equal to 10^-3 liters (a thousandth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'milli-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Millisecond (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Milliliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Hectoliter per Millisecond unit represents 8.64 × 10¹² times more volume-per-time than one Milliliter per Days unit.

Hectoliter per Millisecond is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Milliliter per Days is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Hectoliter per Millisecond (hL/ms) to Milliliter per Days (mL/d) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one hectoliter every millisecond is the same physical flow as 8.64 × 10¹² milliliter(s) every day, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering: a flow reported as 10 hL/ms works out to 8.64 × 10¹³ mL/d, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Milliliter: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.
