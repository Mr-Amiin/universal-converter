Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Years (kL/yr)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Imperial pint per Minutes (imp pt/min)
The pint imperial is 568.26 mL, 1/8 of an imperial gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial pint per Minutes (Imperial)

One Imperial pint per Minutes unit represents 298.87655 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Years unit.

Kiloliter per Years is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial pint per Minutes is commonly used in UK beverage service and dispensing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

A flow of one kiloliter every year is the same physical flow as 0.00334586303467 imperial pint(s) every minute, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Pint imperial: Fixed by the 1824 Weights and Measures Act; still the legally mandated draft beer and cider measure in UK pubs.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
