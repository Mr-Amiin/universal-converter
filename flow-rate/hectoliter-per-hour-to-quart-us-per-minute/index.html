Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Hours (hL/h)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Quart per Minutes (qt/min)
The quart us is 946.35 mL, 1/4 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Hours (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Quart per Minutes (US customary)

One Hectoliter per Hours unit represents 1.761147 times more volume-per-time than one Quart per Minutes unit.

Hectoliter per Hours is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Quart per Minutes is commonly used in US food and beverage packaging rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Quart us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage inherited from English wine-measure standards.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
