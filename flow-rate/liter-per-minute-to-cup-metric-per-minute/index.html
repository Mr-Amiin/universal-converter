Unit definitions

Liter per Minutes (L/min)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Metric cup per Minutes (metric cup/min)
The cup metric is a rounded serving unit equal to exactly 250 milliliters. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Liter per Minutes (Metric) vs Metric cup per Minutes (Cooking (metric))

One Liter per Minutes unit represents 4 times more volume-per-time than one Metric cup per Minutes unit.

Liter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Metric cup per Minutes is commonly used in recipe scaling, kitchen dispensing rates, and beverage service.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one liter every minute is the same physical flow as 4 metric cup(s) every minute, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cup metric: The metric cup was standardized at 250 mL during 20th-century metrication in Australia, New Zealand, and other Commonwealth countries, replacing older imperial cup sizes that varied by region.
