Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Years (hL/yr)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Imperial gallon per Decades (imp gal/decade)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial gallon per Decades (Imperial)

One Hectoliter per Years unit represents 219.969248 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Decades unit.

Hectoliter per Years is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Imperial gallon per Decades is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one hectoliter every year is the same physical flow as 219.969248299 imperial gallon(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
