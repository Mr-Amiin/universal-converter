Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Months (hL/mo)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Metric teaspoon per Decades (metric tsp/decade)
The teaspoon metric is 5 mL, the standard small dosing unit in medicine and cooking. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Metric teaspoon per Decades (Cooking (metric))

One Hectoliter per Months unit represents 2,400,000 times more volume-per-time than one Metric teaspoon per Decades unit.

Hectoliter per Months is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Metric teaspoon per Decades is commonly used in medicine dosing rates and small-scale recipe scaling.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Teaspoon metric: 5 mL was formally adopted as the standard medicinal teaspoon dose by international pharmacopeias in the 20th century, replacing the inconsistent traditional household teaspoon.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
