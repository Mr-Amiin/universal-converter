Unit definitions

Gallon per Decades (gal/decade)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Cubic inch per Fortnight (in3/fn)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Decades (US customary) vs Cubic inch per Fortnight (Imperial/US engineering)

One Cubic inch per Fortnight unit represents 1.129383 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Decades unit.

Gallon per Decades is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic inch per Fortnight is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
