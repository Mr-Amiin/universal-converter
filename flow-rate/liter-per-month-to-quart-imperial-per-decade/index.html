Unit definitions

Liter per Months (L/mo)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Imperial quart per Decades (imp qt/decade)
The quart imperial is 1.137 liters, 1/4 of an imperial gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Liter per Months (Metric) vs Imperial quart per Decades (Imperial)

One Liter per Months unit represents 105.585239 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial quart per Decades unit.

Liter per Months is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Imperial quart per Decades is commonly used in UK bulk-liquid packaging rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Quart imperial: Part of the 1824 British Imperial system alongside the pint and gallon.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
