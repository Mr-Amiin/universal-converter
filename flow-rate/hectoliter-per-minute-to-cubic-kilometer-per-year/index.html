Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Minutes (hL/min)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cubic Kilometer per Years (km3/yr)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Kilometer per Years (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Years unit represents 19013.243104 times more volume-per-time than one Hectoliter per Minutes unit.

Hectoliter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic Kilometer per Years is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
