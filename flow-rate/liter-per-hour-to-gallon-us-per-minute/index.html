Unit definitions

Liter per Hours (L/h)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Gallon per Minutes (gal/min)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Liter per Hours (Metric) vs Gallon per Minutes (US customary)

One Gallon per Minutes unit represents 227.124707 times more volume-per-time than one Liter per Hours unit.

Liter per Hours is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Gallon per Minutes is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one liter every hour is the same physical flow as 0.0044028675393 gallon(s) every minute, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
