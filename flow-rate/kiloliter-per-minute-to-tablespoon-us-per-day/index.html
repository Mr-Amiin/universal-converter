Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Minutes (kL/min)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Tablespoon per Days (tbsp/d)
The tablespoon us is 14.79 mL, equal to 3 US teaspoons or 1/2 US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Tablespoon per Days (Cooking (US))

One Kiloliter per Minutes unit represents 97384385.381308 times more volume-per-time than one Tablespoon per Days unit.

Kiloliter per Minutes is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Tablespoon per Days is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Tablespoon us: Standardized alongside the rest of the US customary cooking-measure family in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.
