Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Hours (kL/h)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks (imp fl oz/wk)
The fluid ounce imperial is 28.41 mL, 1/20 of an imperial pint. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Hours (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks (Imperial)

One Kiloliter per Hours unit represents 5912773.394279 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks unit.

Kiloliter per Hours is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks is commonly used in pharmacy dosing and UK beverage-service rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Fluid ounce imperial: Fixed by the UK Weights and Measures Act of 1824, which unified Britain's competing gallon and ounce definitions.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
