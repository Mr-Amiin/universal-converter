Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Days (imp gal/d)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Gallon per Days (gal/d)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Days (Imperial) vs Gallon per Days (US customary)

One Imperial gallon per Days unit represents 1.20095 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Days unit.

Imperial gallon per Days is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Gallon per Days is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.
