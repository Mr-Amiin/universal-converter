Unit definitions

Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Oil barrel per Minutes (bbl/min)
The barrel oil is a unit of volume standardized at exactly 42 US gallons (158.987 liters). This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Oil barrel per Minutes (Oil and gas)

One Oil barrel per Minutes unit represents 836.192406 times more volume-per-time than one Megaliter per Decades unit.

Megaliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Oil barrel per Minutes is commonly used in crude oil and refined-product flow measurement, pipeline throughput, and well production rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Barrel oil: The 42-gallon barrel became the informal US standard during the 1860s Pennsylvania oil boom. Early producers shipped crude in used wine, whiskey, and fish barrels of inconsistent sizes, so in 1866 the Petroleum Producers' Association settled on 42 gallons -- partly to cover spillage and evaporation losses -- and the figure stuck even after oil moved to pipelines and tankers.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
