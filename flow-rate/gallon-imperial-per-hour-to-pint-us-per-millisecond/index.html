Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Pint per Millisecond (pt/ms)
The pint us is 473.18 mL, 1/8 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Hours (Imperial) vs Pint per Millisecond (US customary)

One Pint per Millisecond unit represents 374703.383083 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Hours unit.

Imperial gallon per Hours is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Pint per Millisecond is commonly used in US beverage service and small-batch process rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h) to Pint per Millisecond (pt/ms) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one imperial gallon every hour is the same physical flow as 2.66877761223e-06 pint(s) every millisecond, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow: a flow reported as 10 imp gal/h works out to 0.000027 pt/ms, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Pint us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage as the US cup and quart.

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
