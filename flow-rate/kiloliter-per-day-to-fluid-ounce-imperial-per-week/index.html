Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Days (kL/d)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks (imp fl oz/wk)
The fluid ounce imperial is 28.41 mL, 1/20 of an imperial pint. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks (Imperial)

One Kiloliter per Days unit represents 246365.558095 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks unit.

Kiloliter per Days is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial fluid ounce per Weeks is commonly used in pharmacy dosing and UK beverage-service rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Fluid ounce imperial: Fixed by the UK Weights and Measures Act of 1824, which unified Britain's competing gallon and ounce definitions.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
