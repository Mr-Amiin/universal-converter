Unit definitions

Gallon per Minutes (gal/min)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Tablespoon per Millisecond (tbsp/ms)
The tablespoon us is 14.79 mL, equal to 3 US teaspoons or 1/2 US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Minutes (US customary) vs Tablespoon per Millisecond (Cooking (US))

One Tablespoon per Millisecond unit represents 234.375 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Minutes unit.

Gallon per Minutes is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Tablespoon per Millisecond is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Minutes (gal/min) to Tablespoon per Millisecond (tbsp/ms) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every minute is the same physical flow as 0.00426666666667 tablespoon(s) every millisecond, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/min works out to 0.042667 tbsp/ms, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Tablespoon us: Standardized alongside the rest of the US customary cooking-measure family in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
