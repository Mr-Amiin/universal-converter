Unit definitions

Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Centiliter per Years (cL/yr)
The centiliter is equal to 10^-2 liters (a hundredth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'centi-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Centiliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Fortnight unit represents 2.609 × 10⁹ times more volume-per-time than one Centiliter per Years unit.

Megaliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Centiliter per Years is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn) to Centiliter per Years (cL/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every fortnight is the same physical flow as 2.60887 × 10⁹ centiliter(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/fn works out to 2.609 × 10¹⁰ cL/yr, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Centiliter: The 'centi-' prefix (c, 10^-2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
