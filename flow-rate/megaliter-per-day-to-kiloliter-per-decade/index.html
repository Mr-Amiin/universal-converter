Unit definitions

Megaliter per Days (ML/d)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Kiloliter per Decades (kL/decade)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Kiloliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Days unit represents 3,652,425 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Decades unit.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Megaliter per Days (ML/d) to Kiloliter per Decades (kL/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every day is the same physical flow as 3652425 kiloliter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/d works out to 36524250 kL/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
