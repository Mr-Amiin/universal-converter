Unit definitions

Gallon per Minutes (gal/min)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cubic Meter per Months (m3/mo)
The cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume: the space occupied by a cube measuring 1 meter on each edge. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Minutes (US customary) vs Cubic Meter per Months (Metric SI)

One Gallon per Minutes unit represents 165.911192 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Meter per Months unit.

Gallon per Minutes is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic Meter per Months is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one gallon every minute is the same physical flow as 165.911191622 cubic meter(s) every month, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/min works out to 1659.111916 m3/mo, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cubic meter: As the natural volume unit of the metric system established in 1799, the cubic meter is the reference unit engineers and hydrologists use for river discharge, reservoir capacity, and industrial process design (m^3/s is the SI-coherent flow-rate unit).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
