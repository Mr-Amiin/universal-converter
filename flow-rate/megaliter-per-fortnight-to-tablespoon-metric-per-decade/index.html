Unit definitions

Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Metric tablespoon per Decades (metric tbsp/decade)
The tablespoon metric is 15 mL, a rounded, decimal-friendly cooking measure. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Metric tablespoon per Decades (Cooking (metric))

One Megaliter per Fortnight unit represents 1.739 × 10¹⁰ times more volume-per-time than one Metric tablespoon per Decades unit.

Megaliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Metric tablespoon per Decades is commonly used in recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

A flow of one megaliter every fortnight is the same physical flow as 1.73925 × 10¹⁰ metric tablespoon(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/fn works out to 1.739 × 10¹¹ metric tbsp/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Tablespoon metric: Adopted during 20th-century metrication as a simple 15 mL figure, close enough to the older household tablespoon to substitute directly in most recipes.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
