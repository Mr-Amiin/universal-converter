Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Months (imp gal/mo)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Fluid ounce per Minutes (fl oz/min)
The fluid ounce us is 29.57 mL, 1/16 of a US pint. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Months (Imperial) vs Fluid ounce per Minutes (US customary)

One Fluid ounce per Minutes unit represents 285.120001 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Months unit.

Imperial gallon per Months is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Fluid ounce per Minutes is commonly used in beverage dispensing and US pharmacy dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Fluid ounce us: Derived from the old English wine gallon the American colonies kept after independence, later fixed under the 231-cubic-inch US gallon.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
