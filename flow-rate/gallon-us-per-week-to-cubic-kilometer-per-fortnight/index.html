Unit definitions

Gallon per Weeks (gal/wk)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight (km3/fn)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Weeks (US customary) vs Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight unit represents 1.321 × 10¹¹ times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Weeks unit.

Gallon per Weeks is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
