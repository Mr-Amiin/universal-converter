Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Years (imp gal/yr)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Cubic Centimeter per Decades (cm3/decade)
The cubic centimeter is the volume of a cube measuring one centimeter (10^-2 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^-6 cubic meters, not merely 10^-2. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Years (Imperial) vs Cubic Centimeter per Decades (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Imperial gallon per Years unit represents 45460.9 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Centimeter per Decades unit.

Imperial gallon per Years is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Cubic Centimeter per Decades is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Imperial gallon per Years (imp gal/yr) to Cubic Centimeter per Decades (cm3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one imperial gallon every year is the same physical flow as 45460.9 cubic centimeter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow: a flow reported as 10 imp gal/yr works out to 454,609 cm3/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Cubic centimeter: The 'centi-' prefix (c, 10^-2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
