From definition

Metric teaspoon per Fortnight (metric tsp/fn) is a volumetric flow rate unit that measures how much volume, expressed in metric teaspoon(s), passes a given point every fortnight. It is used to compare flow across pipes, pumps, channels, and instruments that report volume moved over that particular time interval.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Metric teaspoon per Fortnight (metric tsp/fn) to Fluid ounce per Year (fl oz/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one metric teaspoon every fortnight is the same physical flow as 4.41082792381 fluid ounce(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units. For example, a kitchen faucet or garden hose running at 1 metric tsp/fn is moving flow equivalent to 4.41082792381 fl oz/yr — handy when one spec sheet lists flow in metric teaspoon per fortnight and another lists it in fluid ounce per year.

Metric flow units are built from SI volume and time units established under the metric system, while US customary flow units combine older trade-derived volume measures (like the gallon or cup) with standard time units, which is why the same physical flow rate looks very different when expressed in each system.
