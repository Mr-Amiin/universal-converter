Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Months (kL/mo)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Microliter per Years (uL/yr)
The microliter is equal to 10^-6 liters (a millionth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'micro-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Microliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Kiloliter per Months unit represents 1.2 × 10¹⁰ times more volume-per-time than one Microliter per Years unit.

Kiloliter per Months is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Microliter per Years is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Kiloliter per Months (kL/mo) to Microliter per Years (uL/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one kiloliter every month is the same physical flow as 1.2 × 10¹⁰ microliter(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 kL/mo works out to 1.2 × 10¹¹ uL/yr, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Microliter: The 'micro-' prefix (u, 10^-6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
