Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Seconds (kL/s)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Cup per Minutes (cup/min)
The cup us is 8 US fluid ounces (about 236.6 mL), the standard US recipe-measuring unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Seconds (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cup per Minutes (Cooking (US))

One Kiloliter per Seconds unit represents 253605.170264 times more volume-per-time than one Cup per Minutes unit.

Kiloliter per Seconds is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cup per Minutes is commonly used in US recipe scaling and household liquid dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Cup us: The US cup traces to 19th-century American cookbook standardization, most notably Fannie Farmer's insistence on precise, standardized measuring cups in her 1896 cookbook, which helped fix the cup at 1/16 of a US gallon.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
