Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Decades (kL/decade)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Metric teaspoon per Weeks (metric tsp/wk)
The teaspoon metric is 5 mL, the standard small dosing unit in medicine and cooking. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Metric teaspoon per Weeks (Cooking (metric))

One Kiloliter per Decades unit represents 383.306981 times more volume-per-time than one Metric teaspoon per Weeks unit.

Kiloliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Metric teaspoon per Weeks is commonly used in medicine dosing rates and small-scale recipe scaling.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Teaspoon metric: 5 mL was formally adopted as the standard medicinal teaspoon dose by international pharmacopeias in the 20th century, replacing the inconsistent traditional household teaspoon.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
