Unit definitions

Gallon per Minutes (gal/min)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Kiloliter per Months (kL/mo)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Minutes (US customary) vs Kiloliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Gallon per Minutes unit represents 165.911192 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Months unit.

Gallon per Minutes is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Kiloliter per Months is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one gallon every minute is the same physical flow as 165.911191622 kiloliter(s) every month, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/min works out to 1659.111916 kL/mo, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
