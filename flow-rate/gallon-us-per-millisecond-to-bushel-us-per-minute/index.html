Unit definitions

Gallon per Millisecond (gal/ms)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Bushel per Minutes (bu/min)
The bushel us is a customary dry-volume unit equal to 2,150.42 cubic inches (about 35.24 liters). This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Millisecond (US customary) vs Bushel per Minutes (Agriculture)

One Gallon per Millisecond unit represents 6445.252555 times more volume-per-time than one Bushel per Minutes unit.

Gallon per Millisecond is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Bushel per Minutes is commonly used in grain elevator throughput, harvest yield rates, and crop-drying airflow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Bushel us: The US bushel descends from the medieval English Winchester bushel, a grain-measuring standard formalized in the 15th century. The United States kept the Winchester bushel even after England adopted the larger Imperial bushel in 1824, so US and Imperial bushels differ slightly to this day.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
