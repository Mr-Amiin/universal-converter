Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Decades (hL/decade)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Cubic inch per Years (in3/yr)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic inch per Years (Imperial/US engineering)

One Hectoliter per Decades unit represents 610.237441 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic inch per Years unit.

Hectoliter per Decades is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic inch per Years is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

A flow of one hectoliter every decade is the same physical flow as 610.237440947 cubic inch(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
