Unit definitions

Gallon per Years (gal/yr)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Cubic inch per Decades (in3/decade)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Years (US customary) vs Cubic inch per Decades (Imperial/US engineering)

One Gallon per Years unit represents 2,310 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic inch per Decades unit.

Gallon per Years is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic inch per Decades is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Years (gal/yr) to Cubic inch per Decades (in3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every year is the same physical flow as 2310 cubic inch(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/yr works out to 23,100 in3/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
