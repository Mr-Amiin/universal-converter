Unit definitions

Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Deciliter per Minutes (dL/min)
The deciliter is equal to 10^-1 liters (a tenth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'deci-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Deciliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Decades unit represents 1.901324 times more volume-per-time than one Deciliter per Minutes unit.

Megaliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Deciliter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade) to Deciliter per Minutes (dL/min) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every decade is the same physical flow as 1.90132431041 deciliter(s) every minute, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/decade works out to 19.013243 dL/min, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Deciliter: The 'deci-' prefix (d, 10^-1) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
