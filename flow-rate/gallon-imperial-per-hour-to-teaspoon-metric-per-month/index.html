Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Metric teaspoon per Months (metric tsp/mo)
The teaspoon metric is 5 mL, the standard small dosing unit in medicine and cooking. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Hours (Imperial) vs Metric teaspoon per Months (Cooking (metric))

One Imperial gallon per Hours unit represents 664170.11073 times more volume-per-time than one Metric teaspoon per Months unit.

Imperial gallon per Hours is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Metric teaspoon per Months is commonly used in medicine dosing rates and small-scale recipe scaling.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Teaspoon metric: 5 mL was formally adopted as the standard medicinal teaspoon dose by international pharmacopeias in the 20th century, replacing the inconsistent traditional household teaspoon.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
