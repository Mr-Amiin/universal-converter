Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Seconds (hL/s)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Seconds (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial gallon per Hours (Imperial)

One Hectoliter per Seconds unit represents 79188.929388 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Hours unit.

Hectoliter per Seconds is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Imperial gallon per Hours is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Hectoliter per Seconds (hL/s) to Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one hectoliter every second is the same physical flow as 79188.9293877 imperial gallon(s) every hour, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.
