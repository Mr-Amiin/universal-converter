Unit definitions

Gallon per Months (gal/mo)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Months (US customary) vs Megaliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Fortnight unit represents 574326.55258 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Months unit.

Gallon per Months is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Megaliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one gallon every month is the same physical flow as 1.74116971522e-06 megaliter(s) every fortnight, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
