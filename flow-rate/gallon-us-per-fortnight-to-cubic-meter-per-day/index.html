Unit definitions

Gallon per Fortnight (gal/fn)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Cubic Meter per Days (m3/d)
The cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume: the space occupied by a cube measuring 1 meter on each edge. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Fortnight (US customary) vs Cubic Meter per Days (Metric SI)

One Cubic Meter per Days unit represents 3698.408733 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Fortnight unit.

Gallon per Fortnight is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic Meter per Days is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Cubic meter: As the natural volume unit of the metric system established in 1799, the cubic meter is the reference unit engineers and hydrologists use for river discharge, reservoir capacity, and industrial process design (m^3/s is the SI-coherent flow-rate unit).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.
