Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Years (hL/yr)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Decaliter per Days (daL/d)
The decaliter is equal to 10^1 liters (a ten liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'deca-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Decaliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Decaliter per Days unit represents 36.52425 times more volume-per-time than one Hectoliter per Years unit.

Hectoliter per Years is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Decaliter per Days is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Decaliter: The 'deca-' prefix (da, 10^1) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.
