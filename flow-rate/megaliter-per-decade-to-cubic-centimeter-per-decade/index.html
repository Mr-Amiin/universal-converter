Unit definitions

Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Cubic Centimeter per Decades (cm3/decade)
The cubic centimeter is the volume of a cube measuring one centimeter (10^-2 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^-6 cubic meters, not merely 10^-2. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Centimeter per Decades (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Megaliter per Decades unit represents 1000000000 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Centimeter per Decades unit.

Megaliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic Centimeter per Decades is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade) to Cubic Centimeter per Decades (cm3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every decade is the same physical flow as 1 cubic centimeter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/decade works out to 1 × 10¹⁰ cm3/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Cubic centimeter: The 'centi-' prefix (c, 10^-2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
