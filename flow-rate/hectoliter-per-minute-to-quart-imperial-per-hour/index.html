Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Minutes (hL/min)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Imperial quart per Hours (imp qt/h)
The quart imperial is 1.137 liters, 1/4 of an imperial gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial quart per Hours (Imperial)

One Hectoliter per Minutes unit represents 5279.261959 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial quart per Hours unit.

Hectoliter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Imperial quart per Hours is commonly used in UK bulk-liquid packaging rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Quart imperial: Part of the 1824 British Imperial system alongside the pint and gallon.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.
