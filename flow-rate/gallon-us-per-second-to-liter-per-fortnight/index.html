Unit definitions

Gallon per Seconds (gal/s)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Liter per Fortnight (L/fn)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Seconds (US customary) vs Liter per Fortnight (Metric)

One Gallon per Seconds unit represents 4578834.093926 times more volume-per-time than one Liter per Fortnight unit.

Gallon per Seconds is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Liter per Fortnight is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
