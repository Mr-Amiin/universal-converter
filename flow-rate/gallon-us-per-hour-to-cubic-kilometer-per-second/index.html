Unit definitions

Gallon per Hours (gal/h)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic Kilometer per Seconds (km3/s)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Hours (US customary) vs Cubic Kilometer per Seconds (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Seconds unit represents 9.51 × 10¹⁴ times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Hours unit.

Gallon per Hours is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic Kilometer per Seconds is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

One Gallon per Hours equals 1.0515 × 10⁻¹⁵ Cubic Kilometer per Seconds.

Yes. Divide the Cubic Kilometer per Seconds value by 1.0515 × 10⁻¹⁵, or multiply by 9.51019 × 10¹⁴, to get Gallon per Hours.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Hours (gal/h) to Cubic Kilometer per Seconds (km3/s) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every hour is the same physical flow as 1.0515 × 10⁻¹⁵ cubic kilometer(s) every second, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/h works out to 1.052 × 10⁻¹⁴ km3/s, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
