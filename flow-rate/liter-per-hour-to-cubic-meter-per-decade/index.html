Unit definitions

Liter per Hours (L/h)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic Meter per Decades (m3/decade)
The cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume: the space occupied by a cube measuring 1 meter on each edge. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Liter per Hours (Metric) vs Cubic Meter per Decades (Metric SI)

One Liter per Hours unit represents 87.6582 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Meter per Decades unit.

Liter per Hours is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic Meter per Decades is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Liter per Hours (L/h) to Cubic Meter per Decades (m3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one liter every hour is the same physical flow as 87.6582 cubic meter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering: a flow reported as 10 L/h works out to 876.582 m3/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic meter: As the natural volume unit of the metric system established in 1799, the cubic meter is the reference unit engineers and hydrologists use for river discharge, reservoir capacity, and industrial process design (m^3/s is the SI-coherent flow-rate unit).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
