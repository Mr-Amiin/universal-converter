Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Months (imp gal/mo)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Cubic inch per Seconds (in3/s)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Months (Imperial) vs Cubic inch per Seconds (Imperial/US engineering)

One Cubic inch per Seconds unit represents 9479.314313 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Months unit.

Imperial gallon per Months is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Cubic inch per Seconds is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
