Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Seconds (kL/s)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Tablespoon per Decades (tbsp/decade)
The tablespoon us is 14.79 mL, equal to 3 US teaspoons or 1/2 US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Seconds (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Tablespoon per Decades (Cooking (US))

One Kiloliter per Seconds unit represents 2.134 × 10¹³ times more volume-per-time than one Tablespoon per Decades unit.

Kiloliter per Seconds is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Tablespoon per Decades is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Kiloliter per Seconds (kL/s) to Tablespoon per Decades (tbsp/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one kiloliter every second is the same physical flow as 2.13413 × 10¹³ tablespoon(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 kL/s works out to 2.134 × 10¹⁴ tbsp/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Tablespoon us: Standardized alongside the rest of the US customary cooking-measure family in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
