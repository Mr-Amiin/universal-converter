Unit definitions

Megaliter per Days (ML/d)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Imperial gallon per Minutes (imp gal/min)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial gallon per Minutes (Imperial)

One Megaliter per Days unit represents 152.756422 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Minutes unit.

Megaliter per Days is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial gallon per Minutes is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
