Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Years (hL/yr)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Milliliter per Fortnight (mL/fn)
The milliliter is equal to 10^-3 liters (a thousandth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'milli-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Milliliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Hectoliter per Years unit represents 3833.06981 times more volume-per-time than one Milliliter per Fortnight unit.

Hectoliter per Years is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Milliliter per Fortnight is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Milliliter: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
