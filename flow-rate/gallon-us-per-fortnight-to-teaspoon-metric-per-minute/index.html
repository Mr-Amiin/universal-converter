Unit definitions

Gallon per Fortnight (gal/fn)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Metric teaspoon per Minutes (metric tsp/min)
The teaspoon metric is 5 mL, the standard small dosing unit in medicine and cooking. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Fortnight (US customary) vs Metric teaspoon per Minutes (Cooking (metric))

One Metric teaspoon per Minutes unit represents 26.628543 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Fortnight unit.

Gallon per Fortnight is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Metric teaspoon per Minutes is commonly used in medicine dosing rates and small-scale recipe scaling.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Teaspoon metric: 5 mL was formally adopted as the standard medicinal teaspoon dose by international pharmacopeias in the 20th century, replacing the inconsistent traditional household teaspoon.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
