Unit definitions

Gallon per Decades (gal/decade)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Megaliter per Years (ML/yr)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Decades (US customary) vs Megaliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Years unit represents 2641720.523581 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Decades unit.

Gallon per Decades is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Megaliter per Years is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Decades (gal/decade) to Megaliter per Years (ML/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every decade is the same physical flow as 3.78541 × 10⁻⁷ megaliter(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/decade works out to 0.000004 ML/yr, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
