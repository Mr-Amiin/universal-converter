Unit definitions

Liter per Days (L/d)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Pint per Minutes (pt/min)
The pint us is 473.18 mL, 1/8 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Liter per Days (Metric) vs Pint per Minutes (US customary)

One Pint per Minutes unit represents 681.374121 times more volume-per-time than one Liter per Days unit.

Liter per Days is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Pint per Minutes is commonly used in US beverage service and small-batch process rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Pint us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage as the US cup and quart.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
