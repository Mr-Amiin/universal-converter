Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is imperial fluid ounces per minute (imp fl oz/min). The Imperial Fluid Ounce (imp fl oz) is a unit of volume. The Minute (min) is a unit of time.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is cubic yards per millisecond (yd3/ms). The Cubic Yard (yd3) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

How do I convert imperial fluid ounces per minute to cubic yards per millisecond?

Multiply the imperial fluid ounces per minute value by 6.19381e-10 to get cubic yards per millisecond. For example, 10 imp fl oz/min × 6.19381e-10 = 6.19381e-9 yd3/ms.

Is the imperial fluid ounces per minute to cubic yards per millisecond conversion exact?

It's exact under the SI-based volume and time definitions used here, since both units convert to cubic meters per second through fixed, non-rounded factors.

Where is this conversion commonly used?

Flow rate conversions like this are used in plumbing, HVAC, irrigation, chemical processing, fuel and water metering, and engineering flow calculations.

Can I convert cubic yards per millisecond back to imperial fluid ounces per minute?

Yes. Divide the cubic yards per millisecond value by 6.19381e-10 (or multiply by 1.61451e+9) to get imperial fluid ounces per minute.

About this conversion

This page converts imperial fluid ounces per minute (imp fl oz/min) into cubic yards per millisecond (yd3/ms), two units of volumetric flow rate.

The Imperial Fluid Ounce (imp fl oz) is a unit of volume. The Minute (min) is a unit of time.

The Cubic Yard (yd3) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

The formula is yd3/ms = imp fl oz/min × 6.19381e-10, based on the fixed relationship between these two flow rate units.

Flow rate is calculated as a volume unit divided by a time unit, so every combination of a volume unit and a time unit forms a valid, exact flow rate unit.
