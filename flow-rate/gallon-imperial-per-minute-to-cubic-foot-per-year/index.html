Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Minutes (imp gal/min)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cubic foot per Years (ft3/yr)
The cubic foot is 28.3168 liters, the standard US/imperial unit for gas, airflow, and bulk volume. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Minutes (Imperial) vs Cubic foot per Years (Imperial/US engineering)

One Imperial gallon per Minutes unit represents 84437.805984 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic foot per Years unit.

Imperial gallon per Minutes is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Cubic foot per Years is commonly used in HVAC airflow (CFM), natural-gas metering, and construction volume estimates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cubic foot: The cubic foot has been used in English-speaking construction and trade since medieval standardization of the foot. It remains the default unit for US natural-gas billing and HVAC airflow rate, expressed as cubic feet per minute (CFM).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
