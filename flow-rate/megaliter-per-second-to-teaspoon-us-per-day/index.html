Unit history

Megaliter per Seconds: The liter dates to the 1795 French metric system, originally defined as the volume of one kilogram of water. The second was originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day; since 1967 it has been defined by a fixed number of caesium-133 atomic transitions.

Teaspoon per Days: The teaspoon became a standardized cooking measure in the US in the late 19th century. The day as a 24-hour period was formalized by the ancient Egyptians and refined by Hellenistic astronomers.
