Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (imp gal/ms)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Teaspoon per Years (tsp/yr)
The teaspoon us is 4.93 mL, 1/3 of a US tablespoon and 1/6 of a US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (Imperial) vs Teaspoon per Years (Cooking (US))

One Imperial gallon per Millisecond unit represents 2.911 × 10¹³ times more volume-per-time than one Teaspoon per Years unit.

Imperial gallon per Millisecond is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Teaspoon per Years is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Imperial gallon per Millisecond (imp gal/ms) to Teaspoon per Years (tsp/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one imperial gallon every millisecond is the same physical flow as 2.91059 × 10¹³ teaspoon(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow: a flow reported as 10 imp gal/ms works out to 2.911 × 10¹⁴ tsp/yr, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Teaspoon us: Standardized alongside the US cup and tablespoon in the late 19th century.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
