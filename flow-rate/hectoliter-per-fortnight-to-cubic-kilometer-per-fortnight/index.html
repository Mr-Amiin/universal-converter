Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Fortnight (hL/fn)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight (km3/fn)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight unit represents 1 × 10¹⁰ times more volume-per-time than one Hectoliter per Fortnight unit.

Hectoliter per Fortnight is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic Kilometer per Fortnight is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

A flow of one hectoliter every fortnight is the same physical flow as 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ cubic kilometer(s) every fortnight, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering: a flow reported as 10 hL/fn works out to 1 × 10⁻⁹ km3/fn, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
