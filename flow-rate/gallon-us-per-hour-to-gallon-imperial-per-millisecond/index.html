Unit definitions

Gallon per Hours (gal/h)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (imp gal/ms)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Hours (US customary) vs Imperial gallon per Millisecond (Imperial)

One Imperial gallon per Millisecond unit represents 4323419.731817 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Hours unit.

Gallon per Hours is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Imperial gallon per Millisecond is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
