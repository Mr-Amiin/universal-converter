Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Millisecond (hL/ms)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Cubic Kilometer per Weeks (km3/wk)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Millisecond (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Kilometer per Weeks (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Weeks unit represents 16.534392 times more volume-per-time than one Hectoliter per Millisecond unit.

Hectoliter per Millisecond is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic Kilometer per Weeks is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
