Unit history

Megaliter per Seconds: The liter dates to the 1795 French metric system, originally defined as the volume of one kilogram of water. The second was originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day; since 1967 it has been defined by a fixed number of caesium-133 atomic transitions.

Kiloliter per Minutes: The liter dates to the 1795 French metric system, originally defined as the volume of one kilogram of water. The minute (1/60 of an hour) descends from the Babylonian sexagesimal (base-60) counting system.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert Megaliter per Seconds to Kiloliter per Minutes?

Multiply the Megaliter per Seconds value by 60000 to get Kiloliter per Minutes. The formula is kL/min = ML/s × 60000.

How many Kiloliter per Minutes are in one Megaliter per Seconds?

One Megaliter per Seconds equals 60000 Kiloliter per Minutes.

Can I convert Kiloliter per Minutes back to Megaliter per Seconds?

Yes. Divide the Kiloliter per Minutes value by 60000, or multiply by 1.66666666667e-05, to get Megaliter per Seconds.

When would I use a second-based flow unit like Megaliter per Seconds?

Megaliter per Seconds expresses volume moved per second, which is common in kitchen, lab, and light-industrial contexts where flow is measured or billed over that time period.

About this conversion

This page converts Megaliter per Seconds (ML/s) to Kiloliter per Minutes (kL/min) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every second is the same physical flow as 60000 kiloliter(s) every minute. Flows in this range come up in everyday and light-industrial settings — kitchen and laboratory measurement, irrigation, small pumps, and household plumbing — so this pairing is genuinely useful for recipe scaling, fieldwork, or unit-system conversions between metric and US/imperial systems.

For example, a small pump or tap running at 12 ML/s is delivering the same flow as 720000 kL/min — a scale you could measure directly with a household or lab container.
