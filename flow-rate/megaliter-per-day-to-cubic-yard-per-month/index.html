Unit definitions

Megaliter per Days (ML/d)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Cubic yard per Months (yd3/mo)
The cubic yard is 764.55 liters (27 cubic feet), a bulk volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic yard per Months (Imperial/US engineering)

One Megaliter per Days unit represents 39809.929506 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic yard per Months unit.

Megaliter per Days is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic yard per Months is commonly used in concrete pour rates, earthmoving, and bulk aggregate delivery.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Cubic yard: The cubic yard follows from the English yard, standardized under King Edward I in the 13th century, and remains the standard billing unit for ready-mix concrete delivery in the United States.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
