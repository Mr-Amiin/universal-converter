Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Weeks (kL/wk)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Imperial pint per Decades (imp pt/decade)
The pint imperial is 568.26 mL, 1/8 of an imperial gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Weeks (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial pint per Decades (Imperial)

One Kiloliter per Weeks unit represents 918195.63625 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial pint per Decades unit.

Kiloliter per Weeks is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial pint per Decades is commonly used in UK beverage service and dispensing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Pint imperial: Fixed by the 1824 Weights and Measures Act; still the legally mandated draft beer and cider measure in UK pubs.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
