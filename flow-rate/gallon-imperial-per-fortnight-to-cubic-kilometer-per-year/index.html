Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Fortnight (imp gal/fn)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Cubic Kilometer per Years (km3/yr)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Fortnight (Imperial) vs Cubic Kilometer per Years (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Years unit represents 8.432 × 10⁹ times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Fortnight unit.

Imperial gallon per Fortnight is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Cubic Kilometer per Years is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
