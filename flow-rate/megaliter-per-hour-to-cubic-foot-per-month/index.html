Unit definitions

Megaliter per Hours (ML/h)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic foot per Months (ft3/mo)
The cubic foot is 28.3168 liters, the standard US/imperial unit for gas, airflow, and bulk volume. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Hours (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic foot per Months (Imperial/US engineering)

One Megaliter per Hours unit represents 25796834.320047 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic foot per Months unit.

Megaliter per Hours is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic foot per Months is commonly used in HVAC airflow (CFM), natural-gas metering, and construction volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Megaliter per Hours (ML/h) to Cubic foot per Months (ft3/mo) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one megaliter every hour is the same physical flow as 25796834.32 cubic foot(s) every month, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/h works out to 257968343.200466 ft3/mo, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic foot: The cubic foot has been used in English-speaking construction and trade since medieval standardization of the foot. It remains the default unit for US natural-gas billing and HVAC airflow rate, expressed as cubic feet per minute (CFM).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
