Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Hours (hL/h)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic yard per Years (yd3/yr)
The cubic yard is 764.55 liters (27 cubic feet), a bulk volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Hours (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic yard per Years (Imperial/US engineering)

One Hectoliter per Hours unit represents 1146.52597 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic yard per Years unit.

Hectoliter per Hours is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic yard per Years is commonly used in concrete pour rates, earthmoving, and bulk aggregate delivery.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic yard: The cubic yard follows from the English yard, standardized under King Edward I in the 13th century, and remains the standard billing unit for ready-mix concrete delivery in the United States.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
