Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Years (imp gal/yr)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Pint per Months (pt/mo)
The pint us is 473.18 mL, 1/8 of a US gallon. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Years (Imperial) vs Pint per Months (US customary)

One Pint per Months unit represents 1.249011 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Years unit.

Imperial gallon per Years is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Pint per Months is commonly used in US beverage service and small-batch process rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Imperial gallon per Years (imp gal/yr) to Pint per Months (pt/mo) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one imperial gallon every year is the same physical flow as 0.80063328367 pint(s) every month, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow: a flow reported as 10 imp gal/yr works out to 8.006333 pt/mo, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Pint us: Shares the 231-cubic-inch US gallon lineage as the US cup and quart.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
