From definition

Teaspoon per Millisecond (tsp/ms) is a volumetric flow rate unit that measures how much volume, expressed in teaspoon(s), passes a given point every millisecond. It is used to compare flow across pipes, pumps, channels, and instruments that report volume moved over that particular time interval.

To definition

Megaliter per Week (ML/wk) is a volumetric flow rate unit that measures how much volume, expressed in megaliter(s), passes a given point every week. It is used to compare flow across pipes, pumps, channels, and instruments that report volume moved over that particular time interval.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Teaspoon per Millisecond (tsp/ms) to Megaliter per Week (ML/wk) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one teaspoon every millisecond is the same physical flow as 2.9810117799 megaliter(s) every week, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units. For example, a kitchen faucet or garden hose running at 1 tsp/ms is moving flow equivalent to 2.9810117799 ML/wk — handy when one spec sheet lists flow in teaspoon per millisecond and another lists it in megaliter per week.

Metric flow units are built from SI volume and time units established under the metric system, while US customary flow units combine older trade-derived volume measures (like the gallon or cup) with standard time units, which is why the same physical flow rate looks very different when expressed in each system.
