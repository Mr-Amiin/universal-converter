Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Millisecond (kL/ms)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Teaspoon per Weeks (tsp/wk)
The teaspoon us is 4.93 mL, 1/3 of a US tablespoon and 1/6 of a US fluid ounce. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Millisecond (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Teaspoon per Weeks (Cooking (US))

One Kiloliter per Millisecond unit represents 1.227 × 10¹⁴ times more volume-per-time than one Teaspoon per Weeks unit.

Kiloliter per Millisecond is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Teaspoon per Weeks is commonly used in US recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Kiloliter per Millisecond (kL/ms) to Teaspoon per Weeks (tsp/wk) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one kiloliter every millisecond is the same physical flow as 1.22704 × 10¹⁴ teaspoon(s) every week, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 kL/ms works out to 1.227 × 10¹⁵ tsp/wk, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Teaspoon us: Standardized alongside the US cup and tablespoon in the late 19th century.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
