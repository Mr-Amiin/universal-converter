Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Fortnight (kL/fn)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Imperial gallon per Hours (imp gal/h)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Imperial gallon per Hours (Imperial)

One Imperial gallon per Hours unit represents 1.527486 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Fortnight unit.

Kiloliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Imperial gallon per Hours is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.
