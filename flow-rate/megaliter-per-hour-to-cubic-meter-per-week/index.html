Unit definitions

Megaliter per Hours (ML/h)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic Meter per Weeks (m3/wk)
The cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume: the space occupied by a cube measuring 1 meter on each edge. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Hours (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic Meter per Weeks (Metric SI)

One Megaliter per Hours unit represents 168,000 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Meter per Weeks unit.

Megaliter per Hours is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic Meter per Weeks is commonly used in hydrology, civil engineering, and industrial process design.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

About this conversion

A flow of one megaliter every hour is the same physical flow as 168000 cubic meter(s) every week, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/h works out to 1,680,000 m3/wk, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic meter: As the natural volume unit of the metric system established in 1799, the cubic meter is the reference unit engineers and hydrologists use for river discharge, reservoir capacity, and industrial process design (m^3/s is the SI-coherent flow-rate unit).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
