Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Years (kL/yr)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Fluid ounce per Seconds (fl oz/s)
The fluid ounce us is 29.57 mL, 1/16 of a US pint. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Years (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Fluid ounce per Seconds (US customary)

One Fluid ounce per Seconds unit represents 933.250453 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Years unit.

Kiloliter per Years is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Fluid ounce per Seconds is commonly used in beverage dispensing and US pharmacy dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Fluid ounce us: Derived from the old English wine gallon the American colonies kept after independence, later fixed under the 231-cubic-inch US gallon.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
