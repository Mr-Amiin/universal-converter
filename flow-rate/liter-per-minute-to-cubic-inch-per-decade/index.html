Unit definitions

Liter per Minutes (L/min)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cubic inch per Decades (in3/decade)
The cubic inch is 16.387 mL, a small imperial volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Liter per Minutes (Metric) vs Cubic inch per Decades (Imperial/US engineering)

One Liter per Minutes unit represents 320953893.876292 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic inch per Decades unit.

Liter per Minutes is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic inch per Decades is commonly used in small hydraulic and pneumatic system flow, and legacy engine calculations.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cubic inch: Cubic inches described US and UK engine displacement for most of the 20th century (a '350 cubic-inch' V8, for instance) before the industry largely shifted to liters.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
