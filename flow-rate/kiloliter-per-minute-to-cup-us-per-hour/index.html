Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Minutes (kL/min)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Cup per Hours (cup/h)
The cup us is 8 US fluid ounces (about 236.6 mL), the standard US recipe-measuring unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cup per Hours (Cooking (US))

One Kiloliter per Minutes unit represents 253605.170264 times more volume-per-time than one Cup per Hours unit.

Kiloliter per Minutes is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cup per Hours is commonly used in US recipe scaling and household liquid dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Cup us: The US cup traces to 19th-century American cookbook standardization, most notably Fannie Farmer's insistence on precise, standardized measuring cups in her 1896 cookbook, which helped fix the cup at 1/16 of a US gallon.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.
