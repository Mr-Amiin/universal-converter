Unit definitions

Megaliter per Fortnight (ML/fn)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Decaliter per Decades (daL/decade)
The decaliter is equal to 10^1 liters (a ten liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'deca-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Decaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Fortnight unit represents 26088750 times more volume-per-time than one Decaliter per Decades unit.

Megaliter per Fortnight is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Decaliter per Decades is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

A flow of one megaliter every fortnight is the same physical flow as 2608875 decaliter(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting: a flow reported as 10 ML/fn works out to 260887500 daL/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Decaliter: The 'deca-' prefix (da, 10^1) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
