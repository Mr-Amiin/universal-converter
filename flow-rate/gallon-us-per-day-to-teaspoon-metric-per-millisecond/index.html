Unit definitions

Gallon per Days (gal/d)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Metric teaspoon per Millisecond (metric tsp/ms)
The teaspoon metric is 5 mL, the standard small dosing unit in medicine and cooking. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Days (US customary) vs Metric teaspoon per Millisecond (Cooking (metric))

One Metric teaspoon per Millisecond unit represents 114122.326619 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Days unit.

Gallon per Days is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Metric teaspoon per Millisecond is commonly used in medicine dosing rates and small-scale recipe scaling.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Teaspoon metric: 5 mL was formally adopted as the standard medicinal teaspoon dose by international pharmacopeias in the 20th century, replacing the inconsistent traditional household teaspoon.

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).
