Unit definitions

Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Metric tablespoon per Minutes (metric tbsp/min)
The tablespoon metric is 15 mL, a rounded, decimal-friendly cooking measure. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Megaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Metric tablespoon per Minutes (Cooking (metric))

One Megaliter per Decades unit represents 12.675495 times more volume-per-time than one Metric tablespoon per Minutes unit.

Megaliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Metric tablespoon per Minutes is commonly used in recipe scaling and small dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Tablespoon metric: Adopted during 20th-century metrication as a simple 15 mL figure, close enough to the older household tablespoon to substitute directly in most recipes.

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
