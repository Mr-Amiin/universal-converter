Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Days (hL/d)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Cup per Days (cup/d)
The cup us is 8 US fluid ounces (about 236.6 mL), the standard US recipe-measuring unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cup per Days (Cooking (US))

One Hectoliter per Days unit represents 422.675284 times more volume-per-time than one Cup per Days unit.

Hectoliter per Days is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cup per Days is commonly used in US recipe scaling and household liquid dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.

Cup us: The US cup traces to 19th-century American cookbook standardization, most notably Fannie Farmer's insistence on precise, standardized measuring cups in her 1896 cookbook, which helped fix the cup at 1/16 of a US gallon.
