Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Months (kL/mo)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Cubic yard per Seconds (yd3/s)
The cubic yard is 764.55 liters (27 cubic feet), a bulk volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Cubic yard per Seconds (Imperial/US engineering)

One Cubic yard per Seconds unit represents 2010585.079564 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Months unit.

Kiloliter per Months is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Cubic yard per Seconds is commonly used in concrete pour rates, earthmoving, and bulk aggregate delivery.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

A flow of one kiloliter every month is the same physical flow as 4.97368 × 10⁻⁷ cubic yard(s) every second, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.

Cubic yard: The cubic yard follows from the English yard, standardized under King Edward I in the 13th century, and remains the standard billing unit for ready-mix concrete delivery in the United States.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
