Unit definitions

Gallon per Seconds (gal/s)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Decaliter per Weeks (daL/wk)
The decaliter is equal to 10^1 liters (a ten liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'deca-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Seconds (US customary) vs Decaliter per Weeks (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Gallon per Seconds unit represents 228941.704696 times more volume-per-time than one Decaliter per Weeks unit.

Gallon per Seconds is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Decaliter per Weeks is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Decaliter: The 'deca-' prefix (da, 10^1) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.
