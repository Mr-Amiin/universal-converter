Unit definitions

Gallon per Years (gal/yr)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Decaliter per Seconds (daL/s)
The decaliter is equal to 10^1 liters (a ten liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'deca-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Years (US customary) vs Decaliter per Seconds (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Decaliter per Seconds unit represents 83364647.760076 times more volume-per-time than one Gallon per Years unit.

Gallon per Years is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Decaliter per Seconds is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Years (gal/yr) to Decaliter per Seconds (daL/s) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every year is the same physical flow as 1.19955 × 10⁻⁸ decaliter(s) every second, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/yr works out to 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ daL/s, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.

Decaliter: The 'deca-' prefix (da, 10^1) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
