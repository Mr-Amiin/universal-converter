Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Decades (imp gal/decade)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Gallon per Months (gal/mo)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Decades (Imperial) vs Gallon per Months (US customary)

One Gallon per Months unit represents 99.920902 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Decades unit.

Imperial gallon per Decades is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Gallon per Months is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
