Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (imp gal/ms)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every millisecond (the millisecond is equal to 10^-3 seconds (a thousandth of a second)).

Kiloliter per Days (kL/d)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every day (the day is 86,400 seconds by convention (one mean solar day)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Millisecond (Imperial) vs Kiloliter per Days (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Imperial gallon per Millisecond unit represents 392782.176 times more volume-per-time than one Kiloliter per Days unit.

Imperial gallon per Millisecond is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Kiloliter per Days is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Millisecond: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Day: The day is humanity's oldest natural time unit, defined by Earth's rotation; civil timekeeping fixes it at exactly 86,400 SI seconds, with leap seconds periodically reconciling it to the planet's actual (slightly irregular) rotation.
