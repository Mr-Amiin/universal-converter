Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Seconds (imp gal/s)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Cubic yard per Hours (yd3/h)
The cubic yard is 764.55 liters (27 cubic feet), a bulk volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Seconds (Imperial) vs Cubic yard per Hours (Imperial/US engineering)

One Imperial gallon per Seconds unit represents 21.40582 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic yard per Hours unit.

Imperial gallon per Seconds is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Cubic yard per Hours is commonly used in concrete pour rates, earthmoving, and bulk aggregate delivery.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.

Cubic yard: The cubic yard follows from the English yard, standardized under King Edward I in the 13th century, and remains the standard billing unit for ready-mix concrete delivery in the United States.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.
