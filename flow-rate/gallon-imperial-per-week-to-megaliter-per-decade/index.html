Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Weeks (imp gal/wk)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Megaliter per Decades (ML/decade)
The megaliter is equal to 10^6 liters (a million liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'mega-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Weeks (Imperial) vs Megaliter per Decades (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Megaliter per Decades unit represents 421.578742 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Weeks unit.

Imperial gallon per Weeks is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Megaliter per Decades is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Megaliter: The 'mega-' prefix (M, 10^6) was adopted by the 11th CGPM in 1960.

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
