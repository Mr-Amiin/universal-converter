Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is deciliters per decade (dL/decade). The Deciliter (dL) is a unit of volume. The Decade (decade) is a unit of time.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is imperial pints per millisecond (imp pt/ms). The Imperial Pint (imp pt) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

How do I convert deciliters per decade to imperial pints per millisecond?

Multiply the deciliters per decade value by 5.57644e-13 to get imperial pints per millisecond. For example, 10 dL/decade × 5.57644e-13 = 5.57644e-12 imp pt/ms.

Is the deciliters per decade to imperial pints per millisecond conversion exact?

It's exact under the SI-based volume and time definitions used here, since both units convert to cubic meters per second through fixed, non-rounded factors.

Where is this conversion commonly used?

Flow rate conversions like this are used in plumbing, HVAC, irrigation, chemical processing, fuel and water metering, and engineering flow calculations.

Can I convert imperial pints per millisecond back to deciliters per decade?

Yes. Divide the imperial pints per millisecond value by 5.57644e-13 (or multiply by 1.79326e+12) to get deciliters per decade.

About this conversion

This page converts deciliters per decade (dL/decade) into imperial pints per millisecond (imp pt/ms), two units of volumetric flow rate.

The Deciliter (dL) is a unit of volume. The Decade (decade) is a unit of time.

The Imperial Pint (imp pt) is a unit of volume. The Millisecond (ms) is a unit of time.

The formula is imp pt/ms = dL/decade × 5.57644e-13, based on the fixed relationship between these two flow rate units.

Flow rate is calculated as a volume unit divided by a time unit, so every combination of a volume unit and a time unit forms a valid, exact flow rate unit.
