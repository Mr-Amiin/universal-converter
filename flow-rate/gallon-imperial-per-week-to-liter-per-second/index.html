Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Weeks (imp gal/wk)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Liter per Seconds (L/s)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every second (the second is the SI base unit of time).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Weeks (Imperial) vs Liter per Seconds (Metric)

One Liter per Seconds unit represents 133037.401371 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Weeks unit.

Imperial gallon per Weeks is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Liter per Seconds is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Second: Originally 1/86,400 of a mean solar day, the second was redefined by the 13th CGPM in 1967 in terms of the cesium-133 atomic transition frequency (9,192,631,770 cycles), removing its dependence on Earth's rotation.
