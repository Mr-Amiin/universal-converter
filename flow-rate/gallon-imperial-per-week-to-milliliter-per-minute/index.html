Unit definitions

Imperial gallon per Weeks (imp gal/wk)
The gallon imperial is 4.546 liters, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Milliliter per Minutes (mL/min)
The milliliter is equal to 10^-3 liters (a thousandth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'milli-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Imperial gallon per Weeks (Imperial) vs Milliliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Milliliter per Minutes unit represents 2.21729 times more volume-per-time than one Imperial gallon per Weeks unit.

Imperial gallon per Weeks is commonly used in UK fuel-economy figures and Commonwealth plumbing/irrigation flow, while Milliliter per Minutes is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Gallon imperial: Established by the British Weights and Measures Act of 1824 to unify the many competing 'gallon' definitions then in use (ale gallon, wine gallon, corn gallon).

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Milliliter: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.
