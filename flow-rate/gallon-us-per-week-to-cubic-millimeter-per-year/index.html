Unit definitions

Gallon per Weeks (gal/wk)
The gallon us is 3.785 liters (231 cubic inches), the standard US liquid-volume unit. This flow unit measures that volume moved every week (the week is 7 days (604,800 seconds)).

Cubic Millimeter per Years (mm3/yr)
The cubic millimeter is the volume of a cube measuring one millimeter (10^-3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^-9 cubic meters, not merely 10^-3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every year (the year is the average Gregorian calendar year, 365.2425 days (31,556,952 seconds)).

Unit comparison

Gallon per Weeks (US customary) vs Cubic Millimeter per Years (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Gallon per Weeks unit represents 197513323.35966 times more volume-per-time than one Cubic Millimeter per Years unit.

Gallon per Weeks is commonly used in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing, while Cubic Millimeter per Years is commonly used in precision engineering, microfabrication, and lab-scale volume measurement.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Gallon per Weeks (gal/wk) to Cubic Millimeter per Years (mm3/yr) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one gallon every week is the same physical flow as 197513323.36 cubic millimeter(s) every year, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in US plumbing codes (fixture GPM ratings), irrigation, and fuel dispensing: a flow reported as 10 gal/wk works out to 1.975 × 10⁹ mm3/yr, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Gallon us: Derives from the old English wine gallon (231 cubic inches), which American colonies kept even after Britain switched to the larger imperial gallon in 1824.

Week: The 7-day week is not part of the SI system but derives from Babylonian and Judeo-Christian tradition, historically tied to the sun, moon, and five visible planets known to antiquity.

Cubic millimeter: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Year: The Gregorian calendar's 365.2425-day average year (Pope Gregory XIII, 1582) refined the Julian year of exactly 365.25 days to better track Earth's actual orbital period.
