Unit definitions

Liter per Hours (L/h)
The liter is a non-SI metric unit of volume equal to exactly one cubic decimeter (0.001 m^3). This flow unit measures that volume moved every hour (the hour is 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)).

Cubic Kilometer per Decades (km3/decade)
The cubic kilometer is the volume of a cube measuring one kilometer (10^3 m) on each edge -- since volume scales with the cube of length, that works out to 10^9 cubic meters, not merely 10^3. This flow unit measures that volume moved every decade (the decade is 10 years).

Unit comparison

Liter per Hours (Metric) vs Cubic Kilometer per Decades (Metric SI (prefixed cubic meter))

One Cubic Kilometer per Decades unit represents 11407945.862452 times more volume-per-time than one Liter per Hours unit.

Liter per Hours is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Cubic Kilometer per Decades is commonly used in large-scale civil engineering, hydrology, and geographic volume estimates.

Because the two units differ by many orders of magnitude, use scientific notation rather than rounding to a fixed number of decimal places to avoid losing precision.

About this conversion

This flow rate page converts Liter per Hours (L/h) to Cubic Kilometer per Decades (km3/decade) using the exact volumetric flow rate factor from the Universal Converter engine. A flow of one liter every hour is the same physical flow as 8.76582 × 10⁻⁸ cubic kilometer(s) every decade, so this page is useful for pipeline sizing, pump and pipe selection, HVAC airflow and liquid handling, irrigation and water-supply planning, and lab or industrial process calculations that mix metric, US customary, or imperial flow units.

This conversion shows up most often in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering: a flow reported as 10 L/h works out to 8.766 × 10⁻⁷ km3/decade, which is useful when a spec sheet, recipe, or regulation states a rate in one unit system and you need it in the other.

Unit history

Liter: Introduced in 1795 during French Revolutionary metrication, the liter was briefly redefined in 1901 as the volume of 1 kg of pure water at maximum density (making it 1.000028 dm^3) before the 12th CGPM restored the simpler cubic-decimeter definition in 1964.

Hour: Traces to the same Babylonian sexagesimal system as the minute; the modern equal-length hour became standard only after mechanical clocks replaced sundials in the 14th century.

Cubic kilometer: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Decade: From the Greek 'deka' (ten); used in long-range planning rather than any single legal standard.
