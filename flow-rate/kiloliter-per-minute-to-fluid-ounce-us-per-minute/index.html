Unit definitions

Kiloliter per Minutes (kL/min)
The kiloliter is equal to 10^3 liters (a thousand liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'kilo-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Fluid ounce per Minutes (fl oz/min)
The fluid ounce us is 29.57 mL, 1/16 of a US pint. This flow unit measures that volume moved every minute (the minute is 60 seconds).

Unit comparison

Kiloliter per Minutes (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Fluid ounce per Minutes (US customary)

One Kiloliter per Minutes unit represents 33814.022702 times more volume-per-time than one Fluid ounce per Minutes unit.

Kiloliter per Minutes is commonly used in municipal water treatment, reservoir capacity, and utility-scale flow reporting, while Fluid ounce per Minutes is commonly used in beverage dispensing and US pharmacy dosing rates.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Kiloliter: The 'kilo-' prefix (k, 10^3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Minute: Along with the hour, the minute traces to the Babylonian base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system used in ancient astronomy, later carried into medieval mechanical clockmaking.

Fluid ounce us: Derived from the old English wine gallon the American colonies kept after independence, later fixed under the 231-cubic-inch US gallon.
