Unit definitions

Hectoliter per Fortnight (hL/fn)
The hectoliter is equal to 10^2 liters (a hundred liters), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'hecto-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every fortnight (the fortnight is 14 days (two weeks, 1,209,600 seconds)).

Milliliter per Months (mL/mo)
The milliliter is equal to 10^-3 liters (a thousandth of a liter), formed by attaching the SI prefix 'milli-' to the liter. This flow unit measures that volume moved every month (the month is an average calendar month, taken as 1/12 of the 365.2425-day Gregorian year (about 30.44 days)).

Unit comparison

Hectoliter per Fortnight (Metric (SI-prefixed liter)) vs Milliliter per Months (Metric (SI-prefixed liter))

One Hectoliter per Fortnight unit represents 217406.25 times more volume-per-time than one Milliliter per Months unit.

Hectoliter per Fortnight is commonly used in beverages, fuel, laboratory glassware, and municipal water metering, while Milliliter per Months is commonly used in laboratory dosing, IV infusion rates, and microfluidic/pharmaceutical dispensing.

The two units are close enough in scale that ordinary decimal rounding (4-6 decimal places) keeps results accurate for most practical uses.

Unit history

Hectoliter: The 'hecto-' prefix (h, 10^2) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Fortnight: From the Old English 'feowertyne niht' ('fourteen nights'); still in everyday use in the UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.

Milliliter: The 'milli-' prefix (m, 10^-3) dates to the original 1795 French decimal-metric system, decades before the CGPM was even established (1889).

Month: Calendar months originally tracked the roughly 29.5-day lunar (synodic) cycle; because civil months now vary from 28 to 31 days, unit converters use the year-based average for a single consistent value.
