Frequently asked questions

How many milligrams per microliter are in one millimole per liter?

One Millimole per liter equals 0.001 milligrams per microliter.

Why is millimole per liter used in chemistry?

Millimole per liter is preferred whenever the number of reacting particles matters more than their mass, which is commonly used in clinical chemistry and medical lab results, such as blood glucose or electrolyte levels.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both millimole per liters and milligrams per microliter measure the same kind of quantity, so this conversion uses a fixed, exact factor.

Can I use this conversion for any chemical?

Yes. Since millimole per liter and milligram per microliter both measure the same kind of quantity, this factor applies regardless of which chemical is involved.

When would you use millimole per liters instead of milligrams per microliter?

Millimole per liter is typically used in commonly used in clinical chemistry and medical lab results, such as blood glucose or electrolyte levels, while milligram per microliter is typically used in reporting concentrations in used in analytical and biochemical work with very small sample volumes using a metric mass unit (milligram).

About this conversion

Millimoles per liter and Milligrams per microliter are units used in the Universal Converter chemistry category. Millimole per liter (mmol/L): Millimolar concentration. Milligram per Microliter (mg/uL): Mass concentration.

Millimoles per liter belongs to the molar concentration family, while Milligrams per microliter belongs to the mass concentration family. The chemistry converter is dimension-aware, so a direct conversion between units from different families is not chemically meaningful without extra context (such as a substance's molar mass or solution density); the scale factor above is provided purely for numeric reference.
