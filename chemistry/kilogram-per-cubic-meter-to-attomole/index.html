Frequently asked questions

How many attomoles are in one kilogram per cubic meter?

One kilogram per cubic meter equals 1×10^18 attomoles on this converter's fixed reference assumption.

Why is kilogram per cubic meter used in chemistry?

Kilogram per cubic meter is used specifically when used for large-scale industrial and environmental concentration reporting, such as air or water quality, expressed via a metric mass unit (kilogram).

Is this conversion exact?

Not for a specific real substance. kilogram per cubic meter measures amount of substance while attomole measures mass-based concentration, and converting between them genuinely requires the molar mass of the exact chemical involved. This page uses a fixed reference assumption, so treat the result as illustrative rather than substance-specific.

Not directly -- because it crosses between amount-of-substance and mass-based concentration, the real-world factor depends on the molar mass of whatever chemical you're working with. Use this page as a reference, then substitute the actual molar mass for precise lab work.
