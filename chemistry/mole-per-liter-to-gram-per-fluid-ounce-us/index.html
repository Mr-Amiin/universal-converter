Frequently asked questions

How many grams per fluid ounce are in one mole per liter?

One Mole per liter equals 29.57353 grams per fluid ounce.

Why is mole per liter used in chemistry?

Mole per liter is preferred whenever the number of reacting particles matters more than their mass, which is the standard SI unit of molar concentration (molarity), central to reaction stoichiometry.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both mole per liters and grams per fluid ounce measure the same kind of quantity, so this conversion uses a fixed, exact factor.

Can I use this conversion for any chemical?

Yes. Since mole per liter and gram per fluid ounce both measure the same kind of quantity, this factor applies regardless of which chemical is involved.

When would you use mole per liters instead of grams per fluid ounce?

Mole per liter is typically used in the standard SI unit of molar concentration (molarity), central to reaction stoichiometry, while gram per fluid ounce is typically used in reporting concentrations in used in US-based food and consumer-chemistry concentration labeling using a metric mass unit (gram).

About this conversion

Moles per liter and Grams per fluid ounce are units used in the Universal Converter chemistry category. Mole per liter (mol/L): Molar concentration. Gram per Fluid ounce (g/fl oz): Mass concentration.

Moles per liter belongs to the molar concentration family, while Grams per fluid ounce belongs to the mass concentration family. The chemistry converter is dimension-aware, so a direct conversion between units from different families is not chemically meaningful without extra context (such as a substance's molar mass or solution density); the scale factor above is provided purely for numeric reference.
