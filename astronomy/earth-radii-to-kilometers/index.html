About Converting Earth Radii to Kilometers

Earth radius and kilometer are both units of distance. The nominal equatorial Earth radius is exactly 6,378.1 kilometers (IAU 2015). A kilometer is 1,000 meters.

Formula

km = R⊕ × 6378.1

This factor comes from the defined size of each unit: 1 earth radius equals 6378.1 kilometers, and the reverse factor is 0.0001567865038.

Simple example

1 R⊕ × 6378.1 = 6378.1 km

1 earth radius = 6378.1 kilometers.

Real-world example

10 R⊕ × 6378.1 = 63781 km

10 earth radius = 63781 kilometers.

Convention used on this page

IAU 2015 nominal equatorial radius, exactly 6.3781 × 10⁶ m. The polar radius is smaller (6,356.8 km) and the volumetric mean is 6,371.0 km.

Understanding the Earth radius

Earth radius (R⊕) measures distance. The nominal equatorial Earth radius is exactly 6,378.1 kilometers (IAU 2015).

Convention: IAU 2015 nominal equatorial radius, exactly 6.3781 × 10⁶ m. The polar radius is smaller (6,356.8 km) and the volumetric mean is 6,371.0 km.

Standard and definition

The earth radius is covered by IAU 2015 Resolution B3 — Nominal values for selected solar and planetary quantities.

Uses of the earth radius today

  • Sizes of planets and exoplanets
  • Orbit and mission design
