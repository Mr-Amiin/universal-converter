Frequently asked questions

How many centuries are in one second?

One Second equals 3.168874×10^-10 centuries.

Why is the century used in astronomy?

Some astronomical effects, like the precession of Earth's axis, unfold so slowly that expressing them in years alone would produce unwieldy numbers, so astronomers use centuries instead.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both seconds and centuries measure time, so this conversion uses a fixed, exact factor.

How large is one second in real terms?

The second is the SI base unit of time, currently defined by a fixed number of oscillations of a cesium-133 atom.

When is seconds to centuries conversion commonly used?

It comes up in precise timing of orbital mechanics, signal delays, and spacecraft maneuvers, as well as describing slow astronomical effects like axial precession and secular orbital drift.
