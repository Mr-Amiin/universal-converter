About Converting Astronomical Units to Light-minutes

Astronomical unit and light-minute are both units of distance. The astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 meters (IAU 2012), about the mean Earth–Sun distance. A light-minute is the distance light travels in vacuum in one minute: 17,987,547,480 meters.

Understanding the Astronomical unit

Astronomical unit (au) measures distance. The astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 meters (IAU 2012), about the mean Earth–Sun distance.

Standard and definition

The astronomical unit is covered by IAU 2015 Resolution B3 — Nominal values for selected solar and planetary quantities.

Uses of the astronomical unit today

  • Solar-system distances
  • Orbital mechanics

Understanding the Light-minute

Light-minute (lmin) measures distance. A light-minute is the distance light travels in vacuum in one minute: 17,987,547,480 meters.

Standard and definition

The light-minute is covered by BIPM — The International System of Units (SI Brochure).

Uses of the light-minute today

  • Describing solar-system distances (the Sun is about 8.3 light-minutes away)
  • Communication-delay calculations for spacecraft
