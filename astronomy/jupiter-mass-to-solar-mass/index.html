Frequently asked questions

How many solar masses are in one jupiter mass?

One Jupiter mass equals 0.000955 solar masses.

Why is the jupiter mass used in astronomy?

Because so many known exoplanets are gas giants roughly Jupiter's size, astronomers report their masses in Jupiter masses rather than Earth masses to keep the numbers close to 1.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both jupiter masses and solar masses measure mass, so this conversion uses a fixed, exact factor.

How large is one jupiter mass in real terms?

A Jupiter mass is a unit of mass equal to the mass of the planet Jupiter, used to describe gas giants and large exoplanets.

When is jupiter masses to solar masses conversion commonly used?

It comes up in describing the mass of gas giant planets and large exoplanets, as well as describing the mass of stars, black holes, and galactic components.

About this conversion

This page converts jupiter mass (MJ) to solar mass (M☉) within the astronomy converter.

Jupiter mass is the mass of Jupiter, used as a reference unit for giant planets.

Solar mass is the mass of the Sun, used as a reference unit for stars, black holes, and galaxies.

The formula is solar mass = jupiter mass × 0.000954568085, based on each unit's fixed relationship to the astronomy converter's mass base unit.

The conversion is exact, since it relies on a fixed numerical ratio between the two units rather than a measured or approximate value.
