Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the decanewton (daN). A decanewton is 10 newtons, a metric multiple of the newton.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the femtonewton (fN). A femtonewton is a metric submultiple of the newton equal to 10^-15 newtons.

How do I convert decanewton to femtonewton?

Multiply the decanewton value by 1.000000e+16. For example, 5 daN × 1.000000e+16 = 5.000000e+16 fN.

Why do decanewton and femtonewton need converting at all?

Deca (da) represents 10^1 and is one of the least commonly used SI prefixes. Femto (f) represents 10^-15, used in particle physics and ultrafast optics.

Is the decanewton to femtonewton conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between decanewton and femtonewton is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+16), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert femtonewton back to decanewton?

Yes. Multiply the femtonewton value by 1.000000e-16 (or divide by 1.000000e+16) to get decanewton.
