Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the newton (N). The newton (N) is the SI derived unit of force, equal to the force needed to accelerate a 1 kilogram mass at 1 meter per second squared.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the yoctonewton (yN). A yoctonewton is a metric submultiple of the newton equal to 10^-24 newtons.

How do I convert newton to yoctonewton?

Multiply the newton value by 1.000000e+24. For example, 5 N × 1.000000e+24 = 5.000000e+24 yN.

Why do newton and yoctonewton need converting at all?

The newton is the SI unit of force, defined as the force needed to accelerate 1 kilogram at 1 meter per second squared. Yocto (y) represents 10^-24, historically the smallest SI prefix before 2022.

Is the newton to yoctonewton conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between newton and yoctonewton is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+24), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert yoctonewton back to newton?

Yes. Multiply the yoctonewton value by 1.000000e-24 (or divide by 1.000000e+24) to get newton.
