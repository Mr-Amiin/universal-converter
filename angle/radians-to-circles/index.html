Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the radian (rad). The radian is the SI angle unit.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the circle (circle). One complete circle.

How do I convert radian to circle?

Multiply the radian value by 0.159155. For example, 10 rad × 0.159155 = 1.591549 circle.

Is the radian to circle conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between these units is a fixed mathematical or SI-defined ratio, so the conversion factor is exact rather than a measured approximation.

Where is this conversion commonly used?

It's used whenever a value expressed in radian needs to be compared with, or reported in, circle, such as in engineering, scientific, or everyday calculations.

Can I convert circle back to radian?

Yes. Multiply the circle value by 6.283185 to get radian.

Why would I use radian instead of circle?

Radian is the standard choice for calculus, physics, and engineering formulas, such as angular velocity and trigonometric functions, while circle is preferred for rotation and revolution counting. Converting between them lets you move a measurement from one workflow to the other without redoing the underlying calculation.

What is 90 rad in circle?

90 rad equals 14.32395 circle, using the same multiply-by-factor method as any other value on this page.
