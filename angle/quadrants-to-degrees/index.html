About Converting Quadrants to Degrees

Quadrant and degree are both units of plane angle. A quadrant is a quarter of a full circle: 90 degrees or π/2 radians. A degree is 1/360 of a full circle: π/180 radians.

Formula

° = quadrant × 90

This factor comes from the defined size of each unit: 1 quadrant equals 90 degrees, and the reverse factor is 0.01111111111.

Simple example

1 quadrant × 90 = 90 °

1 quadrant = 90 degrees.

Real-world example

10 quadrant × 90 = 900 °

10 quadrants = 900 degrees.

Understanding the Quadrant

Quadrant (quadrant) measures plane angle. A quadrant is a quarter of a full circle: 90 degrees or π/2 radians.

Standard and definition

The quadrant is covered by BIPM — The International System of Units (SI Brochure).

Uses of the quadrant today

  • Dividing the plane into four sectors in trigonometry and navigation
  • Historic astronomical and surveying instruments

Frequently asked questions

How many degrees are in 1 quadrant?

1 quadrant equals 90 degrees.

How do I convert quadrant to degree?

Multiply the quadrant value by 90. The converter above does this instantly to whatever precision you set.

How do I convert degree back to quadrant?

Use the reverse factor: 1 degree equals 0.01111111111 quadrants. You can also use the swap control in the converter above to flip the direction instantly.

What is a quadrant?

Quadrant (quadrant) measures plane angle. A quadrant is a quarter of a full circle: 90 degrees or π/2 radians.

What is a degree?

Degree (°) measures plane angle. A degree is 1/360 of a full circle: π/180 radians.

Is the quadrant to degree conversion exact?

Yes. Both units are fixed by definition, so the factor is exact and any rounding you see is a display choice.

Where is the quadrant used today?

Dividing the plane into four sectors in trigonometry and navigation.
