Formula

Pound-inch (lb in) measures torque (rotational force), while Decipascal (dPa) measures pressure and mechanical stress. These are different physical quantities, so there is no single multiplication factor that converts one into the other.

lb in relates to its own torque (rotational force) base unit by a factor of 0.112985. dPa relates to its own pressure and mechanical stress base unit by a factor of 0.1.

Frequently asked questions

Can Pound-inch be converted to Decipascal?

Not directly. Pound-inch measures torque (rotational force) and Decipascal measures pressure and mechanical stress. Converting between different physical quantities needs an extra physical relationship (such as area, distance, or time), not just a unit ratio.

What is Pound-inch used for?

The Pound-inch (lb in) is a unit of torque (rotational force). Torque from one pound-force at one inch.

What is Decipascal used for?

The Decipascal (dPa) is a unit of pressure and mechanical stress. Decipascal is 0.1 pascal units.

Why does the converter show an incompatible units message?

How can I relate Pound-inch to Decipascal in practice?

Pound-inch measures torque (rotational force) and Decipascal measures pressure. Since these are different physical dimensions, there is no unit-only formula between them. In practice you would combine the pressure with an area and a lever-arm distance specific to your system; there's no single bridging quantity, using data specific to your situation rather than a fixed conversion factor.

About this conversion

This page compares Pound-inch (lb in) and Decipascal (dPa) within the engineering converter.

Torque from one pound-force at one inch. Decipascal is 0.1 pascal units.

Because Pound-inch measures torque (rotational force) and Decipascal measures pressure and mechanical stress, the two units are not directly convertible: there is no fixed ratio that turns one into the other without additional information.

The calculator above will report that these units are not compatible. For a working result, choose two units from the same measurement family — see the related conversions below.

For scale, a bicycle pedal stroke applies roughly 40-80 newton-metres of torque, while a car engine can produce several hundred newton-metres. On the pressure side, normal atmospheric pressure at sea level is about 101,325 pascals, and a typical car tyre is inflated to roughly 220,000 pascals. Seeing both scales side by side is often more useful than a single converted number would be.
