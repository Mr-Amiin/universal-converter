Formula

Quectopascal (qPa) measures pressure and mechanical stress, while Petanewton (PN) measures force. These are different physical quantities, so there is no single multiplication factor that converts one into the other.

qPa relates to its own pressure and mechanical stress base unit by a factor of 0. PN relates to its own force base unit by a factor of 1e+15.

Frequently asked questions

Can Quectopascal be converted to Petanewton?

Not directly. Quectopascal measures pressure and mechanical stress and Petanewton measures force. Converting between different physical quantities needs an extra physical relationship (such as area, distance, or time), not just a unit ratio.

What is Quectopascal used for?

The Quectopascal (qPa) is a unit of pressure and mechanical stress. Quectopascal is 1e-30 pascal units.

What is Petanewton used for?

The Petanewton (PN) is a unit of force. Petanewton is 1000000000000000 newton units.

How can I relate Quectopascal to Petanewton in practice?

Quectopascal measures pressure and Petanewton measures force. Since these are different physical dimensions, there is no unit-only formula between them. In practice you would multiply the pressure by an area (force = pressure × area), or divide a force by an area to get pressure, using data specific to your situation rather than a fixed conversion factor.

About this conversion

This page compares Quectopascal (qPa) and Petanewton (PN) within the engineering converter.

Quectopascal is 1e-30 pascal units. Petanewton is 1000000000000000 newton units.

Because Quectopascal measures pressure and mechanical stress and Petanewton measures force, the two units are not directly convertible: there is no fixed ratio that turns one into the other without additional information.

The calculator above will report that these units are not compatible. For a working result, choose two units from the same measurement family — see the related conversions below.

For scale, normal atmospheric pressure at sea level is about 101,325 pascals, and a typical car tyre is inflated to roughly 220,000 pascals. On the force side, the weight of a 1 kg object on Earth is about 9.8 newtons, and a car engine can produce thrust in the thousands of newtons. Seeing both scales side by side is often more useful than a single converted number would be.
