Formula

Rontojoule (rJ) measures energy, while Nanowatt (nW) measures power. These are different physical quantities, so there is no single multiplication factor that converts one into the other.

rJ relates to its own energy base unit by a factor of 0. nW relates to its own power base unit by a factor of 1e-09.

Frequently asked questions

Can Rontojoule be converted to Nanowatt?

Not directly. Rontojoule measures energy and Nanowatt measures power. Converting between different physical quantities needs an extra physical relationship (such as area, distance, or time), not just a unit ratio.

What is Rontojoule used for?

The Rontojoule (rJ) is a unit of energy. Rontojoule is 1e-27 joule units.

What is Nanowatt used for?

The Nanowatt (nW) is a unit of power. Nanowatt is 1e-9 watt units.

Why does the converter show an incompatible units message?

The Universal Converter only calculates a numeric result when both units share the same physical dimension. Since Rontojoule and Nanowatt don't, the tool flags them as not directly compatible rather than returning a meaningless number.

What should I convert Rontojoule to instead?

Use another energy unit, such as one of the related Rontojoule conversions linked below.

What should I convert Nanowatt to instead?

How can I relate Rontojoule to Nanowatt in practice?

Rontojoule measures energy and Nanowatt measures power. Since these are different physical dimensions, there is no unit-only formula between them. In practice you would divide the energy by a time (power = energy ÷ time), or multiply a power by a time to get energy, using data specific to your situation rather than a fixed conversion factor.

Why doesn't the Universal Converter just estimate a value for Rontojoule to Nanowatt?

Guessing a number would be misleading, since Rontojoule and Nanowatt don't share a fixed physical ratio. The calculator only returns a result when both units measure the same quantity, so you can trust every number it does show.

About this conversion

This page compares Rontojoule (rJ) and Nanowatt (nW) within the engineering converter.

Rontojoule is 1e-27 joule units. Nanowatt is 1e-9 watt units.

Because Rontojoule measures energy and Nanowatt measures power, the two units are not directly convertible: there is no fixed ratio that turns one into the other without additional information.

The calculator above will report that these units are not compatible. For a working result, choose two units from the same measurement family — see the related conversions below.

For scale, a typical phone battery stores on the order of 40,000 joules, and a household kettle uses roughly 1,000,000 joules to boil a litre of water. On the power side, a household lightbulb runs around 60 watts, a microwave around 1,000 watts, and a small car engine on the order of 100,000 watts. Seeing both scales side by side is often more useful than a single converted number would be.
