About Converting Kilojoules per Kilogram-Kelvin to BTU per Pound-F

Kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin and BTU per pound-°F are both units of specific heat. One kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin is 1,000 J/(kg·K). One BTU per pound-°F is exactly 4,186.8 J/(kg·K), the International Table definition of specific heat of water.

Formula

BTU/(lb·°F) = kJ/(kg·K) × 0.2388458966

This factor comes from the defined size of each unit: 1 kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin equals 0.2388458966 BTU per pound-°F, and the reverse factor is 4.1868.

Simple example

1 kJ/(kg·K) × 0.2388458966 = 0.2388458966 BTU/(lb·°F)

1 kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin = 0.2388458966 BTU per pound-°F.

Understanding the Kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin

Standard and definition

The kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin is covered by ISO 80000-5 — Quantities and units: thermodynamics.

Uses of the kilojoule per kilogram-kelvin today

  • Steam and refrigerant property tables
  • Engineering thermodynamics

Understanding the BTU per pound-°F

BTU per pound-°F (BTU/(lb·°F)) measures specific heat. One BTU per pound-°F is exactly 4,186.8 J/(kg·K), the International Table definition of specific heat of water.

Convention: International Table BTU, giving exactly 4,186.8 J/(kg·K).

Standard and definition

The BTU per pound-°F is covered by NIST Special Publication 811 — Guide for the Use of the International System of Units (SI).

Uses of the BTU per pound-°F today

  • US specific-heat data and steam tables
  • HVAC and process heat calculations
