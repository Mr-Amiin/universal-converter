Frequently asked questions

How many milligrams are in one metric teaspoon?

One Metric teaspoon equals 5000 milligrams (assuming water-like density).

Why is the metric teaspoon used in this kind of recipe?

Medicine dosing instructions worldwide use the 5 mL metric teaspoon specifically because it's an exact, unambiguous round number.

Is this conversion exact?

Not exactly for every ingredient. This conversion assumes water-like density (1 gram per milliliter), which is accurate for water, milk, and similar liquids, but ingredients like flour, sugar, or oil will weigh a different amount for the same volume because they pack or float differently.

Can I use this conversion for any ingredient?

Use it with care: because it relies on a water-density assumption, it's most accurate for liquids and less accurate for dry or airy ingredients, which vary by how they're packed.

When would a recipe use metric teaspoons instead of milligrams?

Metric teaspoon is typically used in recipes published outside the US, and medicine dosing spoons worldwide, while milligram shows up in measuring nutritional content (sodium, vitamins), food additives, and spice-level seasoning in scientific recipe formulation.
