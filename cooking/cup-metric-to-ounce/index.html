Frequently asked questions

How many ounces are in one metric cup?

One Metric cup equals 8.81849 ounces (assuming water-like density).

Why is the metric cup used in this kind of recipe?

Recipe writers outside the US chose a round 250 mL figure specifically so metric cup measures would divide cleanly, unlike the US customary cup.

Is this conversion exact?

Not exactly for every ingredient. This conversion assumes water-like density (1 gram per milliliter), which is accurate for water, milk, and similar liquids, but ingredients like flour, sugar, or oil will weigh a different amount for the same volume because they pack or float differently.

Can I use this conversion for any ingredient?

Use it with care: because it relies on a water-density assumption, it's most accurate for liquids and less accurate for dry or airy ingredients, which vary by how they're packed.

When would a recipe use metric cups instead of ounces?

Metric cup is typically used in recipes published in Australia, New Zealand, the UK, and much of Europe, while ounce shows up in measuring dry ingredients like meat, cheese, and packaged goods in US recipes and grocery labeling.
