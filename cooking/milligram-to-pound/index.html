Frequently asked questions

How many pounds are in one milligram?

One Milligram equals 2.204623×10^-6 pounds.

Why is the milligram used in this kind of recipe?

Nutrition labels and food-science recipes need the milligram because ingredients like sodium and vitamins are active in quantities too small to sensibly express in grams.

Is this conversion exact?

Yes. Both milligrams and pounds measure the same physical quantity, so this conversion uses a fixed, exact factor with no density assumption involved.

Can I use this conversion for any ingredient?

Yes. Since milligram and pound both measure the same kind of quantity, this conversion applies equally to any ingredient.

When would a recipe use milligrams instead of pounds?

Milligram is typically used in measuring nutritional content (sodium, vitamins), food additives, and spice-level seasoning in scientific recipe formulation, while pound shows up in purchasing and measuring meat, flour, sugar, and other bulk ingredients in the US.
