Frequently asked questions

How many pounds are in one metric tablespoon?

One Metric tablespoon equals 0.033069 pounds (assuming water-like density).

Why is the metric tablespoon used in this kind of recipe?

Metric-system countries round the tablespoon to 15 mL so it converts cleanly with milliliters and liters elsewhere in a recipe.

Is this conversion exact?

Not exactly for every ingredient. This conversion assumes water-like density (1 gram per milliliter), which is accurate for water, milk, and similar liquids, but ingredients like flour, sugar, or oil will weigh a different amount for the same volume because they pack or float differently.

Can I use this conversion for any ingredient?

Use it with care: because it relies on a water-density assumption, it's most accurate for liquids and less accurate for dry or airy ingredients, which vary by how they're packed.

When would a recipe use metric tablespoons instead of pounds?

Metric tablespoon is typically used in recipes published in Australia, the UK, and much of Europe, while pound shows up in purchasing and measuring meat, flour, sugar, and other bulk ingredients in the US.
