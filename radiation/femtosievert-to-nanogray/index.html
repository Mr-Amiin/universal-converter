Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the femtosievert (fSv). Femtosievert is 1e-15 sievert units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the nanogray (nGy). Nanogray is 1e-9 gray units.

How do I convert femtosievert to nanogray?

Multiply the femtosievert value by 1.000000e-06. For example, 5 fSv × 1.000000e-06 = 5.000000e-06 nGy.

Why do femtosievert and nanogray need converting at all?

Femto (f) represents 10^-15, used in particle physics and ultrafast optics. Nano (n) represents 10^-9, familiar from nanotechnology and nanometers.

Is the femtosievert to nanogray conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between femtosievert and nanogray is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e-06), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert nanogray back to femtosievert?

Yes. Multiply the nanogray value by 1000000 (or divide by 1.000000e-06) to get femtosievert.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.

About this conversion

This page converts femtosievert (fSv) into nanogray (nGy) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 fSv = 1.000000e-06 nGy.

Worked example: to convert 10 fSv to nGy, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e-06 = 1.000000e-05 nGy.

A dose of 10 fSv corresponds to about 1.000000e-05 nGy.

Reverse conversion: to go from nanogray back to femtosievert, divide by 1.000000e-06 (equivalently, multiply by 1000000).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.

Femtosievert: Femtosievert is 1e-15 sievert units. Nanogray: Nanogray is 1e-9 gray units.

Background: Femto (f) represents 10^-15, used in particle physics and ultrafast optics.

Note: this conversion treats absorbed dose and equivalent dose as numerically equal, which assumes a radiation weighting factor of 1 (true for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta particles, but not for alpha particles or neutrons, which carry higher weighting factors).
