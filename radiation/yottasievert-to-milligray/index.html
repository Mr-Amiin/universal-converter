Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the yottasievert (YSv). Yottasievert is 1e+24 sievert units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the milligray (mGy). Milligray is 0.001 gray units.

How do I convert yottasievert to milligray?

Multiply the yottasievert value by 1.000000e+27. For example, 5 YSv × 1.000000e+27 = 5.000000e+27 mGy.

Why do yottasievert and milligray need converting at all?

Yotta (Y) represents 10^24, historically the largest SI prefix before 2022. Milli (m) represents 10^-3, one of the most common small-scale SI prefixes.

Is the yottasievert to milligray conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between yottasievert and milligray is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+27), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert milligray back to yottasievert?

Yes. Multiply the milligray value by 1.000000e-27 (or divide by 1.000000e+27) to get yottasievert.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.

About this conversion

This page converts yottasievert (YSv) into milligray (mGy) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 YSv = 1.000000e+27 mGy.

Worked example: to convert 10 YSv to mGy, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e+27 = 1.000000e+28 mGy.

A dose of 10 YSv corresponds to about 1.000000e+28 mGy.

Reverse conversion: to go from milligray back to yottasievert, divide by 1.000000e+27 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e-27).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.

Yottasievert: Yottasievert is 1e+24 sievert units. Milligray: Milligray is 0.001 gray units.

Background: Yotta (Y) represents 10^24, historically the largest SI prefix before 2022.

Note: this conversion treats absorbed dose and equivalent dose as numerically equal, which assumes a radiation weighting factor of 1 (true for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta particles, but not for alpha particles or neutrons, which carry higher weighting factors).
