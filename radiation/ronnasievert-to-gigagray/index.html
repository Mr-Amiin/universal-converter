Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the ronnasievert (RSv). Ronnasievert is 1e+27 sievert units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the gigagray (GGy). Gigagray is 1000000000 gray units.

How do I convert ronnasievert to gigagray?

Multiply the ronnasievert value by 1.000000e+18. For example, 5 RSv × 1.000000e+18 = 5.000000e+18 GGy.

Why do ronnasievert and gigagray need converting at all?

Ronna (R) represents 10^27 and is one of the newest SI prefixes, adopted in 2022. Giga (G) represents 10^9, familiar from gigabyte and gigahertz measurements.

Is the ronnasievert to gigagray conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between ronnasievert and gigagray is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+18), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert gigagray back to ronnasievert?

Yes. Multiply the gigagray value by 1.000000e-18 (or divide by 1.000000e+18) to get ronnasievert.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.
