Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the gray (Gy). Gray is absorbed dose in joules per kilogram.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the rad (rad). Rad is 0.01 gray.

How do I convert gray to rad?

Multiply the gray value by 100. For example, 5 Gy × 100 = 500 rad.

Why do gray and rad need converting at all?

The gray is the SI unit of absorbed radiation dose, equal to one joule of energy absorbed per kilogram of matter. The rad, from the older CGS system, equals 0.01 gray and is still used in some US medical contexts.

Is the gray to rad conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between gray and rad is a fixed, defined ratio (× 100), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert rad back to gray?

Yes. Multiply the rad value by 0.01 (or divide by 100) to get gray.

About this conversion

This page converts gray (Gy) into rad (rad) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 Gy = 100 rad.

Worked example: to convert 10 Gy to rad, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 100 = 1000 rad.

A dose of 10 Gy corresponds to about 1000 rad.

Reverse conversion: to go from rad back to gray, divide by 100 (equivalently, multiply by 0.01).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.
