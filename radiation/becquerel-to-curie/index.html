Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the becquerel (Bq). Becquerel is one radioactive decay per second.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the curie (Ci). Curie is 3.7e10 becquerels.

How do I convert becquerel to curie?

Multiply the becquerel value by 2.702703e-11. For example, 5 Bq × 2.702703e-11 = 1.351351e-10 Ci.

Why do becquerel and curie need converting at all?

The becquerel measures radioactivity as one nuclear decay per second, distinct from absorbed or equivalent dose. The curie, an older activity unit, is defined as exactly 3.7×10^10 decays per second.

Is the becquerel to curie conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between becquerel and curie is a fixed, defined ratio (× 2.702703e-11), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert curie back to becquerel?

Yes. Multiply the curie value by 37000000037 (or divide by 2.702703e-11) to get becquerel.

About this conversion

This page converts becquerel (Bq) into curie (Ci) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 Bq = 2.702703e-11 Ci.

Worked example: to convert 10 Bq to Ci, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 2.702703e-11 = 2.702703e-10 Ci.

A dose of 10 Bq corresponds to about 2.702703e-10 Ci.

Reverse conversion: to go from curie back to becquerel, divide by 2.702703e-11 (equivalently, multiply by 37000000037).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.
