Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the yoctosievert (ySv). Yoctosievert is 1e-24 sievert units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the zettagray (ZGy). Zettagray is 1e+21 gray units.

How do I convert yoctosievert to zettagray?

Multiply the yoctosievert value by 1.000000e-45. For example, 5 ySv × 1.000000e-45 = 5.000000e-45 ZGy.

Why do yoctosievert and zettagray need converting at all?

Yocto (y) represents 10^-24, historically the smallest SI prefix before 2022. Zetta (Z) represents 10^21 and is mainly used for extremely large scientific quantities.

Is the yoctosievert to zettagray conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between yoctosievert and zettagray is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e-45), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert zettagray back to yoctosievert?

Yes. Multiply the zettagray value by 1.000000e+45 (or divide by 1.000000e-45) to get yoctosievert.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.

About this conversion

Worked example: to convert 10 ySv to ZGy, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e-45 = 1.000000e-44 ZGy.

A dose of 10 ySv corresponds to about 1.000000e-44 ZGy.

Reverse conversion: to go from zettagray back to yoctosievert, divide by 1.000000e-45 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e+45).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.

Yoctosievert: Yoctosievert is 1e-24 sievert units. Zettagray: Zettagray is 1e+21 gray units.

Background: Yocto (y) represents 10^-24, historically the smallest SI prefix before 2022.

Note: this conversion treats absorbed dose and equivalent dose as numerically equal, which assumes a radiation weighting factor of 1 (true for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta particles, but not for alpha particles or neutrons, which carry higher weighting factors).
