Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the hectogray (hGy). Hectogray is 100 gray units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the zeptosievert (zSv). Zeptosievert is 1e-21 sievert units.

How do I convert hectogray to zeptosievert?

Multiply the hectogray value by 1.000000e+23. For example, 5 hGy × 1.000000e+23 = 5.000000e+23 zSv.

Why do hectogray and zeptosievert need converting at all?

Hecto (h) represents 10^2 and is rarely used outside the hectare and hectopascal. Zepto (z) represents 10^-21, one of the smallest SI prefixes in practical use.

Is the hectogray to zeptosievert conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between hectogray and zeptosievert is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+23), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert zeptosievert back to hectogray?

Yes. Multiply the zeptosievert value by 1.000000e-23 (or divide by 1.000000e+23) to get hectogray.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.

About this conversion

This page converts hectogray (hGy) into zeptosievert (zSv) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 hGy = 1.000000e+23 zSv.

Worked example: to convert 10 hGy to zSv, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e+23 = 1.000000e+24 zSv.

A dose of 10 hGy corresponds to about 1.000000e+24 zSv.

Reverse conversion: to go from zeptosievert back to hectogray, divide by 1.000000e+23 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e-23).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.

Hectogray: Hectogray is 100 gray units. Zeptosievert: Zeptosievert is 1e-21 sievert units.

Background: Hecto (h) represents 10^2 and is rarely used outside the hectare and hectopascal.

Note: this conversion treats absorbed dose and equivalent dose as numerically equal, which assumes a radiation weighting factor of 1 (true for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta particles, but not for alpha particles or neutrons, which carry higher weighting factors).
