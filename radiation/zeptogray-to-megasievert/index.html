Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the zeptogray (zGy). Zeptogray is 1e-21 gray units.

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the megasievert (MSv). Megasievert is 1000000 sievert units.

How do I convert zeptogray to megasievert?

Multiply the zeptogray value by 1.000000e-27. For example, 5 zGy × 1.000000e-27 = 5.000000e-27 MSv.

Why do zeptogray and megasievert need converting at all?

Zepto (z) represents 10^-21, one of the smallest SI prefixes in practical use. Mega (M) represents 10^6, one of the most commonly used SI prefixes.

Is the zeptogray to megasievert conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between zeptogray and megasievert is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e-27), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert megasievert back to zeptogray?

Yes. Multiply the megasievert value by 1.000000e+27 (or divide by 1.000000e-27) to get zeptogray.

Does this conversion always hold exactly?

Only when the radiation weighting factor is 1, as it is for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta radiation. For alpha particles or neutrons, the equivalent dose in sieverts is higher than the absorbed dose in grays by that factor.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptogray (zGy) into megasievert (MSv) within the radiation converter, using the fixed relationship 1 zGy = 1.000000e-27 MSv.

Worked example: to convert 10 zGy to MSv, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e-27 = 1.000000e-26 MSv.

A dose of 10 zGy corresponds to about 1.000000e-26 MSv.

Reverse conversion: to go from megasievert back to zeptogray, divide by 1.000000e-27 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e+27).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.

Zeptogray: Zeptogray is 1e-21 gray units. Megasievert: Megasievert is 1000000 sievert units.

Background: Zepto (z) represents 10^-21, one of the smallest SI prefixes in practical use.

Note: this conversion treats absorbed dose and equivalent dose as numerically equal, which assumes a radiation weighting factor of 1 (true for X-rays, gamma rays, and beta particles, but not for alpha particles or neutrons, which carry higher weighting factors).
