About Converting Coulombs per Kilogram to Roentgens

Coulomb per kilogram and roentgen are both units of exposure. One coulomb per kilogram is the SI unit of radiation exposure. The roentgen is the unit of ionizing-radiation exposure in air: exactly 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ coulombs per kilogram.

Formula

R = C/kg × 3875.968992

This factor comes from the defined size of each unit: 1 coulomb per kilogram equals 3875.968992 roentgens, and the reverse factor is 0.000258.

Simple example

1 C/kg × 3875.968992 = 3875.968992 R

1 coulomb per kilogram = 3875.968992 roentgens.

Real-world example

10 C/kg × 3875.968992 = 38759.68992 R

10 coulombs per kilogram = 38759.68992 roentgens.

Understanding the Roentgen

Roentgen (R) measures exposure. The roentgen is the unit of ionizing-radiation exposure in air: exactly 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ coulombs per kilogram.

Convention: The roentgen measures exposure (ionization in air), not absorbed or equivalent dose, so it does not convert directly to gray or sievert.

Standard and definition

The roentgen is covered by ICRU Report 33 — Radiation Quantities and Units.

Uses of the roentgen today

  • Exposure readings from older survey meters
  • Historic X-ray and gamma exposure limits
