Formula

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert pebibytes to megabytes?

Multiply the pebibyte value by 1,125,899,906.842624. For example, 4 PiB × 1,125,899,906.842624 = 4,503,599,627.370496 MB.

How do I convert megabytes back to pebibytes?

Divide the megabyte value by 1,125,899,906.842624, which is the same as multiplying by 8.881784 × 10-10.

Is the pebibyte to megabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing enterprise data centers, cloud storage platforms, large scientific datasets.

When would I use megabytes instead of pebibytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog is usually described in pebibytes, while a compressed photo or about a minute of streamed music reads more naturally in megabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts pebibytes (PiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 250 bytes, into megabytes (MB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 106 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing enterprise data centers and photos and short audio clips.

The formula is MB = PiB × 1,125,899,906.842624, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “pebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998. The “mega” prefix was adopted by the British Association for the Advancement of Science, standardized in 1873.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
