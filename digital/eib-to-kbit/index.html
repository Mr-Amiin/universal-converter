Formula

Kb = EiB × 9.223372 × 1015

EiB = Kb ÷ 9.223372 × 1015

Exact under each unit's own definition, but note EiB is binary (IEC) while Kb is decimal (SI), so the two prefix families diverge at larger scales.

Worked example

Picture worldwide internet traffic in a single day, sized at about 5 EiB. Using the formula above: 5 EiB × 9.223372 × 1015 = 4.611686 × 1016 Kb.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert exbibytes to kilobits?

Multiply the exbibyte value by 9.223372 × 1015. For example, 4 EiB × 9.223372 × 1015 = 3.689349 × 1016 Kb.

How do I convert kilobits back to exbibytes?

Divide the kilobit value by 9.223372 × 1015, which is the same as multiplying by 1.084202 × 10-16.

Why does it matter whether this is measured in bits or bytes?

A byte is defined as exactly 8 bits, so every bit–byte conversion involves that fixed ×8 factor. Bits are the standard for network and internet connection speeds (e.g. Mbps), while bytes are the standard for file sizes and storage capacity — which is why a fast “Mb” connection and a large “MB” file are easy to mix up.

Is the exbibyte to kilobit conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing national internet traffic measurements, large cloud provider capacity, genomic and physics datasets.

When would I use kilobits instead of exbibytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: roughly the scale of all data crossing the internet in a single day worldwide is usually described in exbibytes, while a short text email or a single page of plain text reads more naturally in kilobits.

About this conversion

This page converts exbibytes (EiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 260 bytes, into kilobits (Kb), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 103 bits.

This conversion is used when comparing national internet traffic measurements and short documents and emails.

The formula is Kb = EiB × 9.223372 × 1015, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “exbi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998. The “kilo” prefix was part of the original French metric system, standardized in 1795.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
