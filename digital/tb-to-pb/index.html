Frequently asked questions

How do I convert terabytes to petabytes?

Multiply the terabyte value by 0.001. For example, 4 TB × 0.001 = 0.004 PB.

How do I convert petabytes back to terabytes?

Divide the petabyte value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the terabyte to petabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing external hard drives, small server storage, surveillance camera archives.

When would I use petabytes instead of terabytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: an external hard drive, or a small business's shared file server is usually described in terabytes, while a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog reads more naturally in petabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts terabytes (TB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1012 bytes, into petabytes (PB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1015 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing external hard drives and enterprise data centers.

The formula is PB = TB × 0.001, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “tera” prefix was formalized alongside giga at the 11th CGPM, standardized in 1960. The “peta” prefix was added at the 15th CGPM as computing needs grew, standardized in 1975.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
