Frequently asked questions

How do I convert gigabits to gigabytes?

Multiply the gigabit value by 0.125. For example, 4 Gb × 0.125 = 0.5 GB.

How do I convert gigabytes back to gigabits?

Divide the gigabyte value by 0.125, which is the same as multiplying by 8.

Why does it matter whether this is measured in bits or bytes?

A byte is defined as exactly 8 bits, so every bit–byte conversion involves that fixed ×8 factor. Bits are the standard for network and internet connection speeds (e.g. Mbps), while bytes are the standard for file sizes and storage capacity — which is why a fast “Mb” connection and a large “MB” file are easy to mix up.

Is the gigabit to gigabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing HD video files, phone and laptop storage tiers, photo and music libraries.

When would I use gigabytes instead of gigabits?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a two-hour HD movie download, or a phone's entire photo library is usually described in gigabits, while a two-hour HD movie download, or a phone's entire photo library reads more naturally in gigabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts gigabits (Gb), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 109 bits, into gigabytes (GB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 109 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing HD video files and HD video files.

The formula is GB = Gb × 0.125, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
