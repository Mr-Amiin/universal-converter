Formula

PiB = Kb × 1.110223 × 10-13

Kb = PiB ÷ 1.110223 × 10-13

Exact under each unit's own definition, but note Kb is decimal (SI) while PiB is binary (IEC), so the two prefix families diverge at larger scales.

Worked example

Picture a short plain-text email, sized at about 5 Kb. Using the formula above: 5 Kb × 1.110223 × 10-13 = 5.551115 × 10-13 PiB.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kilobits to pebibytes?

Multiply the kilobit value by 1.110223 × 10-13. For example, 4 Kb × 1.110223 × 10-13 = 4.440892 × 10-13 PiB.

How do I convert pebibytes back to kilobits?

Divide the pebibyte value by 1.110223 × 10-13, which is the same as multiplying by 9,007,199,254,740.992.

Why does it matter whether this is measured in bits or bytes?

A byte is defined as exactly 8 bits, so every bit–byte conversion involves that fixed ×8 factor. Bits are the standard for network and internet connection speeds (e.g. Mbps), while bytes are the standard for file sizes and storage capacity — which is why a fast “Mb” connection and a large “MB” file are easy to mix up.

Is the kilobit to pebibyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing short documents and emails, configuration files, compressed log entries.

When would I use pebibytes instead of kilobits?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a short text email or a single page of plain text is usually described in kilobits, while a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog reads more naturally in pebibytes.

About this conversion

This page converts kilobits (Kb), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 103 bits, into pebibytes (PiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 250 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing short documents and emails and enterprise data centers.

The formula is PiB = Kb × 1.110223 × 10-13, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “kilo” prefix was part of the original French metric system, standardized in 1795. The “pebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
