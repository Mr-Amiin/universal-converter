Frequently asked questions

How do I convert gigabytes to terabytes?

Multiply the gigabyte value by 0.001. For example, 4 GB × 0.001 = 0.004 TB.

How do I convert terabytes back to gigabytes?

Divide the terabyte value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the gigabyte to terabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing HD video files, phone and laptop storage tiers, photo and music libraries.

When would I use terabytes instead of gigabytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a two-hour HD movie download, or a phone's entire photo library is usually described in gigabytes, while an external hard drive, or a small business's shared file server reads more naturally in terabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts gigabytes (GB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 109 bytes, into terabytes (TB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1012 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing HD video files and external hard drives.

The formula is TB = GB × 0.001, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “giga” prefix was formalized at the 11th General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM), standardized in 1960. The “tera” prefix was formalized alongside giga at the 11th CGPM, standardized in 1960.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
