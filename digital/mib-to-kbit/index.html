Formula

Kb = MiB × 8,388.608

MiB = Kb ÷ 8,388.608

Exact under each unit's own definition, but note MiB is binary (IEC) while Kb is decimal (SI), so the two prefix families diverge at larger scales.

Worked example

Picture a compressed smartphone photo, sized at about 4 MiB. Using the formula above: 4 MiB × 8,388.608 = 33,554.432 Kb.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert mebibytes to kilobits?

Multiply the mebibyte value by 8,388.608. For example, 4 MiB × 8,388.608 = 33,554.432 Kb.

How do I convert kilobits back to mebibytes?

Divide the kilobit value by 8,388.608, which is the same as multiplying by 0.00011920928955078125.

Why does it matter whether this is measured in bits or bytes?

A byte is defined as exactly 8 bits, so every bit–byte conversion involves that fixed ×8 factor. Bits are the standard for network and internet connection speeds (e.g. Mbps), while bytes are the standard for file sizes and storage capacity — which is why a fast “Mb” connection and a large “MB” file are easy to mix up.

Is the mebibyte to kilobit conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing photos and short audio clips, web page assets, mobile app downloads.

When would I use kilobits instead of mebibytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a compressed photo or about a minute of streamed music is usually described in mebibytes, while a short text email or a single page of plain text reads more naturally in kilobits.

About this conversion

This page converts mebibytes (MiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 220 bytes, into kilobits (Kb), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 103 bits.

This conversion is used when comparing photos and short audio clips and short documents and emails.

The formula is Kb = MiB × 8,388.608, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “mebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998. The “kilo” prefix was part of the original French metric system, standardized in 1795.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
