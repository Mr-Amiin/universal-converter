Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yottabytes to gibibytes?

Multiply the yottabyte value by 931,322,574,615,478.515625. For example, 4 YB × 931,322,574,615,478.515625 = 3.72529 × 1015 GiB.

How do I convert gibibytes back to yottabytes?

Divide the gibibyte value by 931,322,574,615,478.515625, which is the same as multiplying by 1.073742 × 10-15.

Why do we need a unit as large as the gibibyte?

The gibibyte (GiB) exists because a two-hour HD movie download, or a phone's entire photo library. At that scale, smaller units like gigabytes or terabytes would require unwieldy strings of digits, so the larger prefix keeps the numbers readable.

Is the yottabyte to gibibyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing theoretical/future-scale data projections, long-range technology forecasting, HD video files.

When would I use gibibytes instead of yottabytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: far beyond any data volume humanity currently produces in a year is usually described in yottabytes, while a two-hour HD movie download, or a phone's entire photo library reads more naturally in gibibytes.

About this conversion

This page converts yottabytes (YB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1024 bytes, into gibibytes (GiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 230 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing theoretical/future-scale data projections and HD video files.

The formula is GiB = YB × 931,322,574,615,478.515625, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “yotta” prefix was added alongside zetta at the 19th CGPM, standardized in 1991. The “gibi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
