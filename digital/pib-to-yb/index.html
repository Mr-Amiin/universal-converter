Frequently asked questions

How do I convert pebibytes to yottabytes?

Multiply the pebibyte value by 1.1259 × 10-9. For example, 4 PiB × 1.1259 × 10-9 = 4.5036 × 10-9 YB.

How do I convert yottabytes back to pebibytes?

Divide the yottabyte value by 1.1259 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 888,178,419.70012523233890533447265625.

Why do we need a unit as large as the pebibyte?

The pebibyte (PiB) exists because a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog. At that scale, smaller units like gigabytes or terabytes would require unwieldy strings of digits, so the larger prefix keeps the numbers readable.

Is the pebibyte to yottabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing enterprise data centers, cloud storage platforms, large scientific datasets.

When would I use yottabytes instead of pebibytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog is usually described in pebibytes, while far beyond any data volume humanity currently produces in a year reads more naturally in yottabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts pebibytes (PiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 250 bytes, into yottabytes (YB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1024 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing enterprise data centers and theoretical/future-scale data projections.

The formula is YB = PiB × 1.1259 × 10-9, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “pebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998. The “yotta” prefix was added alongside zetta at the 19th CGPM, standardized in 1991.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
