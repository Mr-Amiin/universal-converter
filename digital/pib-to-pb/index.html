Frequently asked questions

How do I convert pebibytes to petabytes?

Multiply the pebibyte value by 1.125899906842624. For example, 4 PiB × 1.125899906842624 = 4.503599627370496 PB.

How do I convert petabytes back to pebibytes?

Divide the petabyte value by 1.125899906842624, which is the same as multiplying by 0.88817841970012523233890533447265625.

Why isn't 1 petabyte the same as 1 pebibyte?

The petabyte (PB) uses a power-of-10 base (1015), while the pebibyte (PiB) uses a power-of-2 base (250). They diverge as the prefix grows — at this tier the binary unit is about 12.59% larger, which is why operating systems (binary) and drive manufacturers (decimal) can report different sizes for the same file or disk.

Is the pebibyte to petabyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing enterprise data centers, cloud storage platforms, large scientific datasets.

When would I use petabytes instead of pebibytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog is usually described in pebibytes, while a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog reads more naturally in petabytes.

About this conversion

This page converts pebibytes (PiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 250 bytes, into petabytes (PB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1015 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing enterprise data centers and enterprise data centers.

The formula is PB = PiB × 1.125899906842624, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “pebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998. The “peta” prefix was added at the 15th CGPM as computing needs grew, standardized in 1975.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
