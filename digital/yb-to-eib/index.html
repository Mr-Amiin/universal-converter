How do I convert exbibytes back to yottabytes?

Why do we need a unit as large as the exbibyte?

The exbibyte (EiB) exists because roughly the scale of all data crossing the internet in a single day worldwide. At that scale, smaller units like gigabytes or terabytes would require unwieldy strings of digits, so the larger prefix keeps the numbers readable.

Is the yottabyte to exbibyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

When would I use exbibytes instead of yottabytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: far beyond any data volume humanity currently produces in a year is usually described in yottabytes, while roughly the scale of all data crossing the internet in a single day worldwide reads more naturally in exbibytes.

About this conversion

This page converts yottabytes (YB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1024 bytes, into exbibytes (EiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 260 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing theoretical/future-scale data projections and national internet traffic measurements.

The formula is EiB = YB × 867,361.737988403547205962240695953369140625, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “yotta” prefix was added alongside zetta at the 19th CGPM, standardized in 1991. The “exbi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
