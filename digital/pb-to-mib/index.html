Frequently asked questions

How do I convert petabytes to mebibytes?

Multiply the petabyte value by 953,674,316.40625. For example, 4 PB × 953,674,316.40625 = 3,814,697,265.625 MiB.

How do I convert mebibytes back to petabytes?

Divide the mebibyte value by 953,674,316.40625, which is the same as multiplying by 1.048576 × 10-9.

Is the petabyte to mebibyte conversion exact?

Yes. Both units use fixed, defined prefixes (decimal and/or binary), so the conversion factor is exact rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation, though remember decimal and binary prefixes diverge further apart as the scale grows.

Where is this conversion used in practice?

It comes up when comparing enterprise data centers, cloud storage platforms, large scientific datasets.

When would I use mebibytes instead of petabytes?

Use whichever unit keeps the number easiest to read for the context: a mid-size corporate data center, or a major streaming service's video catalog is usually described in petabytes, while a compressed photo or about a minute of streamed music reads more naturally in mebibytes.

About this conversion

This page converts petabytes (PB), a decimal (SI) unit of digital storage equal to 1015 bytes, into mebibytes (MiB), a binary (IEC) unit of digital storage equal to 220 bytes.

This conversion is used when comparing enterprise data centers and photos and short audio clips.

The formula is MiB = PB × 953,674,316.40625, based on the fixed relationship between these two digital storage units.

The “peta” prefix was added at the 15th CGPM as computing needs grew, standardized in 1975. The “mebi” binary prefix was introduced by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC 60027-2) to remove the ambiguity of reusing decimal SI prefixes for binary, power-of-two computer quantities, standardized in 1998.

Digital storage prefixes follow the International System of Units (SI) for decimal units and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) recommendations for binary units, keeping decimal and binary quantities unambiguous.
