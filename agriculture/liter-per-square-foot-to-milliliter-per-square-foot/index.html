Frequently asked questions

How do I convert liters per square foot to milliliters per square foot?

Multiply the liters per square foot value by 1000 to get milliliters per square foot. For example, 1 L/ft2 = 1000 mL/ft2.

What is the formula for liters per square foot to milliliters per square foot?

Use the formula: value in mL/ft2 = value in L/ft2 × 1000. To convert back, value in L/ft2 = value in mL/ft2 ÷ 1000.

Is the liters per square foot to milliliters per square foot conversion exact?

Yes. Both units belong to the same liquid application rate family, so the factor 1000 gives an exact, direct conversion.

Where is the liters per square foot unit commonly used?

It is commonly used in agriculture and agronomy for liquid application rate calculations such as field records, fertilizer and seed planning, or irrigation and yield tracking.

What is liters per square foot used for in farming?

It's most often seen in liquid input application, for example metric sprayer calibration. Converting it to milliliters per square foot makes it possible to compare that figure against records, labels, or equipment calibrated in a different unit system.

Why does this conversion involve square foot?

Square foot is a small-plot or garden-scale area unit, so it shows up whenever a rate needs to be reported per unit of land area rather than as a total quantity.
