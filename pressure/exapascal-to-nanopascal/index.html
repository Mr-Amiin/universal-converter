Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the exapascal (EPa).

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the nanopascal (nPa).

How do I convert exapascal to nanopascal?

Multiply the exapascal value by 1.000000e+27. For example, 5 EPa × 1.000000e+27 = 5.000000e+27 nPa.

Why do exapascal and nanopascal need converting at all?

Exa (E) represents 10^18, common in describing large-scale data and energy quantities. Nano (n) represents 10^-9, familiar from nanotechnology and nanometers.

Is the exapascal to nanopascal conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between exapascal and nanopascal is a fixed, defined ratio (× 1.000000e+27), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert nanopascal back to exapascal?

Yes. Multiply the nanopascal value by 1.000000e-27 (or divide by 1.000000e+27) to get exapascal.

About this conversion

Worked example: to convert 10 EPa to nPa, multiply by the conversion factor: 10 × 1.000000e+27 = 1.000000e+28 nPa.

A car tire inflated to 32 EPa is at about 3.200000e+28 nPa.

Reverse conversion: to go from nanopascal back to exapascal, divide by 1.000000e+27 (equivalently, multiply by 1.000000e-27).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.
