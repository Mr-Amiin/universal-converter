How the formula works

Both megahenry and petahenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in megahenrys by 1.000000e-09 to get petahenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petahenry value by 1.000000e+09 to get back to megahenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert megahenrys to petahenrys?

Multiply the megahenry value by 1 × 10-9. For example, 4 MH × 1 × 10-9 = 4 × 10-9 PH.

How do I convert petahenrys back to megahenrys?

Divide the petahenry value by 1 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000.

Is the megahenry to petahenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petahenry?

The petahenry (PH) mostly shows up in theoretical inductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a megahenry used for?

The megahenry (MH) is commonly encountered in large industrial transformer and grid-inductor scales.

About this conversion

This page converts megahenrys (MH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 106 henrys (megahenry = mega + henry), into petahenrys (PH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1015 henrys (petahenry = peta + henry).

This conversion is used when working with large industrial transformer and grid-inductor scales.

The formula is PH = MH × 1 × 10-9, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “mega” prefix (M-, 106) was standardized in 1873.
