How the formula works

Both nanohenry and attohenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in nanohenrys by 1.000000e+09 to get attohenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a attohenry value by 1.000000e-09 to get back to nanohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert nanohenrys to attohenrys?

Multiply the nanohenry value by 1,000,000,000. For example, 4 nH × 1,000,000,000 = 4,000,000,000 aH.

How do I convert attohenrys back to nanohenrys?

Divide the attohenry value by 1,000,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-9.

Is the nanohenry to attohenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attohenry?

The attohenry (aH) shows up in the parasitic inductance of a short wire, where the base henry would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a nanohenry used for?

The nanohenry (nH) is commonly encountered in small signal inductors and coils.

About this conversion

This page converts nanohenrys (nH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-9 henrys (nanohenry = nano + henry), into attohenrys (aH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-18 henrys (attohenry = atto + henry).

This conversion is used when working with small signal inductors and coils.

The formula is aH = nH × 1,000,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “nano” prefix (n-, 10-9) was standardized in 1960.
