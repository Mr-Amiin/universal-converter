How the formula works

Both centiwatt and yoctowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in centiwatts by 1.000000e+22 to get yoctowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctowatt value by 1.000000e-22 to get back to centiwatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert centiwatts to yoctowatts?

Multiply the centiwatt value by 1 × 1022. For example, 4 cW × 1 × 1022 = 4 × 1022 yW.

How do I convert yoctowatts back to centiwatts?

Divide the yoctowatt value by 1 × 1022, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-22.

Is the centiwatt to yoctowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctowatt?

The yoctowatt (yW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a centiwatt used for?

The centiwatt (cW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts centiwatts (cW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-2 watts (centiwatt = centi + watt), into yoctowatts (yW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-24 watts (yoctowatt = yocto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is yW = cW × 1 × 1022, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “centi” prefix (c-, 10-2) was standardized in 1795.
