How the formula works

Both decawatt and quectowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decawatts by 1.000000e+31 to get quectowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quectowatt value by 1.000000e-31 to get back to decawatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decawatts to quectowatts?

Multiply the decawatt value by 1 × 1031. For example, 4 daW × 1 × 1031 = 4 × 1031 qW.

How do I convert quectowatts back to decawatts?

Divide the quectowatt value by 1 × 1031, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-31.

Is the decawatt to quectowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the quectowatt?

The quectowatt (qW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a decawatt used for?

The decawatt (daW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts decawatts (daW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 101 watts (decawatt = deca + watt), into quectowatts (qW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-30 watts (quectowatt = quecto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is qW = daW × 1 × 1031, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “deca” prefix (da-, 101) was standardized in 1795.
