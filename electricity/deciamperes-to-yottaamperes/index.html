How the formula works

Both deciampere and yottaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in deciamperes by 1.000000e-25 to get yottaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottaampere value by 1.000000e+25 to get back to deciamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert deciamperes to yottaamperes?

Multiply the deciampere value by 1 × 10-25. For example, 4 dA × 1 × 10-25 = 4 × 10-25 YA.

How do I convert yottaamperes back to deciamperes?

Divide the yottaampere value by 1 × 10-25, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1025.

Is the deciampere to yottaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottaampere?

The yottaampere (YA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a deciampere used for?

The deciampere (dA) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts deciamperes (dA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-1 amperes (deciampere = deci + ampere), into yottaamperes (YA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1024 amperes (yottaampere = yotta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is YA = dA × 1 × 10-25, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
