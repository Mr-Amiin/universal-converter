How the formula works

Both decawatt and gigawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decawatts by 1.000000e-08 to get gigawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a gigawatt value by 1.000000e+08 to get back to decawatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decawatts to gigawatts?

Multiply the decawatt value by 1 × 10-8. For example, 4 daW × 1 × 10-8 = 4 × 10-8 GW.

How do I convert gigawatts back to decawatts?

Divide the gigawatt value by 1 × 10-8, which is the same as multiplying by 100,000,000.

Is the decawatt to gigawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a decawatt used for?

The decawatt (daW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts decawatts (daW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 101 watts (decawatt = deca + watt), into gigawatts (GW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 109 watts (gigawatt = giga + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is GW = daW × 1 × 10-8, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “deca” prefix (da-, 101) was standardized in 1795.
