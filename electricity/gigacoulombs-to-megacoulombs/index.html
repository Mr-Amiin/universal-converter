How the formula works

Both gigacoulomb and megacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in gigacoulombs by 1000 to get megacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megacoulomb value by 0.001 to get back to gigacoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert gigacoulombs to megacoulombs?

Multiply the gigacoulomb value by 1,000. For example, 4 GC × 1,000 = 4,000 MC.

How do I convert megacoulombs back to gigacoulombs?

Divide the megacoulomb value by 1,000, which is the same as multiplying by 0.001.

Is the gigacoulomb to megacoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a gigacoulomb used for?

The gigacoulomb (GC) is commonly encountered in large battery-bank and lightning-strike charge scales.

About this conversion

This page converts gigacoulombs (GC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 109 coulombs (gigacoulomb = giga + coulomb), into megacoulombs (MC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 106 coulombs (megacoulomb = mega + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with large battery-bank and lightning-strike charge scales.

The formula is MC = GC × 1,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “giga” prefix (G-, 109) was standardized in 1960.
