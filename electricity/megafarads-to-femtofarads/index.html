How the formula works

Both megafarad and femtofarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in megafarads by 1.000000e+21 to get femtofarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a femtofarad value by 1.000000e-21 to get back to megafarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert megafarads to femtofarads?

Multiply the megafarad value by 1 × 1021. For example, 4 MF × 1 × 1021 = 4 × 1021 fF.

How do I convert femtofarads back to megafarads?

Divide the femtofarad value by 1 × 1021, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-21.

Is the megafarad to femtofarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtofarad?

The femtofarad (fF) shows up in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces, where the base farad would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a megafarad used for?

The megafarad (MF) is commonly encountered in large energy-storage supercapacitor banks.

About this conversion

This page converts megafarads (MF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 106 farads (megafarad = mega + farad), into femtofarads (fF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-15 farads (femtofarad = femto + farad).

This conversion is used when working with large energy-storage supercapacitor banks.

The formula is fF = MF × 1 × 1021, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “mega” prefix (M-, 106) was standardized in 1873.
