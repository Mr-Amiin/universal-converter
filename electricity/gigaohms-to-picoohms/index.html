How the formula works

Both gigaohm and picoohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in gigaohms by 1.000000e+21 to get picoohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picoohm value by 1.000000e-21 to get back to gigaohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert gigaohms to picoohms?

Multiply the gigaohm value by 1 × 1021. For example, 4 GΩ × 1 × 1021 = 4 × 1021 pΩ.

How do I convert picoohms back to gigaohms?

Divide the picoohm value by 1 × 1021, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-21.

Is the gigaohm to picoohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a gigaohm used for?

The gigaohm (GΩ) is commonly encountered in pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts gigaohms (GΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 109 ohms (gigaohm = giga + ohm), into picoohms (pΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-12 ohms (picoohm = pico + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

The formula is pΩ = GΩ × 1 × 1021, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “giga” prefix (G-, 109) was standardized in 1960.
