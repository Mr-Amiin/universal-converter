How the formula works

Both picocoulomb and gigacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in picocoulombs by 1.000000e-21 to get gigacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a gigacoulomb value by 1.000000e+21 to get back to picocoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert picocoulombs to gigacoulombs?

Multiply the picocoulomb value by 1 × 10-21. For example, 4 pC × 1 × 10-21 = 4 × 10-21 GC.

How do I convert gigacoulombs back to picocoulombs?

Divide the gigacoulomb value by 1 × 10-21, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1021.

Is the picocoulomb to gigacoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a picocoulomb used for?

The picocoulomb (pC) is commonly encountered in the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

About this conversion

This page converts picocoulombs (pC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-12 coulombs (picocoulomb = pico + coulomb), into gigacoulombs (GC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 109 coulombs (gigacoulomb = giga + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

The formula is GC = pC × 1 × 10-21, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “pico” prefix (p-, 10-12) was standardized in 1960.
