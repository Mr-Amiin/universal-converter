How the formula works

Both milliohm and decaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliohms by 0.0001 to get decaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decaohm value by 10000 to get back to milliohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliohms to decaohms?

Multiply the milliohm value by 0.0001. For example, 4 mΩ × 0.0001 = 0.0004 daΩ.

How do I convert decaohms back to milliohms?

Divide the decaohm value by 0.0001, which is the same as multiplying by 10,000.

Is the milliohm to decaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a milliohm used for?

The milliohm (mΩ) is commonly encountered in wire and PCB trace resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts milliohms (mΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-3 ohms (milliohm = milli + ohm), into decaohms (daΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 101 ohms (decaohm = deca + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with wire and PCB trace resistances.

The formula is daΩ = mΩ × 0.0001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
