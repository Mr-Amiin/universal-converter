How the formula works

Both centivolt and petavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in centivolts by 1.000000e-17 to get petavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petavolt value by 1.000000e+17 to get back to centivolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert centivolts to petavolts?

Multiply the centivolt value by 1 × 10-17. For example, 4 cV × 1 × 10-17 = 4 × 10-17 PV.

How do I convert petavolts back to centivolts?

Divide the petavolt value by 1 × 10-17, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1017.

Is the centivolt to petavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petavolt?

The petavolt (PV) mostly shows up in theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system, where using the base volt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a centivolt used for?

The centivolt (cV) is commonly encountered in household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

About this conversion

This page converts centivolts (cV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-2 volts (centivolt = centi + volt), into petavolts (PV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1015 volts (petavolt = peta + volt).

This conversion is used when working with household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

The formula is PV = cV × 1 × 10-17, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “centi” prefix (c-, 10-2) was standardized in 1795.
