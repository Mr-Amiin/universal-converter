How the formula works

Both megafarad and exafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in megafarads by 1.000000e-12 to get exafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a exafarad value by 1.000000e+12 to get back to megafarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert megafarads to exafarads?

Multiply the megafarad value by 1 × 10-12. For example, 4 MF × 1 × 10-12 = 4 × 10-12 EF.

How do I convert exafarads back to megafarads?

Divide the exafarad value by 1 × 10-12, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000,000.

Is the megafarad to exafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the exafarad?

The exafarad (EF) mostly shows up in theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base farad would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a megafarad used for?

The megafarad (MF) is commonly encountered in large energy-storage supercapacitor banks.

About this conversion

This page converts megafarads (MF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 106 farads (megafarad = mega + farad), into exafarads (EF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 1018 farads (exafarad = exa + farad).

This conversion is used when working with large energy-storage supercapacitor banks.

The formula is EF = MF × 1 × 10-12, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “mega” prefix (M-, 106) was standardized in 1873.
