How the formula works

Both volt and petavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in volts by 1.000000e-15 to get petavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petavolt value by 1.000000e+15 to get back to volts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert volts to petavolts?

Multiply the volt value by 1 × 10-15. For example, 4 V × 1 × 10-15 = 4 × 10-15 PV.

How do I convert petavolts back to volts?

Divide the petavolt value by 1 × 10-15, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1015.

Is the volt to petavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petavolt?

The petavolt (PV) mostly shows up in theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system, where using the base volt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a volt used for?

The volt (V) is commonly encountered in household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

About this conversion

This page converts volts (V), the SI base unit of voltage, equal to the energy per unit charge (one joule per coulomb), into petavolts (PV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1015 volts (petavolt = peta + volt).

This conversion is used when working with household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

The formula is PV = V × 1 × 10-15, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.
