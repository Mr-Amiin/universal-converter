How the formula works

Both terasiemens and quettasiemens measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in terasiemens by 1.000000e-18 to get quettasiemens. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettasiemens value by 1.000000e+18 to get back to terasiemens.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert terasiemens to quettasiemens?

Multiply the terasiemens value by 1 × 10-18. For example, 4 TS × 1 × 10-18 = 4 × 10-18 QS.

How do I convert quettasiemens back to terasiemens?

Divide the quettasiemens value by 1 × 10-18, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1018.

Is the terasiemens to quettasiemens conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of conductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettasiemens?

The quettasiemens (QS) mostly shows up in theoretical conductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base siemens would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a terasiemens used for?

The terasiemens (TS) is commonly encountered in high-conductance busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts terasiemens (TS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 1012 siemens (terasiemens = tera + siemens), into quettasiemens (QS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 1030 siemens (quettasiemens = quetta + siemens).

This conversion is used when working with high-conductance busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is QS = TS × 1 × 10-18, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The siemens is named after Ernst Werner von Siemens and was adopted by the CGPM in 1971, replacing the older non-SI “mho”. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
