How the formula works

Both kilosiemens and femtosiemens measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kilosiemens by 1.000000e+18 to get femtosiemens. To reverse the conversion, multiply a femtosiemens value by 1.000000e-18 to get back to kilosiemens.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kilosiemens to femtosiemens?

Multiply the kilosiemens value by 1 × 1018. For example, 4 kS × 1 × 1018 = 4 × 1018 fS.

How do I convert femtosiemens back to kilosiemens?

Divide the femtosiemens value by 1 × 1018, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-18.

Is the kilosiemens to femtosiemens conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of conductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtosiemens?

The femtosiemens (fS) shows up in the conductance of near-perfect insulators, where the base siemens would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a kilosiemens used for?

The kilosiemens (kS) is commonly encountered in typical resistor conductances (a 1 kΩ resistor is 1 mS).

About this conversion

This page converts kilosiemens (kS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 103 siemens (kilosiemens = kilo + siemens), into femtosiemens (fS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 10-15 siemens (femtosiemens = femto + siemens).

This conversion is used when working with typical resistor conductances (a 1 kΩ resistor is 1 mS).

The formula is fS = kS × 1 × 1018, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The siemens is named after Ernst Werner von Siemens and was adopted by the CGPM in 1971, replacing the older non-SI “mho”. The “kilo” prefix (k-, 103) was standardized in 1795.
