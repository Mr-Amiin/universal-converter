How the formula works

Both quectovolt and decavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in quectovolts by 1.000000e-31 to get decavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decavolt value by 1.000000e+31 to get back to quectovolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert quectovolts to decavolts?

Multiply the quectovolt value by 1 × 10-31. For example, 4 qV × 1 × 10-31 = 4 × 10-31 daV.

How do I convert decavolts back to quectovolts?

Divide the decavolt value by 1 × 10-31, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1031.

Is the quectovolt to decavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the quectovolt?

The quectovolt (qV) shows up in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels, where the base volt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a quectovolt used for?

The quectovolt (qV) is commonly encountered in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

About this conversion

This page converts quectovolts (qV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-30 volts (quectovolt = quecto + volt), into decavolts (daV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 101 volts (decavolt = deca + volt).

This conversion is used when working with thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

The formula is daV = qV × 1 × 10-31, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “quecto” prefix (q-, 10-30) was standardized in 2022.
