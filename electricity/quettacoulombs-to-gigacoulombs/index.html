How the formula works

Both quettacoulomb and gigacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in quettacoulombs by 1.000000e+21 to get gigacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a gigacoulomb value by 1.000000e-21 to get back to quettacoulombs.

How do I convert quettacoulombs to gigacoulombs?

Is the quettacoulomb to gigacoulomb conversion exact?

About this conversion

This page converts quettacoulombs (QC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1030 coulombs (quettacoulomb = quetta + coulomb), into gigacoulombs (GC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 109 coulombs (gigacoulomb = giga + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is GC = QC × 1 × 1021, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “quetta” prefix (Q-, 1030) was standardized in 2022.
