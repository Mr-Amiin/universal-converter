How the formula works

Both quectohenry and microhenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in quectohenrys by 1.000000e-24 to get microhenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a microhenry value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to quectohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert quectohenrys to microhenrys?

Multiply the quectohenry value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 qH × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 uH.

How do I convert microhenrys back to quectohenrys?

Divide the microhenry value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the quectohenry to microhenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the quectohenry?

The quectohenry (qH) shows up in the parasitic inductance of a short wire, where the base henry would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a quectohenry used for?

About this conversion

This page converts quectohenrys (qH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-30 henrys (quectohenry = quecto + henry), into microhenrys (uH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-6 henrys (microhenry = micro + henry).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

The formula is uH = qH × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “quecto” prefix (q-, 10-30) was standardized in 2022.
