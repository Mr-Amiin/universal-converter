How the formula works

Both microcoulomb and centicoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microcoulombs by 0.0001 to get centicoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centicoulomb value by 10000 to get back to microcoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert microcoulombs to centicoulombs?

Multiply the microcoulomb value by 0.0001. For example, 4 uC × 0.0001 = 0.0004 cC.

How do I convert centicoulombs back to microcoulombs?

Divide the centicoulomb value by 0.0001, which is the same as multiplying by 10,000.

Is the microcoulomb to centicoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a microcoulomb used for?

The microcoulomb (uC) is commonly encountered in the charge delivered by a camera-flash capacitor.

About this conversion

This page converts microcoulombs (uC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-6 coulombs (microcoulomb = micro + coulomb), into centicoulombs (cC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-2 coulombs (centicoulomb = centi + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge delivered by a camera-flash capacitor.

The formula is cC = uC × 0.0001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “micro” prefix (u-, 10-6) was standardized in 1873.
