How the formula works

Both hectohenry and zeptohenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in hectohenrys by 1.000000e+23 to get zeptohenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zeptohenry value by 1.000000e-23 to get back to hectohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert hectohenrys to zeptohenrys?

Multiply the hectohenry value by 1 × 1023. For example, 4 hH × 1 × 1023 = 4 × 1023 zH.

How do I convert zeptohenrys back to hectohenrys?

Divide the zeptohenry value by 1 × 1023, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-23.

Is the hectohenry to zeptohenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the zeptohenry?

The zeptohenry (zH) shows up in the parasitic inductance of a short wire, where the base henry would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a hectohenry used for?

The hectohenry (hH) is commonly encountered in power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

About this conversion

This page converts hectohenrys (hH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 102 henrys (hectohenry = hecto + henry), into zeptohenrys (zH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-21 henrys (zeptohenry = zepto + henry).

This conversion is used when working with power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

The formula is zH = hH × 1 × 1023, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “hecto” prefix (h-, 102) was standardized in 1795.
