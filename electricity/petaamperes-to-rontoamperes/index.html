How the formula works

Both petaampere and rontoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petaamperes by 1.000000e+42 to get rontoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a rontoampere value by 1.000000e-42 to get back to petaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert petaamperes to rontoamperes?

Multiply the petaampere value by 1 × 1042. For example, 4 PA × 1 × 1042 = 4 × 1042 rA.

How do I convert rontoamperes back to petaamperes?

Divide the rontoampere value by 1 × 1042, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-42.

Is the petaampere to rontoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaampere?

The petaampere (PA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere), into rontoamperes (rA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-27 amperes (rontoampere = ronto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is rA = PA × 1 × 1042, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “peta” prefix (P-, 1015) was standardized in 1975.
