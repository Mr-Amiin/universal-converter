How the formula works

Both milliwatt and watt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliwatts by 0.001 to get watts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a watt value by 1000 to get back to milliwatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliwatts to watts?

Multiply the milliwatt value by 0.001. For example, 4 mW × 0.001 = 0.004 W.

How do I convert watts back to milliwatts?

Divide the watt value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the milliwatt to watt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a milliwatt used for?

The milliwatt (mW) is commonly encountered in LED and phone-charger power draw.

About this conversion

This page converts milliwatts (mW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-3 watts (milliwatt = milli + watt), into watts (W), the SI base unit of power, equal to one joule of energy transferred per second.

This conversion is used when working with LED and phone-charger power draw.

The formula is W = mW × 0.001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
