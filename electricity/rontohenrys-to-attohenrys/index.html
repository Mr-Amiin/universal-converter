How the formula works

Both rontohenry and attohenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in rontohenrys by 1.000000e-09 to get attohenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a attohenry value by 1.000000e+09 to get back to rontohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert rontohenrys to attohenrys?

Multiply the rontohenry value by 1 × 10-9. For example, 4 rH × 1 × 10-9 = 4 × 10-9 aH.

How do I convert attohenrys back to rontohenrys?

Divide the attohenry value by 1 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000.

Is the rontohenry to attohenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the attohenry?

The attohenry (aH) mostly shows up in the parasitic inductance of a short wire, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a rontohenry used for?

About this conversion

This page converts rontohenrys (rH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-27 henrys (rontohenry = ronto + henry), into attohenrys (aH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-18 henrys (attohenry = atto + henry).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

The formula is aH = rH × 1 × 10-9, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “ronto” prefix (r-, 10-27) was standardized in 2022.
