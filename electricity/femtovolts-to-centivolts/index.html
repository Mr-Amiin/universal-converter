How the formula works

Both femtovolt and centivolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtovolts by 1.000000e-13 to get centivolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centivolt value by 1.000000e+13 to get back to femtovolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtovolts to centivolts?

Multiply the femtovolt value by 1 × 10-13. For example, 4 fV × 1 × 10-13 = 4 × 10-13 cV.

How do I convert centivolts back to femtovolts?

Divide the centivolt value by 1 × 10-13, which is the same as multiplying by 10,000,000,000,000.

Is the femtovolt to centivolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtovolt?

The femtovolt (fV) shows up in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels, where the base volt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtovolt used for?

The femtovolt (fV) is commonly encountered in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

About this conversion

This page converts femtovolts (fV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-15 volts (femtovolt = femto + volt), into centivolts (cV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-2 volts (centivolt = centi + volt).

This conversion is used when working with thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

The formula is cV = fV × 1 × 10-13, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
