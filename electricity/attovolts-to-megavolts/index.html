How the formula works

Both attovolt and megavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attovolts by 1.000000e-24 to get megavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megavolt value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to attovolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attovolts to megavolts?

Multiply the attovolt value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 aV × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 MV.

How do I convert megavolts back to attovolts?

Divide the megavolt value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the attovolt to megavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attovolt?

The attovolt (aV) shows up in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels, where the base volt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is an attovolt used for?

The attovolt (aV) is commonly encountered in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

About this conversion

This page converts attovolts (aV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-18 volts (attovolt = atto + volt), into megavolts (MV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 106 volts (megavolt = mega + volt).

This conversion is used when working with thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels.

The formula is MV = aV × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
