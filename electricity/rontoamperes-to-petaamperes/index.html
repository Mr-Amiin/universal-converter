How the formula works

Both rontoampere and petaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in rontoamperes by 1.000000e-42 to get petaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petaampere value by 1.000000e+42 to get back to rontoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert rontoamperes to petaamperes?

Multiply the rontoampere value by 1 × 10-42. For example, 4 rA × 1 × 10-42 = 4 × 10-42 PA.

How do I convert petaamperes back to rontoamperes?

Divide the petaampere value by 1 × 10-42, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1042.

Is the rontoampere to petaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaampere?

The petaampere (PA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a rontoampere used for?

The rontoampere (rA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts rontoamperes (rA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-27 amperes (rontoampere = ronto + ampere), into petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is PA = rA × 1 × 10-42, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “ronto” prefix (r-, 10-27) was standardized in 2022.
