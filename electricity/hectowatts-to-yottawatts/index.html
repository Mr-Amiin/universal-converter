How the formula works

Both hectowatt and yottawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in hectowatts by 1.000000e-22 to get yottawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottawatt value by 1.000000e+22 to get back to hectowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert hectowatts to yottawatts?

Multiply the hectowatt value by 1 × 10-22. For example, 4 hW × 1 × 10-22 = 4 × 10-22 YW.

How do I convert yottawatts back to hectowatts?

Divide the yottawatt value by 1 × 10-22, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1022.

Is the hectowatt to yottawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottawatt?

The yottawatt (YW) mostly shows up in planetary or stellar energy-output scales, far beyond engineered systems, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a hectowatt used for?

The hectowatt (hW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts hectowatts (hW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 102 watts (hectowatt = hecto + watt), into yottawatts (YW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1024 watts (yottawatt = yotta + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is YW = hW × 1 × 10-22, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “hecto” prefix (h-, 102) was standardized in 1795.
