How the formula works

Both rontoohm and yottaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in rontoohms by 1.000000e-51 to get yottaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottaohm value by 1.000000e+51 to get back to rontoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert rontoohms to yottaohms?

Multiply the rontoohm value by 1 × 10-51. For example, 4 rΩ × 1 × 10-51 = 4 × 10-51 YΩ.

How do I convert yottaohms back to rontoohms?

Divide the yottaohm value by 1 × 10-51, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1051.

Is the rontoohm to yottaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottaohm?

The yottaohm (YΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a rontoohm used for?

The rontoohm (rΩ) is commonly encountered in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts rontoohms (rΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-27 ohms (rontoohm = ronto + ohm), into yottaohms (YΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1024 ohms (yottaohm = yotta + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is YΩ = rΩ × 1 × 10-51, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “ronto” prefix (r-, 10-27) was standardized in 2022.
