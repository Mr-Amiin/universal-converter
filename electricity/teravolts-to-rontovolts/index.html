How the formula works

Both teravolt and rontovolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in teravolts by 1.000000e+39 to get rontovolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a rontovolt value by 1.000000e-39 to get back to teravolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert teravolts to rontovolts?

Multiply the teravolt value by 1 × 1039. For example, 4 TV × 1 × 1039 = 4 × 1039 rV.

How do I convert rontovolts back to teravolts?

Divide the rontovolt value by 1 × 1039, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-39.

Is the teravolt to rontovolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the rontovolt?

The rontovolt (rV) shows up in thermoelectric and biosensor signal levels, where the base volt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a teravolt used for?

The teravolt (TV) is commonly encountered in power-transmission line voltages (high-voltage grids run to hundreds of kV).

About this conversion

This page converts teravolts (TV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1012 volts (teravolt = tera + volt), into rontovolts (rV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-27 volts (rontovolt = ronto + volt).

This conversion is used when working with power-transmission line voltages (high-voltage grids run to hundreds of kV).

The formula is rV = TV × 1 × 1039, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
