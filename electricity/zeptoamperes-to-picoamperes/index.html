How the formula works

Both zeptoampere and picoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptoamperes by 1.000000e-09 to get picoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picoampere value by 1.000000e+09 to get back to zeptoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptoamperes to picoamperes?

Multiply the zeptoampere value by 1 × 10-9. For example, 4 zA × 1 × 10-9 = 4 × 10-9 pA.

How do I convert picoamperes back to zeptoamperes?

Divide the picoampere value by 1 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000.

Is the zeptoampere to picoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the zeptoampere?

The zeptoampere (zA) shows up in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research, where the base ampere would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a zeptoampere used for?

The zeptoampere (zA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptoamperes (zA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-21 amperes (zeptoampere = zepto + ampere), into picoamperes (pA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-12 amperes (picoampere = pico + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is pA = zA × 1 × 10-9, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
