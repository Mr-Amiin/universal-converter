How the formula works

Both rontoampere and yoctoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in rontoamperes by 0.001 to get yoctoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctoampere value by 1000 to get back to rontoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert rontoamperes to yoctoamperes?

Multiply the rontoampere value by 0.001. For example, 4 rA × 0.001 = 0.004 yA.

How do I convert yoctoamperes back to rontoamperes?

Divide the yoctoampere value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the rontoampere to yoctoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yoctoampere?

The yoctoampere (yA) mostly shows up in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a rontoampere used for?

The rontoampere (rA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts rontoamperes (rA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-27 amperes (rontoampere = ronto + ampere), into yoctoamperes (yA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-24 amperes (yoctoampere = yocto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is yA = rA × 0.001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.
