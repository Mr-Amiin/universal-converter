How the formula works

Both deciampere and kiloampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in deciamperes by 0.0001 to get kiloamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a kiloampere value by 10000 to get back to deciamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert deciamperes to kiloamperes?

Multiply the deciampere value by 0.0001. For example, 4 dA × 0.0001 = 0.0004 kA.

How do I convert kiloamperes back to deciamperes?

Divide the kiloampere value by 0.0001, which is the same as multiplying by 10,000.

Is the deciampere to kiloampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a deciampere used for?

The deciampere (dA) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts deciamperes (dA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-1 amperes (deciampere = deci + ampere), into kiloamperes (kA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 103 amperes (kiloampere = kilo + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is kA = dA × 0.0001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
