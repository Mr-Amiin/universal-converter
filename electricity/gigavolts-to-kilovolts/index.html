How the formula works

Both gigavolt and kilovolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in gigavolts by 1.000000e+06 to get kilovolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a kilovolt value by 0.000001 to get back to gigavolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert gigavolts to kilovolts?

Multiply the gigavolt value by 1,000,000. For example, 4 GV × 1,000,000 = 4,000,000 kV.

How do I convert kilovolts back to gigavolts?

Divide the kilovolt value by 1,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 0.000001.

Is the gigavolt to kilovolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a gigavolt used for?

The gigavolt (GV) is commonly encountered in power-transmission line voltages (high-voltage grids run to hundreds of kV).

About this conversion

This page converts gigavolts (GV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 109 volts (gigavolt = giga + volt), into kilovolts (kV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 103 volts (kilovolt = kilo + volt).

This conversion is used when working with power-transmission line voltages (high-voltage grids run to hundreds of kV).

The formula is kV = GV × 1,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “giga” prefix (G-, 109) was standardized in 1960.
