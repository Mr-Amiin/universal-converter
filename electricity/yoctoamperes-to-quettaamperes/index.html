How the formula works

Both yoctoampere and quettaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yoctoamperes by 1.000000e-54 to get quettaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettaampere value by 1.000000e+54 to get back to yoctoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yoctoamperes to quettaamperes?

Multiply the yoctoampere value by 1 × 10-54. For example, 4 yA × 1 × 10-54 = 4 × 10-54 QA.

How do I convert quettaamperes back to yoctoamperes?

Divide the quettaampere value by 1 × 10-54, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1054.

Is the yoctoampere to quettaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettaampere?

The quettaampere (QA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a yoctoampere used for?

The yoctoampere (yA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts yoctoamperes (yA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-24 amperes (yoctoampere = yocto + ampere), into quettaamperes (QA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1030 amperes (quettaampere = quetta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is QA = yA × 1 × 10-54, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “yocto” prefix (y-, 10-24) was standardized in 1991.
