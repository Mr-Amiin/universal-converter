How the formula works

Both zettaampere and yoctoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zettaamperes by 1.000000e+45 to get yoctoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctoampere value by 1.000000e-45 to get back to zettaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zettaamperes to yoctoamperes?

Multiply the zettaampere value by 1 × 1045. For example, 4 ZA × 1 × 1045 = 4 × 1045 yA.

How do I convert yoctoamperes back to zettaamperes?

Divide the yoctoampere value by 1 × 1045, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-45.

Is the zettaampere to yoctoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettaampere?

The zettaampere (ZA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts zettaamperes (ZA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1021 amperes (zettaampere = zetta + ampere), into yoctoamperes (yA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-24 amperes (yoctoampere = yocto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is yA = ZA × 1 × 1045, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “zetta” prefix (Z-, 1021) was standardized in 1991.
