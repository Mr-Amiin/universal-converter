How the formula works

Both zeptocoulomb and coulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptocoulombs by 1.000000e-21 to get coulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a coulomb value by 1.000000e+21 to get back to zeptocoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptocoulombs to coulombs?

Multiply the zeptocoulomb value by 1 × 10-21. For example, 4 zC × 1 × 10-21 = 4 × 10-21 C.

How do I convert coulombs back to zeptocoulombs?

Divide the coulomb value by 1 × 10-21, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1021.

Is the zeptocoulomb to coulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the zeptocoulomb?

The zeptocoulomb (zC) shows up in the charge carried by a handful of electrons, where the base coulomb would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a zeptocoulomb used for?

The zeptocoulomb (zC) is commonly encountered in the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptocoulombs (zC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-21 coulombs (zeptocoulomb = zepto + coulomb), into coulombs (C), the SI base unit of charge, equal to the charge transported by a one-ampere current in one second.

This conversion is used when working with the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

The formula is C = zC × 1 × 10-21, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
