How the formula works

Both microampere and yoctoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microamperes by 1.000000e+18 to get yoctoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctoampere value by 1.000000e-18 to get back to microamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert microamperes to yoctoamperes?

Multiply the microampere value by 1 × 1018. For example, 4 uA × 1 × 1018 = 4 × 1018 yA.

How do I convert yoctoamperes back to microamperes?

Divide the yoctoampere value by 1 × 1018, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-18.

Is the microampere to yoctoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctoampere?

The yoctoampere (yA) shows up in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research, where the base ampere would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a microampere used for?

The microampere (uA) is commonly encountered in microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

About this conversion

This page converts microamperes (uA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-6 amperes (microampere = micro + ampere), into yoctoamperes (yA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-24 amperes (yoctoampere = yocto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

The formula is yA = uA × 1 × 1018, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.
