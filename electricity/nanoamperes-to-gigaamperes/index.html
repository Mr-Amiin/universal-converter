How the formula works

Both nanoampere and gigaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in nanoamperes by 1.000000e-18 to get gigaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a gigaampere value by 1.000000e+18 to get back to nanoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert nanoamperes to gigaamperes?

Multiply the nanoampere value by 1 × 10-18. For example, 4 nA × 1 × 10-18 = 4 × 10-18 GA.

How do I convert gigaamperes back to nanoamperes?

Divide the gigaampere value by 1 × 10-18, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1018.

Is the nanoampere to gigaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a nanoampere used for?

The nanoampere (nA) is commonly encountered in microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

About this conversion

This page converts nanoamperes (nA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-9 amperes (nanoampere = nano + ampere), into gigaamperes (GA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 109 amperes (gigaampere = giga + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

The formula is GA = nA × 1 × 10-18, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “nano” prefix (n-, 10-9) was standardized in 1960.
