How the formula works

Both ronnaampere and petaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in ronnaamperes by 1.000000e+12 to get petaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petaampere value by 1.000000e-12 to get back to ronnaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert ronnaamperes to petaamperes?

Multiply the ronnaampere value by 1,000,000,000,000. For example, 4 RA × 1,000,000,000,000 = 4,000,000,000,000 PA.

How do I convert petaamperes back to ronnaamperes?

Divide the petaampere value by 1,000,000,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-12.

Is the ronnaampere to petaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the ronnaampere?

The ronnaampere (RA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts ronnaamperes (RA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1027 amperes (ronnaampere = ronna + ampere), into petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is PA = RA × 1,000,000,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “ronna” prefix (R-, 1027) was standardized in 2022.
