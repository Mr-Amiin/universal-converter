How the formula works

Both milliohm and yottaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliohms by 1.000000e-27 to get yottaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottaohm value by 1.000000e+27 to get back to milliohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliohms to yottaohms?

Multiply the milliohm value by 1 × 10-27. For example, 4 mΩ × 1 × 10-27 = 4 × 10-27 YΩ.

How do I convert yottaohms back to milliohms?

Divide the yottaohm value by 1 × 10-27, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1027.

Is the milliohm to yottaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottaohm?

The yottaohm (YΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a milliohm used for?

The milliohm (mΩ) is commonly encountered in wire and PCB trace resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts milliohms (mΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-3 ohms (milliohm = milli + ohm), into yottaohms (YΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1024 ohms (yottaohm = yotta + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with wire and PCB trace resistances.

The formula is YΩ = mΩ × 1 × 10-27, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
