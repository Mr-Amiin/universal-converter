How the formula works

Both quectoohm and ronnaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in quectoohms by 1.000000e-57 to get ronnaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a ronnaohm value by 1.000000e+57 to get back to quectoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert quectoohms to ronnaohms?

Multiply the quectoohm value by 1 × 10-57. For example, 4 qΩ × 1 × 10-57 = 4 × 10-57 RΩ.

How do I convert ronnaohms back to quectoohms?

Divide the ronnaohm value by 1 × 10-57, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1057.

Is the quectoohm to ronnaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the ronnaohm?

The ronnaohm (RΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a quectoohm used for?

The quectoohm (qΩ) is commonly encountered in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts quectoohms (qΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-30 ohms (quectoohm = quecto + ohm), into ronnaohms (RΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1027 ohms (ronnaohm = ronna + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is RΩ = qΩ × 1 × 10-57, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “quecto” prefix (q-, 10-30) was standardized in 2022.
