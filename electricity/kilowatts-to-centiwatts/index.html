How the formula works

Both kilowatt and centiwatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kilowatts by 100000 to get centiwatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centiwatt value by 0.00001 to get back to kilowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kilowatts to centiwatts?

Multiply the kilowatt value by 100,000. For example, 4 kW × 100,000 = 400,000 cW.

How do I convert centiwatts back to kilowatts?

Divide the centiwatt value by 100,000, which is the same as multiplying by 0.00001.

Is the kilowatt to centiwatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a kilowatt used for?

The kilowatt (kW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts kilowatts (kW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 103 watts (kilowatt = kilo + watt), into centiwatts (cW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-2 watts (centiwatt = centi + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is cW = kW × 100,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “kilo” prefix (k-, 103) was standardized in 1795.
