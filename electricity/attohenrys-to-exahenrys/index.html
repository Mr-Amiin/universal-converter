How the formula works

Both attohenry and exahenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attohenrys by 1.000000e-36 to get exahenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a exahenry value by 1.000000e+36 to get back to attohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attohenrys to exahenrys?

Multiply the attohenry value by 1 × 10-36. For example, 4 aH × 1 × 10-36 = 4 × 10-36 EH.

How do I convert exahenrys back to attohenrys?

Divide the exahenry value by 1 × 10-36, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1036.

Is the attohenry to exahenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the exahenry?

The exahenry (EH) mostly shows up in theoretical inductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is an attohenry used for?

The attohenry (aH) is commonly encountered in the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

About this conversion

This page converts attohenrys (aH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-18 henrys (attohenry = atto + henry), into exahenrys (EH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1018 henrys (exahenry = exa + henry).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

The formula is EH = aH × 1 × 10-36, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
