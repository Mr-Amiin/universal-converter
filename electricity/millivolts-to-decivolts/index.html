How the formula works

Both millivolt and decivolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in millivolts by 0.01 to get decivolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decivolt value by 100 to get back to millivolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert millivolts to decivolts?

Multiply the millivolt value by 0.01. For example, 4 mV × 0.01 = 0.04 dV.

How do I convert decivolts back to millivolts?

Divide the decivolt value by 0.01, which is the same as multiplying by 100.

Is the millivolt to decivolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a millivolt used for?

The millivolt (mV) is commonly encountered in battery-cell and low-voltage logic signals (a AA battery is 1.5 V).

About this conversion

This page converts millivolts (mV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-3 volts (millivolt = milli + volt), into decivolts (dV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-1 volts (decivolt = deci + volt).

This conversion is used when working with battery-cell and low-voltage logic signals (a AA battery is 1.5 V).

The formula is dV = mV × 0.01, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
