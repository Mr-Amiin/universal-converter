How the formula works

Both decihenry and zettahenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decihenrys by 1.000000e-22 to get zettahenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettahenry value by 1.000000e+22 to get back to decihenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decihenrys to zettahenrys?

Multiply the decihenry value by 1 × 10-22. For example, 4 dH × 1 × 10-22 = 4 × 10-22 ZH.

How do I convert zettahenrys back to decihenrys?

Divide the zettahenry value by 1 × 10-22, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1022.

Is the decihenry to zettahenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettahenry?

The zettahenry (ZH) mostly shows up in theoretical inductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a decihenry used for?

The decihenry (dH) is commonly encountered in power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

About this conversion

This page converts decihenrys (dH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-1 henrys (decihenry = deci + henry), into zettahenrys (ZH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1021 henrys (zettahenry = zetta + henry).

This conversion is used when working with power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

The formula is ZH = dH × 1 × 10-22, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
