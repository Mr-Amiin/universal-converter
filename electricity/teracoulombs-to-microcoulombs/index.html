How the formula works

Both teracoulomb and microcoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in teracoulombs by 1.000000e+18 to get microcoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a microcoulomb value by 1.000000e-18 to get back to teracoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert teracoulombs to microcoulombs?

Multiply the teracoulomb value by 1 × 1018. For example, 4 TC × 1 × 1018 = 4 × 1018 uC.

How do I convert microcoulombs back to teracoulombs?

Divide the microcoulomb value by 1 × 1018, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-18.

Is the teracoulomb to microcoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a teracoulomb used for?

The teracoulomb (TC) is commonly encountered in large battery-bank and lightning-strike charge scales.

About this conversion

This page converts teracoulombs (TC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1012 coulombs (teracoulomb = tera + coulomb), into microcoulombs (uC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-6 coulombs (microcoulomb = micro + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with large battery-bank and lightning-strike charge scales.

The formula is uC = TC × 1 × 1018, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
