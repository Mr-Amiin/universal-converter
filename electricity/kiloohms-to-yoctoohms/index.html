How the formula works

Both kiloohm and yoctoohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kiloohms by 1.000000e+27 to get yoctoohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctoohm value by 1.000000e-27 to get back to kiloohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kiloohms to yoctoohms?

Multiply the kiloohm value by 1 × 1027. For example, 4 kΩ × 1 × 1027 = 4 × 1027 yΩ.

How do I convert yoctoohms back to kiloohms?

Divide the yoctoohm value by 1 × 1027, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-27.

Is the kiloohm to yoctoohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctoohm?

The yoctoohm (yΩ) shows up in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts, where the base ohm would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a kiloohm used for?

The kiloohm (kΩ) is commonly encountered in common resistor values in electronics (typical resistors run 220 Ω-10 kΩ).

About this conversion

This page converts kiloohms (kΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 103 ohms (kiloohm = kilo + ohm), into yoctoohms (yΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-24 ohms (yoctoohm = yocto + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with common resistor values in electronics (typical resistors run 220 Ω-10 kΩ).

The formula is yΩ = kΩ × 1 × 1027, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “kilo” prefix (k-, 103) was standardized in 1795.
