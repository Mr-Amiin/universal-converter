How the formula works

Both femtosiemens and quectosiemens measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtosiemens by 1.000000e+15 to get quectosiemens. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quectosiemens value by 1.000000e-15 to get back to femtosiemens.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtosiemens to quectosiemens?

Multiply the femtosiemens value by 1 × 1015. For example, 4 fS × 1 × 1015 = 4 × 1015 qS.

How do I convert quectosiemens back to femtosiemens?

Divide the quectosiemens value by 1 × 1015, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-15.

Is the femtosiemens to quectosiemens conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of conductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the femtosiemens?

The femtosiemens (fS) mostly shows up in the conductance of near-perfect insulators, where using the base siemens would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a femtosiemens used for?

About this conversion

This page converts femtosiemens (fS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 10-15 siemens (femtosiemens = femto + siemens), into quectosiemens (qS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 10-30 siemens (quectosiemens = quecto + siemens).

This conversion is used when working with the conductance of near-perfect insulators.

The formula is qS = fS × 1 × 1015, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The siemens is named after Ernst Werner von Siemens and was adopted by the CGPM in 1971, replacing the older non-SI “mho”. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
