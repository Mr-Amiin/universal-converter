How the formula works

Both hectocoulomb and picocoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in hectocoulombs by 1.000000e+14 to get picocoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picocoulomb value by 1.000000e-14 to get back to hectocoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert hectocoulombs to picocoulombs?

Multiply the hectocoulomb value by 100,000,000,000,000. For example, 4 hC × 100,000,000,000,000 = 400,000,000,000,000 pC.

How do I convert picocoulombs back to hectocoulombs?

Divide the picocoulomb value by 100,000,000,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-14.

Is the hectocoulomb to picocoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a hectocoulomb used for?

The hectocoulomb (hC) is commonly encountered in the charge moved by a phone battery over about an hour (a few thousand coulombs).

About this conversion

This page converts hectocoulombs (hC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 102 coulombs (hectocoulomb = hecto + coulomb), into picocoulombs (pC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-12 coulombs (picocoulomb = pico + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge moved by a phone battery over about an hour (a few thousand coulombs).

The formula is pC = hC × 100,000,000,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “hecto” prefix (h-, 102) was standardized in 1795.
