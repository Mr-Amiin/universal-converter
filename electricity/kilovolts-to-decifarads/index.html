Relationship (no fixed conversion)

Kilovolt (kV) measures voltage. Decifarad (dF) measures capacitance. Because these are different physical dimensions, there is no single fixed factor that converts one directly into the other — a numeric answer here would be fabricated and misleading. They are related through the capacitor charge/energy relationship: given just these two values, you can calculate charge (Q = C × V) and energy stored (E = ½ × C × V²).

Worked example

Say you have 12 kV and 100 dF. Using Q = C × V, you can work out the charge directly from just those two numbers — no extra measurement required.

How the formula works

Both kilovolt and decifarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kilovolts by 10000 to get decifarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decifarad value by 0.0001 to get back to kilovolts.

About this comparison

Kilovolt (kV) measures voltage: the SI unit of electrical potential difference, equal to the energy per unit charge (one joule per coulomb). Decifarad (dF) measures capacitance: the SI unit of capacitance, equal to the capacitance that stores one coulomb of charge per volt.
