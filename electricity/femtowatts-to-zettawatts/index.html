How the formula works

Both femtowatt and zettawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtowatts by 1.000000e-36 to get zettawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettawatt value by 1.000000e+36 to get back to femtowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtowatts to zettawatts?

Multiply the femtowatt value by 1 × 10-36. For example, 4 fW × 1 × 10-36 = 4 × 10-36 ZW.

How do I convert zettawatts back to femtowatts?

Divide the zettawatt value by 1 × 10-36, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1036.

Is the femtowatt to zettawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettawatt?

The zettawatt (ZW) mostly shows up in planetary or stellar energy-output scales, far beyond engineered systems, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a femtowatt used for?

The femtowatt (fW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts femtowatts (fW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-15 watts (femtowatt = femto + watt), into zettawatts (ZW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1021 watts (zettawatt = zetta + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is ZW = fW × 1 × 10-36, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
