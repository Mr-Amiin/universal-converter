How the formula works

Both yoctocoulomb and megacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yoctocoulombs by 1.000000e-30 to get megacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megacoulomb value by 1.000000e+30 to get back to yoctocoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yoctocoulombs to megacoulombs?

Multiply the yoctocoulomb value by 1 × 10-30. For example, 4 yC × 1 × 10-30 = 4 × 10-30 MC.

How do I convert megacoulombs back to yoctocoulombs?

Divide the megacoulomb value by 1 × 10-30, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1030.

Is the yoctocoulomb to megacoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctocoulomb?

The yoctocoulomb (yC) shows up in the charge carried by a handful of electrons, where the base coulomb would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a yoctocoulomb used for?

The yoctocoulomb (yC) is commonly encountered in the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

About this conversion

This page converts yoctocoulombs (yC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-24 coulombs (yoctocoulomb = yocto + coulomb), into megacoulombs (MC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 106 coulombs (megacoulomb = mega + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

The formula is MC = yC × 1 × 10-30, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “yocto” prefix (y-, 10-24) was standardized in 1991.
