How the formula works

Both exaohm and megaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in exaohms by 1.000000e+12 to get megaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megaohm value by 1.000000e-12 to get back to exaohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert exaohms to megaohms?

Multiply the exaohm value by 1,000,000,000,000. For example, 4 EΩ × 1,000,000,000,000 = 4,000,000,000,000 MΩ.

How do I convert megaohms back to exaohms?

Divide the megaohm value by 1,000,000,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-12.

Is the exaohm to megaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the exaohm?

The exaohm (EΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is an exaohm used for?

The exaohm (EΩ) is commonly encountered in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components.

About this conversion

This page converts exaohms (EΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1018 ohms (exaohm = exa + ohm), into megaohms (MΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 106 ohms (megaohm = mega + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components.

The formula is MΩ = EΩ × 1,000,000,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “exa” prefix (E-, 1018) was standardized in 1975.
