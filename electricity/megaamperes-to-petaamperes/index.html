How the formula works

Both megaampere and petaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in megaamperes by 1.000000e-09 to get petaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petaampere value by 1.000000e+09 to get back to megaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert megaamperes to petaamperes?

Multiply the megaampere value by 1 × 10-9. For example, 4 MA × 1 × 10-9 = 4 × 10-9 PA.

How do I convert petaamperes back to megaamperes?

Divide the petaampere value by 1 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000.

Is the megaampere to petaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaampere?

The petaampere (PA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a megaampere used for?

The megaampere (MA) is commonly encountered in industrial motors and EV fast-charging currents.

About this conversion

This page converts megaamperes (MA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 106 amperes (megaampere = mega + ampere), into petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with industrial motors and EV fast-charging currents.

The formula is PA = MA × 1 × 10-9, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “mega” prefix (M-, 106) was standardized in 1873.
