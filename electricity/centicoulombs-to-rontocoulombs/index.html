How the formula works

Both centicoulomb and rontocoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in centicoulombs by 1.000000e+25 to get rontocoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a rontocoulomb value by 1.000000e-25 to get back to centicoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert centicoulombs to rontocoulombs?

Multiply the centicoulomb value by 1 × 1025. For example, 4 cC × 1 × 1025 = 4 × 1025 rC.

How do I convert rontocoulombs back to centicoulombs?

Divide the rontocoulomb value by 1 × 1025, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-25.

Is the centicoulomb to rontocoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the rontocoulomb?

The rontocoulomb (rC) shows up in the charge carried by a handful of electrons, where the base coulomb would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a centicoulomb used for?

The centicoulomb (cC) is commonly encountered in the charge moved by a phone battery over about an hour (a few thousand coulombs).

About this conversion

This page converts centicoulombs (cC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-2 coulombs (centicoulomb = centi + coulomb), into rontocoulombs (rC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-27 coulombs (rontocoulomb = ronto + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge moved by a phone battery over about an hour (a few thousand coulombs).

The formula is rC = cC × 1 × 1025, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “centi” prefix (c-, 10-2) was standardized in 1795.
