How the formula works

Both nanoohm and quectoohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in nanoohms by 1.000000e+21 to get quectoohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quectoohm value by 1.000000e-21 to get back to nanoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert nanoohms to quectoohms?

Multiply the nanoohm value by 1 × 1021. For example, 4 nΩ × 1 × 1021 = 4 × 1021 qΩ.

How do I convert quectoohms back to nanoohms?

Divide the quectoohm value by 1 × 1021, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-21.

Is the nanoohm to quectoohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the quectoohm?

The quectoohm (qΩ) shows up in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts, where the base ohm would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a nanoohm used for?

The nanoohm (nΩ) is commonly encountered in wire and PCB trace resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts nanoohms (nΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-9 ohms (nanoohm = nano + ohm), into quectoohms (qΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-30 ohms (quectoohm = quecto + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with wire and PCB trace resistances.

The formula is qΩ = nΩ × 1 × 1021, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “nano” prefix (n-, 10-9) was standardized in 1960.
