How the formula works

Both ronnacoulomb and zeptocoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in ronnacoulombs by 1.000000e+48 to get zeptocoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zeptocoulomb value by 1.000000e-48 to get back to ronnacoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert ronnacoulombs to zeptocoulombs?

Multiply the ronnacoulomb value by 1 × 1048. For example, 4 RC × 1 × 1048 = 4 × 1048 zC.

How do I convert zeptocoulombs back to ronnacoulombs?

Divide the zeptocoulomb value by 1 × 1048, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-48.

Is the ronnacoulomb to zeptocoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the ronnacoulomb?

The ronnacoulomb (RC) mostly shows up in theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base coulomb would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a ronnacoulomb used for?

About this conversion

This page converts ronnacoulombs (RC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1027 coulombs (ronnacoulomb = ronna + coulomb), into zeptocoulombs (zC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-21 coulombs (zeptocoulomb = zepto + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is zC = RC × 1 × 1048, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “ronna” prefix (R-, 1027) was standardized in 2022.
