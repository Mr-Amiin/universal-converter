How the formula works

Both hectoohm and yottaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in hectoohms by 1.000000e-22 to get yottaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottaohm value by 1.000000e+22 to get back to hectoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert hectoohms to yottaohms?

Multiply the hectoohm value by 1 × 10-22. For example, 4 hΩ × 1 × 10-22 = 4 × 10-22 YΩ.

How do I convert yottaohms back to hectoohms?

Divide the yottaohm value by 1 × 10-22, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1022.

Is the hectoohm to yottaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottaohm?

The yottaohm (YΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a hectoohm used for?

The hectoohm (hΩ) is commonly encountered in common resistor values in electronics (typical resistors run 220 Ω-10 kΩ).

About this conversion

This page converts hectoohms (hΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 102 ohms (hectoohm = hecto + ohm), into yottaohms (YΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1024 ohms (yottaohm = yotta + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with common resistor values in electronics (typical resistors run 220 Ω-10 kΩ).

The formula is YΩ = hΩ × 1 × 10-22, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “hecto” prefix (h-, 102) was standardized in 1795.
