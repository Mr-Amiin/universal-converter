How the formula works

Both zeptohenry and quettahenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptohenrys by 1.000000e-51 to get quettahenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettahenry value by 1.000000e+51 to get back to zeptohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptohenrys to quettahenrys?

Multiply the zeptohenry value by 1 × 10-51. For example, 4 zH × 1 × 10-51 = 4 × 10-51 QH.

How do I convert quettahenrys back to zeptohenrys?

Divide the quettahenry value by 1 × 10-51, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1051.

Is the zeptohenry to quettahenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettahenry?

The quettahenry (QH) mostly shows up in theoretical inductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a zeptohenry used for?

The zeptohenry (zH) is commonly encountered in the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptohenrys (zH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-21 henrys (zeptohenry = zepto + henry), into quettahenrys (QH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1030 henrys (quettahenry = quetta + henry).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic inductance of a short wire.

The formula is QH = zH × 1 × 10-51, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
