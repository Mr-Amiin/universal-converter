How the formula works

Both milliohm and zettaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliohms by 1.000000e-24 to get zettaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettaohm value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to milliohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliohms to zettaohms?

Multiply the milliohm value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 mΩ × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 ZΩ.

How do I convert zettaohms back to milliohms?

Divide the zettaohm value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the milliohm to zettaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettaohm?

The zettaohm (ZΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a milliohm used for?

The milliohm (mΩ) is commonly encountered in wire and PCB trace resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts milliohms (mΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-3 ohms (milliohm = milli + ohm), into zettaohms (ZΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1021 ohms (zettaohm = zetta + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with wire and PCB trace resistances.

The formula is ZΩ = mΩ × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
