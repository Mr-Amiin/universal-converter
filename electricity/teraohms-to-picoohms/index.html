How the formula works

Both teraohm and picoohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in teraohms by 1.000000e+24 to get picoohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picoohm value by 1.000000e-24 to get back to teraohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert teraohms to picoohms?

Multiply the teraohm value by 1 × 1024. For example, 4 TΩ × 1 × 1024 = 4 × 1024 pΩ.

How do I convert picoohms back to teraohms?

Divide the picoohm value by 1 × 1024, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-24.

Is the teraohm to picoohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a teraohm used for?

The teraohm (TΩ) is commonly encountered in pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts teraohms (TΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1012 ohms (teraohm = tera + ohm), into picoohms (pΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-12 ohms (picoohm = pico + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

The formula is pΩ = TΩ × 1 × 1024, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
