How the formula works

Both picofarad and millifarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in picofarads by 1.000000e-09 to get millifarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a millifarad value by 1.000000e+09 to get back to picofarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert picofarads to millifarads?

Multiply the picofarad value by 1 × 10-9. For example, 4 pF × 1 × 10-9 = 4 × 10-9 mF.

How do I convert millifarads back to picofarads?

Divide the millifarad value by 1 × 10-9, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000,000.

Is the picofarad to millifarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a picofarad used for?

The picofarad (pF) is commonly encountered in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

About this conversion

This page converts picofarads (pF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-12 farads (picofarad = pico + farad), into millifarads (mF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-3 farads (millifarad = milli + farad).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

The formula is mF = pF × 1 × 10-9, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “pico” prefix (p-, 10-12) was standardized in 1960.
