How the formula works

Both rontowatt and hectowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in rontowatts by 1.000000e-29 to get hectowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a hectowatt value by 1.000000e+29 to get back to rontowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert rontowatts to hectowatts?

Multiply the rontowatt value by 1 × 10-29. For example, 4 rW × 1 × 10-29 = 4 × 10-29 hW.

How do I convert hectowatts back to rontowatts?

Divide the hectowatt value by 1 × 10-29, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1029.

Is the rontowatt to hectowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the rontowatt?

The rontowatt (rW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a rontowatt used for?

The rontowatt (rW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts rontowatts (rW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-27 watts (rontowatt = ronto + watt), into hectowatts (hW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 102 watts (hectowatt = hecto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is hW = rW × 1 × 10-29, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “ronto” prefix (r-, 10-27) was standardized in 2022.
