How the formula works

Both petafarad and rontofarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petafarads by 1.000000e+42 to get rontofarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a rontofarad value by 1.000000e-42 to get back to petafarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert petafarads to rontofarads?

Multiply the petafarad value by 1 × 1042. For example, 4 PF × 1 × 1042 = 4 × 1042 rF.

How do I convert rontofarads back to petafarads?

Divide the rontofarad value by 1 × 1042, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-42.

Is the petafarad to rontofarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petafarad?

The petafarad (PF) mostly shows up in theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base farad would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts petafarads (PF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 1015 farads (petafarad = peta + farad), into rontofarads (rF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-27 farads (rontofarad = ronto + farad).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is rF = PF × 1 × 1042, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “peta” prefix (P-, 1015) was standardized in 1975.
