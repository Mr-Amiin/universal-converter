How the formula works

Both femtofarad and gigafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtofarads by 1.000000e-24 to get gigafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a gigafarad value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to femtofarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtofarads to gigafarads?

Multiply the femtofarad value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 fF × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 GF.

How do I convert gigafarads back to femtofarads?

Divide the gigafarad value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the femtofarad to gigafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtofarad?

The femtofarad (fF) shows up in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces, where the base farad would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtofarad used for?

The femtofarad (fF) is commonly encountered in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

About this conversion

This page converts femtofarads (fF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-15 farads (femtofarad = femto + farad), into gigafarads (GF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 109 farads (gigafarad = giga + farad).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

The formula is GF = fF × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
