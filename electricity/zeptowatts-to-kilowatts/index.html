How the formula works

Both zeptowatt and kilowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptowatts by 1.000000e-24 to get kilowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a kilowatt value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to zeptowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptowatts to kilowatts?

Multiply the zeptowatt value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 zW × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 kW.

How do I convert kilowatts back to zeptowatts?

Divide the kilowatt value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the zeptowatt to kilowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the zeptowatt?

The zeptowatt (zW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a zeptowatt used for?

The zeptowatt (zW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptowatts (zW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-21 watts (zeptowatt = zepto + watt), into kilowatts (kW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 103 watts (kilowatt = kilo + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is kW = zW × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
