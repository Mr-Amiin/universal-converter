How the formula works

Both kilohenry and exahenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kilohenrys by 1.000000e-15 to get exahenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a exahenry value by 1.000000e+15 to get back to kilohenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kilohenrys to exahenrys?

Multiply the kilohenry value by 1 × 10-15. For example, 4 kH × 1 × 10-15 = 4 × 10-15 EH.

How do I convert exahenrys back to kilohenrys?

Divide the exahenry value by 1 × 10-15, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1015.

Is the kilohenry to exahenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the exahenry?

The exahenry (EH) mostly shows up in theoretical inductance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base henry would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a kilohenry used for?

The kilohenry (kH) is commonly encountered in power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

About this conversion

This page converts kilohenrys (kH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 103 henrys (kilohenry = kilo + henry), into exahenrys (EH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1018 henrys (exahenry = exa + henry).

This conversion is used when working with power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

The formula is EH = kH × 1 × 10-15, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “kilo” prefix (k-, 103) was standardized in 1795.
