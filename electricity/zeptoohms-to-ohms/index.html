How the formula works

Both zeptoohm and ohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptoohms by 1.000000e-21 to get ohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a ohm value by 1.000000e+21 to get back to zeptoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptoohms to ohms?

Multiply the zeptoohm value by 1 × 10-21. For example, 4 zΩ × 1 × 10-21 = 4 × 10-21 Ω.

How do I convert ohms back to zeptoohms?

Divide the ohm value by 1 × 10-21, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1021.

Is the zeptoohm to ohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the zeptoohm?

The zeptoohm (zΩ) shows up in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts, where the base ohm would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a zeptoohm used for?

The zeptoohm (zΩ) is commonly encountered in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptoohms (zΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-21 ohms (zeptoohm = zepto + ohm), into ohms (Ω), the SI base unit of resistance, equal to the resistance that produces a one-volt drop when one ampere flows through it.

This conversion is used when working with the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is Ω = zΩ × 1 × 10-21, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.
