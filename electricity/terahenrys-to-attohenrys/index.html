How the formula works

Both terahenry and attohenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in terahenrys by 1.000000e+30 to get attohenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a attohenry value by 1.000000e-30 to get back to terahenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert terahenrys to attohenrys?

Multiply the terahenry value by 1 × 1030. For example, 4 TH × 1 × 1030 = 4 × 1030 aH.

How do I convert attohenrys back to terahenrys?

Divide the attohenry value by 1 × 1030, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-30.

Is the terahenry to attohenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attohenry?

The attohenry (aH) shows up in the parasitic inductance of a short wire, where the base henry would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a terahenry used for?

The terahenry (TH) is commonly encountered in large industrial transformer and grid-inductor scales.

About this conversion

This page converts terahenrys (TH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 1012 henrys (terahenry = tera + henry), into attohenrys (aH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-18 henrys (attohenry = atto + henry).

This conversion is used when working with large industrial transformer and grid-inductor scales.

The formula is aH = TH × 1 × 1030, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
