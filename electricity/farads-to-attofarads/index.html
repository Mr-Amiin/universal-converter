How the formula works

Both farad and attofarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in farads by 1.000000e+18 to get attofarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a attofarad value by 1.000000e-18 to get back to farads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert farads to attofarads?

Multiply the farad value by 1 × 1018. For example, 4 F × 1 × 1018 = 4 × 1018 aF.

How do I convert attofarads back to farads?

Divide the attofarad value by 1 × 1018, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-18.

Is the farad to attofarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attofarad?

The attofarad (aF) shows up in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces, where the base farad would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a farad used for?

The farad (F) is commonly encountered in supercapacitors, which can reach single farads.

About this conversion

This page converts farads (F), the SI base unit of capacitance, equal to the capacitance that stores one coulomb of charge per volt, into attofarads (aF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-18 farads (attofarad = atto + farad).

This conversion is used when working with supercapacitors, which can reach single farads.

The formula is aF = F × 1 × 1018, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.
