How the formula works

Both yottavolt and volt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yottavolts by 1.000000e+24 to get volts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a volt value by 1.000000e-24 to get back to yottavolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yottavolts to volts?

Multiply the yottavolt value by 1 × 1024. For example, 4 YV × 1 × 1024 = 4 × 1024 V.

How do I convert volts back to yottavolts?

Divide the volt value by 1 × 1024, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-24.

Is the yottavolt to volt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottavolt?

The yottavolt (YV) mostly shows up in theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system, where using the base volt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a yottavolt used for?

About this conversion

This page converts yottavolts (YV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1024 volts (yottavolt = yotta + volt), into volts (V), the SI base unit of voltage, equal to the energy per unit charge (one joule per coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system.

The formula is V = YV × 1 × 1024, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “yotta” prefix (Y-, 1024) was standardized in 1991.
