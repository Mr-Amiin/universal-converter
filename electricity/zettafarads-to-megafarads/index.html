How the formula works

Both zettafarad and megafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zettafarads by 1.000000e+15 to get megafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megafarad value by 1.000000e-15 to get back to zettafarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zettafarads to megafarads?

Multiply the zettafarad value by 1 × 1015. For example, 4 ZF × 1 × 1015 = 4 × 1015 MF.

How do I convert megafarads back to zettafarads?

Divide the megafarad value by 1 × 1015, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-15.

Is the zettafarad to megafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettafarad?

The zettafarad (ZF) mostly shows up in theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base farad would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts zettafarads (ZF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 1021 farads (zettafarad = zetta + farad), into megafarads (MF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 106 farads (megafarad = mega + farad).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is MF = ZF × 1 × 1015, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “zetta” prefix (Z-, 1021) was standardized in 1991.
