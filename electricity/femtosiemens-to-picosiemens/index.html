How the formula works

Both femtosiemens and picosiemens measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtosiemens by 0.001 to get picosiemens. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picosiemens value by 1000 to get back to femtosiemens.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtosiemens to picosiemens?

Multiply the femtosiemens value by 0.001. For example, 4 fS × 0.001 = 0.004 pS.

How do I convert picosiemens back to femtosiemens?

Divide the picosiemens value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the femtosiemens to picosiemens conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of conductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtosiemens?

The femtosiemens (fS) shows up in the conductance of near-perfect insulators, where the base siemens would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtosiemens used for?

The femtosiemens (fS) is commonly encountered in the conductance of near-perfect insulators.

About this conversion

This page converts femtosiemens (fS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 10-15 siemens (femtosiemens = femto + siemens), into picosiemens (pS), an SI-prefixed unit of conductance equal to 10-12 siemens (picosiemens = pico + siemens).

This conversion is used when working with the conductance of near-perfect insulators.

The formula is pS = fS × 0.001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The siemens is named after Ernst Werner von Siemens and was adopted by the CGPM in 1971, replacing the older non-SI “mho”. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975. The siemens is named after Ernst Werner von Siemens and was adopted by the CGPM in 1971, replacing the older non-SI “mho”. The “pico” prefix (p-, 10-12) was standardized in 1960.
