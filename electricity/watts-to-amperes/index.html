How to Convert Watts to Amps

Watts measure power and amps measure electric current, so they cannot be converted with one fixed factor the way meters and feet can. The link between them is the voltage. For alternating current (AC) it also depends on the power factor and on whether the supply is single-phase or three-phase. Choose the case that matches your circuit in the calculator below. The formulas use real electrical power in watts, meaning the power the load actually draws.

Use the electrical power the equipment draws. A motor rating is usually mechanical output power, which is lower than the electrical power it draws, so divide by the efficiency first.

Formula for DC and resistive loads

I = P ÷ V

I is the current in amperes (A), P is the power in watts (W) and V is the voltage in volts (V). Use this for DC circuits and for AC loads with a power factor of 1, such as simple heaters.

Formula for single-phase AC

I = P ÷ (V × PF)

PF is the power factor, a number above 0 and up to 1. A lower power factor means more current for the same real power.

Formula for three-phase AC

I = P ÷ (√3 × V × PF)

P is the total real power of all three phases, V is the line-to-line voltage and the load is assumed balanced. The result is the line current, the current in each supply line.

Worked examples

60 W ÷ 12 V = 5 A

1,500 W ÷ (230 V × 1) = 6.522 A

750 W ÷ (230 V × 0.8) = 4.076 A

15,000 W ÷ (√3 × 400 V × 0.85) = 25.47 A

The four examples are a 12 V DC load, a resistive 230 V load, a 230 V load with a power factor of 0.8, and a balanced three-phase load.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert watts to amps?

Divide the power in watts by the voltage in volts for a DC circuit or a purely resistive AC load: I = P ÷ V. For single-phase AC with a power factor below 1, divide by the voltage times the power factor. For three-phase AC, divide by the square root of 3 times the line-to-line voltage times the power factor.

Can you convert watts to amps without knowing the voltage?

No. Watts measure power and amps measure current, and the voltage is what links them, so there is no single conversion factor. The same wattage draws more current at a lower voltage.

How many amps is 1,000 watts?

It depends on the voltage. Assuming DC or a power factor of 1, 1,000 W is 83.33 A at 12 V, 8.333 A at 120 V and 4.348 A at 230 V. On a 230 V single-phase AC supply with a power factor of 0.9 it is 4.831 A.

What is power factor and which value should I use?

Power factor is the ratio of real power in watts to apparent power in volt-amperes in an AC circuit, a number from 0 to 1. Use the value on the equipment nameplate or data sheet when you have it. Resistive loads such as heaters are close to 1, while motors and many electronic power supplies are lower.

How do I calculate amps from watts for three-phase power?

Use I = P ÷ (√3 × V × PF), where P is the total real power, V is the line-to-line voltage and the load is balanced. For example, 15,000 W at 400 V with a power factor of 0.85 draws a line current of about 25.47 A.

Can I use this for a motor rating or to size a breaker?

The calculator uses the electrical power a load draws. A motor nameplate in kW is usually mechanical output power, so divide by the efficiency to estimate the electrical input power first. The result is a steady-state current, not a starting current, and breaker, fuse and wire sizing must follow your local electrical code and a qualified electrician.
