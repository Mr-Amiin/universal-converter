How the formula works

Both microcoulomb and quettacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microcoulombs by 1.000000e-36 to get quettacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettacoulomb value by 1.000000e+36 to get back to microcoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert microcoulombs to quettacoulombs?

Multiply the microcoulomb value by 1 × 10-36. For example, 4 uC × 1 × 10-36 = 4 × 10-36 QC.

How do I convert quettacoulombs back to microcoulombs?

Divide the quettacoulomb value by 1 × 10-36, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1036.

Is the microcoulomb to quettacoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettacoulomb?

The quettacoulomb (QC) mostly shows up in theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base coulomb would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a microcoulomb used for?

The microcoulomb (uC) is commonly encountered in the charge delivered by a camera-flash capacitor.

About this conversion

This page converts microcoulombs (uC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-6 coulombs (microcoulomb = micro + coulomb), into quettacoulombs (QC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1030 coulombs (quettacoulomb = quetta + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge delivered by a camera-flash capacitor.

The formula is QC = uC × 1 × 10-36, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “micro” prefix (u-, 10-6) was standardized in 1873.
