How the formula works

Both yottaampere and zeptoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yottaamperes by 1.000000e+45 to get zeptoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zeptoampere value by 1.000000e-45 to get back to yottaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yottaamperes to zeptoamperes?

Multiply the yottaampere value by 1 × 1045. For example, 4 YA × 1 × 1045 = 4 × 1045 zA.

How do I convert zeptoamperes back to yottaamperes?

Divide the zeptoampere value by 1 × 1045, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-45.

Is the yottaampere to zeptoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottaampere?

The yottaampere (YA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts yottaamperes (YA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1024 amperes (yottaampere = yotta + ampere), into zeptoamperes (zA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-21 amperes (zeptoampere = zepto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is zA = YA × 1 × 1045, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “yotta” prefix (Y-, 1024) was standardized in 1991.
