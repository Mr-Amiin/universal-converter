How the formula works

Both femtowatt and decawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtowatts by 1.000000e-16 to get decawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decawatt value by 1.000000e+16 to get back to femtowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtowatts to decawatts?

Multiply the femtowatt value by 1 × 10-16. For example, 4 fW × 1 × 10-16 = 4 × 10-16 daW.

How do I convert decawatts back to femtowatts?

Divide the decawatt value by 1 × 10-16, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1016.

Is the femtowatt to decawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtowatt?

The femtowatt (fW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtowatt used for?

The femtowatt (fW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts femtowatts (fW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-15 watts (femtowatt = femto + watt), into decawatts (daW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 101 watts (decawatt = deca + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is daW = fW × 1 × 10-16, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
