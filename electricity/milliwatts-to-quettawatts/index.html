How the formula works

Both milliwatt and quettawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliwatts by 1.000000e-33 to get quettawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettawatt value by 1.000000e+33 to get back to milliwatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliwatts to quettawatts?

Multiply the milliwatt value by 1 × 10-33. For example, 4 mW × 1 × 10-33 = 4 × 10-33 QW.

How do I convert quettawatts back to milliwatts?

Divide the quettawatt value by 1 × 10-33, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1033.

Is the milliwatt to quettawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettawatt?

The quettawatt (QW) mostly shows up in planetary or stellar energy-output scales, far beyond engineered systems, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a milliwatt used for?

The milliwatt (mW) is commonly encountered in LED and phone-charger power draw.

About this conversion

This page converts milliwatts (mW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-3 watts (milliwatt = milli + watt), into quettawatts (QW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1030 watts (quettawatt = quetta + watt).

This conversion is used when working with LED and phone-charger power draw.

The formula is QW = mW × 1 × 10-33, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
