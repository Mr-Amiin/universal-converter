How the formula works

Both attofarad and quettafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attofarads by 1.000000e-48 to get quettafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettafarad value by 1.000000e+48 to get back to attofarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attofarads to quettafarads?

Multiply the attofarad value by 1 × 10-48. For example, 4 aF × 1 × 10-48 = 4 × 10-48 QF.

How do I convert quettafarads back to attofarads?

Divide the quettafarad value by 1 × 10-48, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1048.

Is the attofarad to quettafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettafarad?

The quettafarad (QF) mostly shows up in theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base farad would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is an attofarad used for?

The attofarad (aF) is commonly encountered in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

About this conversion

This page converts attofarads (aF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-18 farads (attofarad = atto + farad), into quettafarads (QF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 1030 farads (quettafarad = quetta + farad).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

The formula is QF = aF × 1 × 10-48, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
