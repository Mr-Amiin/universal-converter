How the formula works

Both zeptoohm and petaohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptoohms by 1.000000e-36 to get petaohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petaohm value by 1.000000e+36 to get back to zeptoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptoohms to petaohms?

Multiply the zeptoohm value by 1 × 10-36. For example, 4 zΩ × 1 × 10-36 = 4 × 10-36 PΩ.

How do I convert petaohms back to zeptoohms?

Divide the petaohm value by 1 × 10-36, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1036.

Is the zeptoohm to petaohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaohm?

The petaohm (PΩ) mostly shows up in theoretical or insulator-scale resistances, far beyond common components, where using the base ohm would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a zeptoohm used for?

The zeptoohm (zΩ) is commonly encountered in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptoohms (zΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-21 ohms (zeptoohm = zepto + ohm), into petaohms (PΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1015 ohms (petaohm = peta + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is PΩ = zΩ × 1 × 10-36, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
