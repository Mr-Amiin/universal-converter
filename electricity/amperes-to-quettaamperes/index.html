How the formula works

Both ampere and quettaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in amperes by 1.000000e-30 to get quettaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettaampere value by 1.000000e+30 to get back to amperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert amperes to quettaamperes?

Multiply the ampere value by 1 × 10-30. For example, 4 A × 1 × 10-30 = 4 × 10-30 QA.

How do I convert quettaamperes back to amperes?

Divide the quettaampere value by 1 × 10-30, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1030.

Is the ampere to quettaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettaampere?

The quettaampere (QA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is an ampere used for?

The ampere (A) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts amperes (A), the SI base unit of current, equal to a flow of one coulomb of charge per second, into quettaamperes (QA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1030 amperes (quettaampere = quetta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is QA = A × 1 × 10-30, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris.
