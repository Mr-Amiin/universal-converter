How the formula works

Both femtoampere and quettaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtoamperes by 1.000000e-45 to get quettaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettaampere value by 1.000000e+45 to get back to femtoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtoamperes to quettaamperes?

Multiply the femtoampere value by 1 × 10-45. For example, 4 fA × 1 × 10-45 = 4 × 10-45 QA.

How do I convert quettaamperes back to femtoamperes?

Divide the quettaampere value by 1 × 10-45, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1045.

Is the femtoampere to quettaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettaampere?

The quettaampere (QA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a femtoampere used for?

The femtoampere (fA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts femtoamperes (fA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-15 amperes (femtoampere = femto + ampere), into quettaamperes (QA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1030 amperes (quettaampere = quetta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is QA = fA × 1 × 10-45, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
