How the formula works

Both attocoulomb and picocoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attocoulombs by 0.000001 to get picocoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picocoulomb value by 1000000 to get back to attocoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attocoulombs to picocoulombs?

Multiply the attocoulomb value by 0.000001. For example, 4 aC × 0.000001 = 0.000004 pC.

How do I convert picocoulombs back to attocoulombs?

Divide the picocoulomb value by 0.000001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000,000.

Is the attocoulomb to picocoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attocoulomb?

The attocoulomb (aC) shows up in the charge carried by a handful of electrons, where the base coulomb would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is an attocoulomb used for?

The attocoulomb (aC) is commonly encountered in the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

About this conversion

This page converts attocoulombs (aC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-18 coulombs (attocoulomb = atto + coulomb), into picocoulombs (pC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-12 coulombs (picocoulomb = pico + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with the charge carried by a handful of electrons.

The formula is pC = aC × 0.000001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
