How the formula works

Both megawatt and ronnawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in megawatts by 1.000000e-21 to get ronnawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a ronnawatt value by 1.000000e+21 to get back to megawatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert megawatts to ronnawatts?

Multiply the megawatt value by 1 × 10-21. For example, 4 MW × 1 × 10-21 = 4 × 10-21 RW.

How do I convert ronnawatts back to megawatts?

Divide the ronnawatt value by 1 × 10-21, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1021.

Is the megawatt to ronnawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the ronnawatt?

The ronnawatt (RW) mostly shows up in planetary or stellar energy-output scales, far beyond engineered systems, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a megawatt used for?

The megawatt (MW) is commonly encountered in power-plant generation capacity, usually quoted in megawatts.

About this conversion

This page converts megawatts (MW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 106 watts (megawatt = mega + watt), into ronnawatts (RW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1027 watts (ronnawatt = ronna + watt).

This conversion is used when working with power-plant generation capacity, usually quoted in megawatts.

The formula is RW = MW × 1 × 10-21, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “mega” prefix (M-, 106) was standardized in 1873.
