How the formula works

Both decaampere and kiloampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decaamperes by 0.01 to get kiloamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a kiloampere value by 100 to get back to decaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decaamperes to kiloamperes?

Multiply the decaampere value by 0.01. For example, 4 daA × 0.01 = 0.04 kA.

How do I convert kiloamperes back to decaamperes?

Divide the kiloampere value by 0.01, which is the same as multiplying by 100.

Is the decaampere to kiloampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a decaampere used for?

The decaampere (daA) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts decaamperes (daA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 101 amperes (decaampere = deca + ampere), into kiloamperes (kA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 103 amperes (kiloampere = kilo + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is kA = daA × 0.01, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “deca” prefix (da-, 101) was standardized in 1795.
