From definition

The henry (H) is the SI unit of inductance, describing the voltage induced per unit rate of change of current.

To definition

A Centiwatt (cW) equals 1/100 watt(s), a scaled unit of electrical power used for centi-scale electrical work.

Formula

No fixed factor — different physical quantities

Henry measures inductance and Centiwatt measures electrical power. These are different physical quantities, so there is no fixed multiplication factor between them. Use Ohm’s law and energy over time: V = I × R, P = V × I, E = P × time.

Relationship (no fixed conversion)

Henry (H) measures inductance. Centiwatt (cW) measures power. Because these are different physical dimensions, there is no single fixed factor that converts one directly into the other — a numeric answer here would be fabricated and misleading. They're related through power delivered over time, but the standard formula needs time (t) as well: energy delivered (E = P × t).

Worked example

Say you have 1 H and 24 cW. That alone isn't enough to find the energy delivered — you'd also need time (t). Once you have that, the formula is E = P × t.

About this comparison

Henry (H) measures inductance: the SI unit of inductance, equal to the inductance that induces one volt when current changes at one ampere per second. Centiwatt (cW) measures power: the SI unit of power, equal to one joule of energy transferred per second.
