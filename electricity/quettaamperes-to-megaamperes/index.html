How the formula works

Both quettaampere and megaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in quettaamperes by 1.000000e+24 to get megaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megaampere value by 1.000000e-24 to get back to quettaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert quettaamperes to megaamperes?

Multiply the quettaampere value by 1 × 1024. For example, 4 QA × 1 × 1024 = 4 × 1024 MA.

How do I convert megaamperes back to quettaamperes?

Divide the megaampere value by 1 × 1024, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-24.

Is the quettaampere to megaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettaampere?

The quettaampere (QA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

About this conversion

This page converts quettaamperes (QA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1030 amperes (quettaampere = quetta + ampere), into megaamperes (MA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 106 amperes (megaampere = mega + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is MA = QA × 1 × 1024, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “quetta” prefix (Q-, 1030) was standardized in 2022.
