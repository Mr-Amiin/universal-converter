How the formula works

Both decaampere and petaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decaamperes by 1.000000e-14 to get petaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a petaampere value by 1.000000e+14 to get back to decaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decaamperes to petaamperes?

Multiply the decaampere value by 1 × 10-14. For example, 4 daA × 1 × 10-14 = 4 × 10-14 PA.

How do I convert petaamperes back to decaamperes?

Divide the petaampere value by 1 × 10-14, which is the same as multiplying by 100,000,000,000,000.

Is the decaampere to petaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaampere?

The petaampere (PA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a decaampere used for?

The decaampere (daA) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts decaamperes (daA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 101 amperes (decaampere = deca + ampere), into petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is PA = daA × 1 × 10-14, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “deca” prefix (da-, 101) was standardized in 1795.
