How the formula works

Both femtoampere and picoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtoamperes by 0.001 to get picoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a picoampere value by 1000 to get back to femtoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtoamperes to picoamperes?

Multiply the femtoampere value by 0.001. For example, 4 fA × 0.001 = 0.004 pA.

How do I convert picoamperes back to femtoamperes?

Divide the picoampere value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the femtoampere to picoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtoampere?

The femtoampere (fA) shows up in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research, where the base ampere would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtoampere used for?

The femtoampere (fA) is commonly encountered in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

About this conversion

This page converts femtoamperes (fA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-15 amperes (femtoampere = femto + ampere), into picoamperes (pA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-12 amperes (picoampere = pico + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is pA = fA × 0.001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
