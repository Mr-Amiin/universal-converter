How the formula works

Both decivolt and zettavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decivolts by 1.000000e-22 to get zettavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettavolt value by 1.000000e+22 to get back to decivolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decivolts to zettavolts?

Multiply the decivolt value by 1 × 10-22. For example, 4 dV × 1 × 10-22 = 4 × 10-22 ZV.

How do I convert zettavolts back to decivolts?

Divide the zettavolt value by 1 × 10-22, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1022.

Is the decivolt to zettavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettavolt?

The zettavolt (ZV) mostly shows up in theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system, where using the base volt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a decivolt used for?

The decivolt (dV) is commonly encountered in household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

About this conversion

This page converts decivolts (dV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-1 volts (decivolt = deci + volt), into zettavolts (ZV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1021 volts (zettavolt = zetta + volt).

This conversion is used when working with household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

The formula is ZV = dV × 1 × 10-22, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
