How the formula works

Both decihenry and centihenry measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decihenrys by 10 to get centihenrys. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centihenry value by 0.1 to get back to decihenrys.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decihenrys to centihenrys?

Multiply the decihenry value by 10. For example, 4 dH × 10 = 40 cH.

How do I convert centihenrys back to decihenrys?

Divide the centihenry value by 10, which is the same as multiplying by 0.1.

Is the decihenry to centihenry conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of inductance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a decihenry used for?

The decihenry (dH) is commonly encountered in power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

About this conversion

This page converts decihenrys (dH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-1 henrys (decihenry = deci + henry), into centihenrys (cH), an SI-prefixed unit of inductance equal to 10-2 henrys (centihenry = centi + henry).

This conversion is used when working with power-supply chokes and transformer windings.

The formula is cH = dH × 10, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The henry is named after Joseph Henry and was formally adopted at the 1893 Chicago International Electrical Congress. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
