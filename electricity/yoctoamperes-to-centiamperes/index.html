How the formula works

Both yoctoampere and centiampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yoctoamperes by 1.000000e-22 to get centiamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centiampere value by 1.000000e+22 to get back to yoctoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yoctoamperes to centiamperes?

Multiply the yoctoampere value by 1 × 10-22. For example, 4 yA × 1 × 10-22 = 4 × 10-22 cA.

How do I convert centiamperes back to yoctoamperes?

Divide the centiampere value by 1 × 10-22, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1022.

Is the yoctoampere to centiampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctoampere?

The yoctoampere (yA) shows up in single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research, where the base ampere would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

About this conversion

This page converts yoctoamperes (yA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-24 amperes (yoctoampere = yocto + ampere), into centiamperes (cA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-2 amperes (centiampere = centi + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with single-electron and quantum-tunneling current measurements in physics research.

The formula is cA = yA × 1 × 10-22, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “yocto” prefix (y-, 10-24) was standardized in 1991.
