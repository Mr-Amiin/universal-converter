How the formula works

Both microvolt and yottavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in microvolts by 1.000000e-30 to get yottavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yottavolt value by 1.000000e+30 to get back to microvolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert microvolts to yottavolts?

Multiply the microvolt value by 1 × 10-30. For example, 4 uV × 1 × 10-30 = 4 × 10-30 YV.

How do I convert yottavolts back to microvolts?

Divide the yottavolt value by 1 × 10-30, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1030.

Is the microvolt to yottavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the yottavolt?

The yottavolt (YV) mostly shows up in theoretical or astrophysical voltage scales, far beyond any engineered system, where using the base volt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a microvolt used for?

The microvolt (uV) is commonly encountered in battery-cell and low-voltage logic signals (a AA battery is 1.5 V).

About this conversion

This page converts microvolts (uV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-6 volts (microvolt = micro + volt), into yottavolts (YV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 1024 volts (yottavolt = yotta + volt).

This conversion is used when working with battery-cell and low-voltage logic signals (a AA battery is 1.5 V).

The formula is YV = uV × 1 × 10-30, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “micro” prefix (u-, 10-6) was standardized in 1873.
