How the formula works

Both zeptowatt and quettawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zeptowatts by 1.000000e-51 to get quettawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettawatt value by 1.000000e+51 to get back to zeptowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zeptowatts to quettawatts?

Multiply the zeptowatt value by 1 × 10-51. For example, 4 zW × 1 × 10-51 = 4 × 10-51 QW.

How do I convert quettawatts back to zeptowatts?

Divide the quettawatt value by 1 × 10-51, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1051.

Is the zeptowatt to quettawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettawatt?

The quettawatt (QW) mostly shows up in planetary or stellar energy-output scales, far beyond engineered systems, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a zeptowatt used for?

The zeptowatt (zW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts zeptowatts (zW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-21 watts (zeptowatt = zepto + watt), into quettawatts (QW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1030 watts (quettawatt = quetta + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is QW = zW × 1 × 10-51, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “zepto” prefix (z-, 10-21) was standardized in 1991.
