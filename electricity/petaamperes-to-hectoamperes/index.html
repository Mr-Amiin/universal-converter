How the formula works

Both petaampere and hectoampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petaamperes by 1.000000e+13 to get hectoamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a hectoampere value by 1.000000e-13 to get back to petaamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert petaamperes to hectoamperes?

Multiply the petaampere value by 10,000,000,000,000. For example, 4 PA × 10,000,000,000,000 = 40,000,000,000,000 hA.

How do I convert hectoamperes back to petaamperes?

Divide the hectoampere value by 10,000,000,000,000, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-13.

Is the petaampere to hectoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the petaampere?

The petaampere (PA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a petaampere used for?

About this conversion

This page converts petaamperes (PA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1015 amperes (petaampere = peta + ampere), into hectoamperes (hA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 102 amperes (hectoampere = hecto + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering.

The formula is hA = PA × 10,000,000,000,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “peta” prefix (P-, 1015) was standardized in 1975.
