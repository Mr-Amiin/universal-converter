How the formula works

Both kiloampere and zettaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in kiloamperes by 1.000000e-18 to get zettaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettaampere value by 1.000000e+18 to get back to kiloamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kiloamperes to zettaamperes?

Multiply the kiloampere value by 1 × 10-18. For example, 4 kA × 1 × 10-18 = 4 × 10-18 ZA.

How do I convert zettaamperes back to kiloamperes?

Divide the zettaampere value by 1 × 10-18, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1018.

Is the kiloampere to zettaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettaampere?

The zettaampere (ZA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a kiloampere used for?

The kiloampere (kA) is commonly encountered in everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

About this conversion

This page converts kiloamperes (kA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 103 amperes (kiloampere = kilo + ampere), into zettaamperes (ZA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1021 amperes (zettaampere = zetta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with everyday household appliances and circuit breakers (a hair dryer draws roughly 10 A).

The formula is ZA = kA × 1 × 10-18, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “kilo” prefix (k-, 103) was standardized in 1795.
