How the formula works

Both attofarad and megafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attofarads by 1.000000e-24 to get megafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a megafarad value by 1.000000e+24 to get back to attofarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attofarads to megafarads?

Multiply the attofarad value by 1 × 10-24. For example, 4 aF × 1 × 10-24 = 4 × 10-24 MF.

How do I convert megafarads back to attofarads?

Divide the megafarad value by 1 × 10-24, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1024.

Is the attofarad to megafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attofarad?

The attofarad (aF) shows up in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces, where the base farad would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is an attofarad used for?

The attofarad (aF) is commonly encountered in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

About this conversion

This page converts attofarads (aF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-18 farads (attofarad = atto + farad), into megafarads (MF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 106 farads (megafarad = mega + farad).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

The formula is MF = aF × 1 × 10-24, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
