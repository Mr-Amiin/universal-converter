How the formula works

Both attowatt and zeptowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in attowatts by 1000 to get zeptowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zeptowatt value by 0.001 to get back to attowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert attowatts to zeptowatts?

Multiply the attowatt value by 1,000. For example, 4 aW × 1,000 = 4,000 zW.

How do I convert zeptowatts back to attowatts?

Divide the zeptowatt value by 1,000, which is the same as multiplying by 0.001.

Is the attowatt to zeptowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the attowatt?

The attowatt (aW) mostly shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where using the base watt would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is an attowatt used for?

The attowatt (aW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts attowatts (aW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-18 watts (attowatt = atto + watt), into zeptowatts (zW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-21 watts (zeptowatt = zepto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is zW = aW × 1,000, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “atto” prefix (a-, 10-18) was standardized in 1975.
