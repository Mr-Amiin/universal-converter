How the formula works

Both yoctowatt and terawatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in yoctowatts by 1.000000e-36 to get terawatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a terawatt value by 1.000000e+36 to get back to yoctowatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert yoctowatts to terawatts?

Multiply the yoctowatt value by 1 × 10-36. For example, 4 yW × 1 × 10-36 = 4 × 10-36 TW.

How do I convert terawatts back to yoctowatts?

Divide the terawatt value by 1 × 10-36, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1036.

Is the yoctowatt to terawatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctowatt?

The yoctowatt (yW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a yoctowatt used for?

The yoctowatt (yW) is commonly encountered in quantum and RF signal power levels.

About this conversion

This page converts yoctowatts (yW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-24 watts (yoctowatt = yocto + watt), into terawatts (TW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 1012 watts (terawatt = tera + watt).

This conversion is used when working with quantum and RF signal power levels.

The formula is TW = yW × 1 × 10-36, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “yocto” prefix (y-, 10-24) was standardized in 1991.
