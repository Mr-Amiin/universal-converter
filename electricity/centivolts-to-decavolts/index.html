How the formula works

Both centivolt and decavolt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in centivolts by 0.001 to get decavolts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a decavolt value by 1000 to get back to centivolts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert centivolts to decavolts?

Multiply the centivolt value by 0.001. For example, 4 cV × 0.001 = 0.004 daV.

How do I convert decavolts back to centivolts?

Divide the decavolt value by 0.001, which is the same as multiplying by 1,000.

Is the centivolt to decavolt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of voltage, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a centivolt used for?

The centivolt (cV) is commonly encountered in household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

About this conversion

This page converts centivolts (cV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 10-2 volts (centivolt = centi + volt), into decavolts (daV), an SI-prefixed unit of voltage equal to 101 volts (decavolt = deca + volt).

This conversion is used when working with household outlets and small electronics (mains power runs 120-240 V).

The formula is daV = cV × 0.001, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The volt is named after Alessandro Volta and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “centi” prefix (c-, 10-2) was standardized in 1795.
