How the formula works

Both picofarad and zettafarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in picofarads by 1.000000e-33 to get zettafarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a zettafarad value by 1.000000e+33 to get back to picofarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert picofarads to zettafarads?

Multiply the picofarad value by 1 × 10-33. For example, 4 pF × 1 × 10-33 = 4 × 10-33 ZF.

How do I convert zettafarads back to picofarads?

Divide the zettafarad value by 1 × 10-33, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1033.

Is the picofarad to zettafarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettafarad?

The zettafarad (ZF) mostly shows up in theoretical capacitance scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base farad would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a picofarad used for?

The picofarad (pF) is commonly encountered in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

About this conversion

This page converts picofarads (pF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-12 farads (picofarad = pico + farad), into zettafarads (ZF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 1021 farads (zettafarad = zetta + farad).

This conversion is used when working with the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces.

The formula is ZF = pF × 1 × 10-33, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “pico” prefix (p-, 10-12) was standardized in 1960.
