How the formula works

Both milliwatt and quectowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in milliwatts by 1.000000e+27 to get quectowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quectowatt value by 1.000000e-27 to get back to milliwatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert milliwatts to quectowatts?

Multiply the milliwatt value by 1 × 1027. For example, 4 mW × 1 × 1027 = 4 × 1027 qW.

How do I convert quectowatts back to milliwatts?

Divide the quectowatt value by 1 × 1027, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-27.

Is the milliwatt to quectowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the quectowatt?

The quectowatt (qW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a milliwatt used for?

The milliwatt (mW) is commonly encountered in LED and phone-charger power draw.

About this conversion

This page converts milliwatts (mW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-3 watts (milliwatt = milli + watt), into quectowatts (qW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-30 watts (quectowatt = quecto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with LED and phone-charger power draw.

The formula is qW = mW × 1 × 1027, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “milli” prefix (m-, 10-3) was standardized in 1795.
