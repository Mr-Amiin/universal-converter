How the formula works

Both decifarad and yoctofarad measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in decifarads by 1.000000e+23 to get yoctofarads. To reverse the conversion, multiply a yoctofarad value by 1.000000e-23 to get back to decifarads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decifarads to yoctofarads?

Multiply the decifarad value by 1 × 1023. For example, 4 dF × 1 × 1023 = 4 × 1023 yF.

How do I convert yoctofarads back to decifarads?

Divide the yoctofarad value by 1 × 1023, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-23.

Is the decifarad to yoctofarad conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of capacitance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the yoctofarad?

The yoctofarad (yF) shows up in the parasitic capacitance between circuit traces, where the base farad would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a decifarad used for?

The decifarad (dF) is commonly encountered in supercapacitors, which can reach single farads.

About this conversion

This page converts decifarads (dF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-1 farads (decifarad = deci + farad), into yoctofarads (yF), an SI-prefixed unit of capacitance equal to 10-24 farads (yoctofarad = yocto + farad).

This conversion is used when working with supercapacitors, which can reach single farads.

The formula is yF = dF × 1 × 1023, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The farad is named after Michael Faraday and was proposed in 1861 and formally adopted in 1881. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
