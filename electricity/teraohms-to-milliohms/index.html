How the formula works

Both teraohm and milliohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in teraohms by 1.000000e+15 to get milliohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a milliohm value by 1.000000e-15 to get back to teraohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert teraohms to milliohms?

Multiply the teraohm value by 1 × 1015. For example, 4 TΩ × 1 × 1015 = 4 × 1015 mΩ.

How do I convert milliohms back to teraohms?

Divide the milliohm value by 1 × 1015, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-15.

Is the teraohm to milliohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

What is a teraohm used for?

The teraohm (TΩ) is commonly encountered in pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

About this conversion

This page converts teraohms (TΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 1012 ohms (teraohm = tera + ohm), into milliohms (mΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-3 ohms (milliohm = milli + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with pull-up resistors and insulation-test resistances.

The formula is mΩ = TΩ × 1 × 1015, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “tera” prefix (T-, 1012) was standardized in 1960.
