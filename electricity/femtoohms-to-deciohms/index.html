How the formula works

Both femtoohm and deciohm measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in femtoohms by 1.000000e-14 to get deciohms. To reverse the conversion, multiply a deciohm value by 1.000000e+14 to get back to femtoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert femtoohms to deciohms?

Multiply the femtoohm value by 1 × 10-14. For example, 4 fΩ × 1 × 10-14 = 4 × 10-14 dΩ.

How do I convert deciohms back to femtoohms?

Divide the deciohm value by 1 × 10-14, which is the same as multiplying by 100,000,000,000,000.

Is the femtoohm to deciohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of resistance, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the femtoohm?

The femtoohm (fΩ) shows up in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts, where the base ohm would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a femtoohm used for?

The femtoohm (fΩ) is commonly encountered in the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

About this conversion

This page converts femtoohms (fΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-15 ohms (femtoohm = femto + ohm), into deciohms (dΩ), an SI-prefixed unit of resistance equal to 10-1 ohms (deciohm = deci + ohm).

This conversion is used when working with the resistance of thick copper busbars and superconducting contacts.

The formula is dΩ = fΩ × 1 × 10-14, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ohm is named after Georg Simon Ohm and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “femto” prefix (f-, 10-15) was standardized in 1975.
