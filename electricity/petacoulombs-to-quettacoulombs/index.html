How the formula works

Both petacoulomb and quettacoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in petacoulombs by 1.000000e-15 to get quettacoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettacoulomb value by 1.000000e+15 to get back to petacoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert petacoulombs to quettacoulombs?

Multiply the petacoulomb value by 1 × 10-15. For example, 4 PC × 1 × 10-15 = 4 × 10-15 QC.

How do I convert quettacoulombs back to petacoulombs?

Divide the quettacoulomb value by 1 × 10-15, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1015.

Is the petacoulomb to quettacoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

About this conversion

This page converts petacoulombs (PC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1015 coulombs (petacoulomb = peta + coulomb), into quettacoulombs (QC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1030 coulombs (quettacoulomb = quetta + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is QC = PC × 1 × 10-15, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “peta” prefix (P-, 1015) was standardized in 1975.
