How the formula works

Both deciwatt and attowatt measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in deciwatts by 1.000000e+17 to get attowatts. To reverse the conversion, multiply a attowatt value by 1.000000e-17 to get back to deciwatts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert deciwatts to attowatts?

Multiply the deciwatt value by 1 × 1017. For example, 4 dW × 1 × 1017 = 4 × 1017 aW.

How do I convert attowatts back to deciwatts?

Divide the attowatt value by 1 × 1017, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-17.

Is the deciwatt to attowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of power, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as small as the attowatt?

The attowatt (aW) shows up in quantum and RF signal power levels, where the base watt would be an inconveniently large unit for the quantities involved.

What is a deciwatt used for?

The deciwatt (dW) is commonly encountered in household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

About this conversion

This page converts deciwatts (dW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-1 watts (deciwatt = deci + watt), into attowatts (aW), an SI-prefixed unit of power equal to 10-18 watts (attowatt = atto + watt).

This conversion is used when working with household appliances (a microwave draws roughly 1,000 W).

The formula is aW = dW × 1 × 1017, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The watt is named after James Watt and was proposed in 1882 and formally adopted by the 2nd International Electrical Congress in 1889. The “deci” prefix (d-, 10-1) was standardized in 1795.
