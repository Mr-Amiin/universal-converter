How the formula works

Both zettacoulomb and centicoulomb measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in zettacoulombs by 1.000000e+23 to get centicoulombs. To reverse the conversion, multiply a centicoulomb value by 1.000000e-23 to get back to zettacoulombs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert zettacoulombs to centicoulombs?

Multiply the zettacoulomb value by 1 × 1023. For example, 4 ZC × 1 × 1023 = 4 × 1023 cC.

How do I convert centicoulombs back to zettacoulombs?

Divide the centicoulomb value by 1 × 1023, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 10-23.

Is the zettacoulomb to centicoulomb conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of charge, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the zettacoulomb?

The zettacoulomb (ZC) mostly shows up in theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart, where using the base coulomb would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a zettacoulomb used for?

About this conversion

This page converts zettacoulombs (ZC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 1021 coulombs (zettacoulomb = zetta + coulomb), into centicoulombs (cC), an SI-prefixed unit of charge equal to 10-2 coulombs (centicoulomb = centi + coulomb).

This conversion is used when working with theoretical charge scales with no real engineered counterpart.

The formula is cC = ZC × 1 × 1023, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The coulomb is named after Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “zetta” prefix (Z-, 1021) was standardized in 1991.
