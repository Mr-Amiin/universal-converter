How the formula works

Both nanoampere and quettaampere measure the same physical quantity, so converting between them is a single multiplication. Multiply a value in nanoamperes by 1.000000e-39 to get quettaamperes. To reverse the conversion, multiply a quettaampere value by 1.000000e+39 to get back to nanoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert nanoamperes to quettaamperes?

Multiply the nanoampere value by 1 × 10-39. For example, 4 nA × 1 × 10-39 = 4 × 10-39 QA.

How do I convert quettaamperes back to nanoamperes?

Divide the quettaampere value by 1 × 10-39, which is the same as multiplying by 1 × 1039.

Is the nanoampere to quettaampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both are SI-prefixed units of current, so the conversion factor is an exact power of ten rather than a rounded, measurement-based approximation.

Why would anyone need a unit as large as the quettaampere?

The quettaampere (QA) mostly shows up in theoretical grid-fault or lightning-scale current levels, far beyond ordinary engineering, where using the base ampere would mean writing out unwieldy strings of digits.

What is a nanoampere used for?

The nanoampere (nA) is commonly encountered in microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

About this conversion

This page converts nanoamperes (nA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 10-9 amperes (nanoampere = nano + ampere), into quettaamperes (QA), an SI-prefixed unit of current equal to 1030 amperes (quettaampere = quetta + ampere).

This conversion is used when working with microcontroller sensor and LED-drive currents.

The formula is QA = nA × 1 × 10-39, a fixed decimal ratio between these two SI-prefixed units.

The ampere is named after André-Marie Ampère and was formally adopted at the 1881 International Electrical Congress in Paris. The “nano” prefix (n-, 10-9) was standardized in 1960.
