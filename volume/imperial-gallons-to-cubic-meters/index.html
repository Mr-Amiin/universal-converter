Frequently asked questions

What is the source unit in this conversion?

The source unit is the imperial gallons (gal (UK)).

What is the destination unit in this conversion?

The destination unit is the cubic meters (m³).

How do I convert imperial gallons to cubic meters?

Multiply the imperial gallons value by 0.004546. For example, 2 gal (UK) × 0.004546 = 0.009092 m³.

Why do imperial gallons and cubic meters need converting at all?

The imperial gallon, used in the UK and some Commonwealth countries, is about 20% larger than the US gallon. The cubic meter is the SI derived unit of volume, used for water, gas, and large-scale industrial measurement.

Is the imperial gallons to cubic meters conversion exact?

Yes. The relationship between imperial gallons and cubic meters is a fixed, defined ratio (× 0.004546), so results are exact rather than a rounded estimate, limited only by the precision you choose to display.

Can I convert cubic meters back to imperial gallons?

Yes. Multiply the cubic meters value by 219.9692482991 (or divide by 0.004546) to get imperial gallons.

About this conversion

This page converts imperial gallons (gal (UK)) into cubic meters (m³) within the volume converter, using the fixed relationship 1 gal (UK) = 0.004546 m³.

Worked example: to convert 5 gal (UK) to m³, multiply by the conversion factor: 5 × 0.004546 = 0.02273 m³.

A 2-gal (UK) bottle holds approximately 0.01 m³.

Reverse conversion: to go from cubic meters back to imperial gallons, divide by 0.004546 (equivalently, multiply by 219.9692482991).

Accuracy: this conversion uses a fixed, defined factor rather than a measured approximation, so results are exact to the precision shown; rounding only affects how many digits are displayed, not the underlying value.
